# Co-Primitive Necessity Analysis

**WP:** WP-TRT-LRT-ONTO-0001  
**Date:** 2026-09-19  
**Track:** A, pure derivation  
**Status:** first pass complete

## Question

Are L, I, and A necessary constituents of any possible actuality, rather than merely constituents of our actual reality?

## 1. Scope discipline

This analysis uses only:
- the TRT hard core;
- the representable -> actualizable -> actual distinction;
- ordinary modal operators as bookkeeping.

It does not use PSR/CGP, Kalam, axiological premises, perfect-being premises, or a divine bearer.

The target is structural necessity first. Numerical identity or a unified bearer is a later obligation.

## 2. Primitive roles

Let:

- I = differentiability / representable informational potential;
- L = logical constraint sufficient at least for excluding contradiction from actuality;
- A = actualization, the obtaining relation/process by which an admissible structure is actual rather than merely admissible;
- X = a determinate actual state or world.

TRT schema:

R(I) --L--> D --A--> X.

## 3. I necessity

Assume a possible actuality X with no differentiability.

For X to be an actuality, there must be at minimum a distinction between:
- X and not-X, or
- what obtains in X and what does not obtain, or
- X and an alternative modal state.

If absolutely no distinction obtains, "X" fails to pick out a determinate actuality. Even a putatively featureless actuality requires identity sufficient to distinguish that actuality from its negation/non-obtaining.

Therefore:

PossibleActuality(X) -> I.

Generalizing:

Box(PossibleActuality -> I).

This is a transcendental/constitutive necessity claim, not a claim that Shannon information exists in every world.

**Status:** DERIVED within the stipulated notion of determinate actuality.  
**Confidence:** MEDIUM-HIGH.

Potential objection: a perfectly simple actuality might contain no internal differentiation.

Reply: internal plurality is not required. The claim requires only determinability/identity, including the distinction between obtaining and not obtaining. "Differentiability" must therefore not be defined as internal complexity.

## 4. L necessity

Assume a possible actuality X in which the identity conditions of X fail or X both obtains and does not obtain in the same respect and interval.

Then no determinate X has been specified. The attempted counterexample relies on enough identity and non-contradiction to identify the counterexample and state what allegedly fails.

Thus logical constraint is constitutive of determinate actuality:

PossibleActuality(X) -> L.

Generalizing:

Box(PossibleActuality -> L).

Caution: this establishes a minimal logical constraint. It does not by itself prove that the exact full L3 package, especially a globally interpreted Excluded Middle, is the uniquely correct logic for every representable formal domain. The stronger repository claim about L3 should remain separately defended.

**Status:** minimal L necessity DERIVED by elenchus/transcendental argument; exact L3 universality remains a stronger claim.  
**Confidence:** HIGH for Identity/Non-Contradiction as conditions of determinate actuality; MEDIUM for the full L3 formulation.

## 5. A necessity

Assume a possible actuality X with no A.

By TRT definition, without A an admissible structure remains in D and is not in X. Therefore if X is actual, the distinction between merely actualizable and actual must be discharged.

If A is defined minimally as the actuality-conferring relation/status transition, then:

Actual(X) -> A.

For every possible actuality:

Box(PossibleActuality -> A).

This is stronger than the earlier ledger formulation only after clarifying the quantifier: A is necessary **in every world in which actuality obtains**.

The remaining question is whether a possible world with no actuality is coherent.

## 6. Can there be a possible world with no actuality?

Two semantics must be distinguished.

### Semantic representation view

A "possible world" may be an abstract representation in the actual world. On this view, an empty-world representation need not contain its own A; its representational token is actual here.

This cannot establish Box A.

### Ontological possible-world view

If a possible world denotes a complete way reality could actually be, then to count as a world there must be some determinate fact of the matter corresponding to that way reality is. A world in which literally nothing is actual is difficult to distinguish from the absence of a world.

However, the null-world question is controversial and cannot be settled by definition without circularity.

Therefore the strongest current pure result is:

Box(Actuality -> I AND L AND A).

Not yet:

Box(I AND L AND A).

## 7. Co-primitive result

For any determinate actuality X:

1. X must be differentiable/identifiable -> I.
2. X must be coherently determinate -> L.
3. X must obtain rather than remain merely admissible -> A.

Therefore:

**Co-Primitive Necessity Theorem candidate**

Box(Actuality -> (I AND L AND A)).

The three are jointly necessary as structural roles for actuality.

Further, each removal produces a distinct failure:
- no I -> no determinate distinction to be actual;
- no L -> no coherent determinacy;
- no A -> no actuality.

This supports genuine co-requirement at the role level.

## 8. What this does not establish

The theorem candidate does not establish:

- Box I, Box L, or Box A simpliciter;
- that I, L, and A are substances or beings;
- that one numerically identical I/L/A triad exists across worlds;
- that the roles have a single bearer;
- that A is intentional agency;
- that the triad is God;
- that maximal perfection follows.

The next bridge is therefore not "prove God" but:

> Can there be metaphysical possibility without actuality of any kind?

If no, then:

Box Actuality

combined with:

Box(Actuality -> I AND L AND A)

would yield:

Box(I AND L AND A).

## 9. A possible stronger route from modal truth

Suppose there is a genuinely possible world w.

The proposition "w is possible" is itself true in the actual modal order. This establishes actual modal truth somewhere, but not necessarily actuality internal to w. Therefore it cannot by itself prove that A is instantiated in every w.

This blocks an easy metalanguage/object-language slide.

## 10. Interim disposition

**Pure-track result:**

Box(Actuality -> (I AND L AND A)).

**Confidence:** MEDIUM-HIGH as a constitutive theorem schema inside TRT.

**Stronger target:**

Box(I AND L AND A).

**Status:** NOT YET DERIVED.

**Exact blocker:** whether actuality itself is necessary, or whether a genuine null actuality/world is metaphysically possible.
