# GLSA-001: Ground-Level Semantic Adequacy

**WP:** WP-TRT-LRT-ONTO-0001
**Date:** 2026-09-19
**Status:** bridge theorem attempt v0.1
**Type:** B/D comparative analysis

## Target

Can a wholly non-semantic ultimate ground adequately ground an actualized determinate domain containing genuinely truth-bearing intentional content?

Target bridge:

[
SemanticActuality(X_s)
land
UltimateGround(U,X_s)
stackrel{?}{Longrightarrow}
SemanticCapacity(U).
]

The theorem must avoid:
- treating derivative minds as upstream grounds;
- assuming semantic content is already primitive in I_R;
- the fallacy of composition;
- assuming every cause must contain its effect in the same form.

## 1. Typed starting point

DRT establishes only:

[
Xightarrow I_R
]

where I_R is differentiated representability.

It does not establish intrinsic semantics.

Therefore GLSA begins with an additional observed/domain claim:

[
ExistsIntrinsicSemanticContent(X_s).
]

This is not a Type-P co-primitive claim merely by being introduced. Its exact status requires philosophical defense.

## 2. Semantic target

Define minimally:

**IntrinsicSemantic(c,o):**
content c has determinate aboutness/reference toward o not merely assigned by an external interpreter.

For truth-bearing content add:

**TruthApt(c):**
c has conditions under which its represented content is true or false.

Examples in finite minds appear to include:
- beliefs;
- judgments;
- propositions entertained as propositions;
- intentions directed toward represented ends.

GLSA does not require qualia.

## 3. First invalid inference

Do not infer:

[
SemanticEffectightarrow SemanticCause.
]

A cause need not instantiate every property of its effects.

Water can ground wet systems without being "wet" at the molecular level; structural combinations can yield properties absent in isolated components.

Therefore simple causal containment is invalid.

## 4. Grounding adequacy is stronger than causal ancestry

The relevant U is not merely an earlier efficient cause.

It is proposed as the adequate ultimate ground of the full ontological conditions from which X_s obtains.

Thus the question is:

Can U contain no semantic/intentional capacity whatsoever while nevertheless being sufficient for the existence of intrinsic semantics downstream?

This is a grounding/emergence question, not simple causal resemblance.

## 5. Emergence route

Candidate IAS response:

Intrinsic semantics emerges when thin I/L/A structure reaches the right organization.

Call:

[
ThinStructure + Organization ightarrow SemanticContent.
]

But under EPR, "emergence" must specify a non-circular relation.

The key question becomes whether the emergence base plus relation can be described without importing:
- representation;
- function;
- correctness;
- reference;
- interpretation;
- goal;
- normativity.

If it can, IAS survives.

If it cannot, semantic content has been smuggled into the emergence relation.

## 6. Structural covariance model

A nonsemantic base can support:

[
Covaries(c,o).
]

But covariance alone underdetermines:
- which correlated item is the referent;
- whether c represents o;
- whether c can misrepresent o;
- whether c is true/false about o.

Therefore:

[
Covariance
otRightarrow IntrinsicSemantic.
]

This does not prove no richer structural relation can succeed.

## 7. Functional model

Candidate:

c means o because c has functional role F with respect to o/system behavior.

Then GLSA asks:
- what grounds F as the relevant function rather than mere causal effect?
- what grounds malfunction/misrepresentation?
- what makes the role semantic rather than merely transition-structural?

Teleosemantic and functional accounts are live rivals.

They must be evaluated without assuming their conclusion.

## 8. Misrepresentation pressure

If c represents o, c can in principle occur when o is absent or differently constituted.

Then:

[
Represents(c,o)land
eg o
]

may count as misrepresentation.

Pure covariance does not naturally yield "wrong about."

Misrepresentation requires a correctness condition:

[
Correct(c,o)
]

or equivalent normative/functional standard.

This creates a semantic-normative bridge but still does not prove subjecthood.

## 9. Truth-aptness pressure

Truth-bearing content requires:

[
True(c) 	ext{or} False(c)
]

relative to represented conditions.

Truth-aptness therefore involves:
- determinate content;
- reference/conditions;
- logical relations among contents;
- correctness.

IAS can respond by making semantic relations primitive structural facts.

But then IAS is no longer wholly non-semantic. It has added intrinsic semantic structure at the ground or derivative law level.

## 10. Grounding trilemma

A wholly nonsemantic IAS has three routes:

### G1. Reduction
Derive intrinsic semantics completely from thin nonsemantic structure.

Burden: provide a non-circular reduction of reference, misrepresentation, and truth-aptness.

### G2. Emergence
Intrinsic semantics emerges from nonsemantic organization.

Burden: specify the emergence relation without semantic/normative smuggling.

### G3. Primitive semantic addition
Add intrinsic semantic relations as irreducible.

Then IAS ceases to be wholly nonsemantic and pays an additional primitive cost.

This trilemma does not by itself select Mind.

## 11. Does semantic capacity imply Mind?

Not automatically.

A structuralist can posit primitive semantic facts without a subject.

Therefore:

[
SemanticCapacity(U)
otRightarrow Mind(U)
]

unless intrinsic semantic relations are shown to require a subject-locus.

That remains the intentionality bridge.

## 12. Semantic Ground Adequacy Principle

A weaker result is defensible:

**SGAP:**

An adequate ultimate ground of a domain containing intrinsic semantics must either:
1. contain semantic capacity intrinsically;
2. supply a non-circular reduction/emergence account from nonsemantic ground-nature;
3. admit intrinsic semantics as an unexplained downstream primitive.

Option 3 weakens ultimate explanatory adequacy because a feature of the domain remains ungrounded by U.

Thus a fully adequate U must satisfy 1 or 2.

## 13. IAS stress test

IAS-thin claims route 2.

To remain a fully thin nonmental rival, it must derive:
- determinate reference;
- misrepresentation;
- truth-aptness;
- semantic correctness;

from nonsemantic L/I/A/C without importing intentional/normative primitives.

This is a substantive burden, not a contradiction.

Current result:

IAS-thin is not defeated, but its adequacy depends on a successful naturalization of intrinsic semantics.

## 14. Mind model

Mind supplies a familiar integrated model in which:
- content is about;
- propositions are truth-apt;
- misrepresentation is possible;
- reasons and interpretations are apprehended.

Thus Mind has direct semantic model-fit.

But:

familiar model-fit != necessary ground theorem.

The abductive advantage strengthens if IAS cannot complete G1/G2.

## 15. Ground-level semantic thickening

If future work establishes:

[
NoNonSemanticReduction(IntrinsicSemantic)
]

then SGAP yields:

[
AdequateUltimateGround(U,X_s)
ightarrow
SemanticCapacity(U).
]

This would thicken E_G from I_R to I_sem.

Then EGN's MindLike bridge becomes stronger but still requires subjecthood:

[
I_{sem}+L_{norm}+A_{source}+C
stackrel{?}{ightarrow}
MindLike.
]

## 16. GLSA-001 result

The strong theorem:

[
SemanticActuality(X_s)
land
UltimateGround(U,X_s)
ightarrow
SemanticCapacity(U)
]

is **NOT YET UNCONDITIONAL** because nonsemantic reduction/emergence remains logically open.

What is established is the Semantic Ground Adequacy Principle/trilemma.

A wholly nonsemantic IAS must provide a successful non-circular naturalization of intrinsic semantics or cease to be fully adequate.

## 17. Research implication

This bridge cannot be closed by conceptual analysis alone unless a contradiction can be shown in nonsemantic semantic-emergence.

The next work should therefore be adversarial literature/research, not more internal definition.

Research targets:
- teleosemantics;
- causal/informational semantics;
- inferential-role semantics;
- structural/functional naturalization of intentionality;
- arguments for original vs derived intentionality;
- misrepresentation problem;
- normativity of content.

The goal is not to prove naturalization impossible by fiat. It is to determine whether any mature account actually closes G1/G2 without presupposing semantic/normative notions.
