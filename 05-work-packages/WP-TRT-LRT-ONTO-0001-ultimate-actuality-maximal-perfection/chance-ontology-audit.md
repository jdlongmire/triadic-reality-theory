# Chance Ontology Audit

**WP:** WP-TRT-LRT-ONTO-0001  
**Date:** 2026-09-19  
**Track:** convergence / sourcehood  
**Target:** can chance explain selection without presupposing L/I/A?  
**Status:** first-pass audit complete

## Question

Can "chance" be defined as an ontological source of determination without presupposing the three co-primitive roles required by TRT?

This audit replaces the earlier framing "AOD versus chance" insofar as that framing treated chance as an explanatory primitive at the same ontological level without first accounting for its dependencies.

## 1. Chance as probability structure

Let a chance process have sample space:

Omega = {omega_1, omega_2, ...}

and probability measure:

P: F -> [0,1].

This already requires:

- I: distinguishable outcomes/events;
- L: identity, set/measure relations, consistency, admissible probability structure.

But:

P(omega_i) > 0

does not entail:

alpha(omega_i).

A probability distribution describes weights over alternatives. It does not by itself actualize an outcome.

Therefore probability structure supplies, at most, L + I structure and remains inert with respect to a determinate outcome absent A.

## 2. Chance as indeterminism

Define indeterminism minimally:

State s does not uniquely necessitate one successor state.

This requires:
- distinguishable states and alternatives: I;
- modal/consequence relations: L;
- if a successor actually obtains: A.

Therefore indeterminism presupposes the co-primitive roles and does not explain them.

Indeterminism is a property of a transition/selection structure, not an ontological ground of actuality.

## 3. Chance as stochastic physical dynamics

A stochastic physical law is already downstream in physical actuality.

Under TRT:

<L,I,A> -> chi_phys -> stochastic physical process.

Therefore a stochastic physical process cannot be used as an upstream explanation of the co-primitives or of the ultimate sourcehood relation without a valid cross-level bridge.

This is an instance of DSIR.

## 4. Chance as uncaused occurrence

Suppose:

Chance(e) := e occurs without a determining cause.

Then:
- e must be identifiable: I;
- "occurs without determining cause" is a coherent relational/modal claim: L;
- e occurs: A/alpha.

Calling the event "chance" adds no positive actuality-conferring ontology. It labels the absence of a specified determining cause.

Thus chance in this sense risks becoming gap accounting:

UnexplainedOccurrence(e) -> label "chance".

No explanatory credit is assigned without additional structure.

## 5. Chance as epistemic uncertainty

If chance means:

an observer does not know which outcome will occur,

then chance is epistemic rather than ontological.

It may model knowledge but cannot ground the actuality of an outcome.

Therefore epistemic chance is irrelevant as an ultimate source of S.

## 6. Chance as primitive propensity

A stronger ontology treats probability/propensity as a real dispositional feature.

Then the propensity itself must:
- have identity/content: I;
- stand in coherent modal relations to outcomes: L;
- obtain as an actual propensity: A/alpha.

If one outcome occurs, the propensity still does not explain actuality unless its manifestation relation includes an actuality-conferring role.

Therefore primitive propensity does not escape co-primitive dependence. It adds ontology downstream of or alongside the triad.

## 7. Chance Presupposition Principle

**CPP:**

Any ontologically meaningful chance structure involving actual alternatives and an actual outcome presupposes the co-primitive roles of differentiation, logical/modal relation, and actuality/action.

Schema:

Chance_actual(x) -> (I_role AND L_role AND A_role).

CPP does not claim every probability model contains a physical action. It claims that an actual chance event cannot be ontologically prior to the conditions required for alternatives, probability relations, and occurrence.

## 8. Consequence for selection S

The prior dichotomy:

selection is either determined or chance/random

is not exhaustive at the ontological level.

"Chance" may describe:
- probability weighting;
- absence of deterministic necessitation;
- epistemic ignorance;
- propensity;
- an unexplained occurrence.

None, merely by being called chance, supplies a non-derivative source of determination.

Therefore:

not NecessitatedByReasons(S)

does not entail:

ChanceIsSource(S).

This blocks the inference:

AOD not determined -> therefore random -> therefore not agentive.

## 9. AOD comparison

AOD proposes:

- a unified rational subject;
- apprehended alternatives;
- reasons constraining a permissible set;
- genuine act;
- source attribution to the subject;
- selection not necessarily uniquely necessitated by comparative reasons.

Chance models can still describe distributions over outcomes of agent acts, but they do not automatically replace sourcehood.

Thus the relevant comparison is:

### AOD
A positive sourcehood ontology is proposed.

### Chance
Must first specify which ontology of chance is meant and how it supplies sourcehood rather than merely non-necessitation.

The burden is symmetric: AOD cannot use "agent" as a gap label, and chance cannot use "random" as one.

## 10. Determination taxonomy

To avoid the false dichotomy, distinguish:

D1. External deterministic determination.
D2. Internal necessary determination.
D3. Stochastic/propensity-governed outcome.
D4. Brute unexplained occurrence.
D5. Agent-originating reason-responsive determination.
D6. Unknown/other determination structure.

These are not assumed exhaustive metaphysically, but they prevent "not D1/D2 -> D3" inference.

## 11. Implication for H_S

H_S cannot invoke "chance" to solve actuality discrimination for free.

If H_S uses stochastic selection, it owes:
- the probability/propensity structure;
- its grounding;
- the manifestation/outcome relation;
- actual outcome status.

Thus stochastic S may add primitives rather than reduce them.

## 12. Implication for H_M∞

H_M∞ does not prove AOD merely because chance fails as an ultimate explanation.

It still owes sourcehood.

But the standard objection:

"if reasons do not uniquely determine the choice, it is just random"

is no longer admissible without an ontology of randomness that demonstrates the equivalence.

## 13. Disposition

- Probability structure presupposes L/I and does not alone entail actual outcome: **HIGH**.
- Actual stochastic event presupposes L/I/A roles: **HIGH within TRT**.
- Indeterminism = ontological source: **REJECTED**.
- Epistemic chance = ontological source: **REJECTED**.
- Uncaused occurrence labeled chance = positive explanation: **REJECTED absent further ontology**.
- Primitive propensity escapes co-primitives: **NO**.
- CPP: **ADOPTED within TRT analysis**.
- Determined-or-random dichotomy: **REJECTED as ontologically under-specified**.
- AOD established by elimination: **NO**.
- AOD remains a live positive sourcehood model: **YES**.
