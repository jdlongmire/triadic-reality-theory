# Necessity Bridge Analysis

**WP:** WP-TRT-LRT-ONTO-0001  
**Date:** 2026-09-19  
**Disposition:** first necessity-bridge pass complete

## Question

Does TRT's distinction between possibility and actuality, together with primitive actualizing action A, entail that the ultimate source of actuality cannot itself be contingently actual, without importing a Principle of Sufficient Reason (PSR)?

## 1. Terms

Let:

- C(x): x is contingent, i.e. x is actual and possibly not actual.
- N(x): x is necessary, i.e. x is actual in every metaphysically possible world.
- G(y,x): y is an ontologically sufficient ground of x's actuality.
- U: the proposed ultimate actuality.
- A: TRT's primitive actualizing action, A: I_adm -> I_actual.

Ultimate actuality is provisionally:

UA(U) := Actual(U) AND GroundsAllDerivativeActuality(U) AND not exists y(Prior(y,U) AND G(y,U)).

The definition contains no necessity clause.

## 2. Direct TRT route

TRT establishes that actuality is a genuine status distinct from possibility and that L3 alone does not entail actuality. A is therefore required for the possibility-to-actual transition.

Attempt:

1. Possibility does not actualize itself merely by being logically admissible.
2. Derivative actuality therefore requires A.
3. U is the ultimate actuality underlying A.
4. Therefore U is necessary.

Step 4 does not follow.

Even if all derivative actuality requires A, the formal structure remains compatible with:

M1: U exists contingently and, when U exists, U actualizes derivative reality.

Nothing in the current TRT hard core states that the actualizer must itself be necessary, self-explanatory, or grounded. The core intentionally treats A as primitive. A primitive can be metaphysically necessary or brute-contingent unless a further constraint decides between them.

**Result:** TRT alone does not presently derive N(U).

## 3. Can a brute contingent U be internally incoherent?

Assume for reductio:

R1. UA(U).
R2. C(U).
R3. Therefore there is a possible world w in which not Actual(U).

No contradiction with L3 follows. At w, either no derivative actuality exists, or some alternative actuality obtains if the modal model permits it. L3 constrains the form of whatever is actual; it does not require that anything be actual. This is already explicit in the TRT hard core.

Therefore:

UA(U) AND C(U)

is not formally inconsistent with current TRT.

**Result:** the brute-contingent-ultimate model survives the internal contradiction test.

## 4. Minimal sufficient-reason bridge

A full unrestricted PSR is stronger than required.

We need only a restricted contingency-grounding principle:

**CGP:** For every x, if x is contingently actual, there exists a sufficient ontological reason or ground for x's actuality rather than its non-actuality.

Formally:

forall x [C(x) -> exists y G(y,x)].

Add a proper-ground condition:

G(y,x) -> y is explanatorily prior to x, or the sufficient reason is internal to x in a way that explains why x cannot fail to exist.

Now:

1. UA(U).
2. Suppose C(U).
3. By CGP, U has a sufficient ground/reason.
4. By UA, U has no ontologically prior ground.
5. If the sufficient reason is wholly internal and entails U's actuality, U is necessary, contradicting C(U).
6. If the sufficient reason is external/prior, UA is contradicted.
7. Therefore not C(U).
8. Given Actual(U), and under the modal partition actual beings = contingent or necessary, N(U).

This is materially narrower than unrestricted PSR. It does not require reasons for every truth, necessary truth, abstract object, negative fact, or possibility. It targets contingent actuality.

## 5. Is CGP derivable from intelligibility?

Candidate transcendental defense:

T1. To call U contingent is to assert a real modal contrast: U is actual but could have failed to be actual.
T2. The two modal states are therefore genuinely differentiated: Actual(U) versus not-Actual(U).
T3. If nothing whatsoever accounts for the selection/determination of Actual(U), then the actuality side of that contrast is brute.
T4. TRT already posits A precisely because admissibility/possibility is insufficient for determinate actuality.
T5. Allowing U itself to be contingently actual without any actualizing/grounding reason reintroduces at the ultimate level the very possibility-to-actuality gap A was introduced to mark.

This produces a strong **internal explanatory pressure** toward CGP, but it is not yet a deductive derivation. The critic can answer:

"Yes. Derivative actuality requires A, while A/U is simply a brute contingent terminus."

That position is explanatorily asymmetric, but not L3-contradictory.

**Result:** CGP is consonant with TRT and arguably extends its actuality/possibility distinction consistently to the ultimate level. It is not currently entailed by L3 + I_infinity + A.

## 6. A stronger route: redefine the target?

We could define "ultimate actuality" as actuality whose sufficient reason lies wholly in its own nature. Then necessity follows much more readily.

Reject this move for the current WP.

It would place the desired conclusion into the definition and violate the package's anti-circularity rule. The research question is whether necessity follows from ultimacy, not whether a necessarily existent thing can be defined.

## 7. Leibniz comparison

Leibniz explicitly uses sufficient reason for contingent truths and argues that an endless series of contingent details does not supply its own final sufficient reason; the final reason must be outside that contingent series and in a necessary substance (*Monadology* §§36-38). He then moves in §§39-41 from sufficient ultimate ground to uniqueness, absence of limitation, maximal reality, and perfection.

Our result converges with Leibniz at the necessity bridge but is more restrictive in its premise:

Leibniz: broad PSR -> necessary ultimate substance.

Proposed TRT-compatible route: contingent actuality requires an actuality-determining ground (CGP) -> ultimate actuality cannot be contingent -> necessary ultimate actuality.

The narrower principle should be tested independently rather than labeled "PSR" without qualification.

## 8. Modal-collapse check

CGP does not by itself entail that every actual state is necessary.

It says contingent actuality has a sufficient reason. A sufficient reason need not necessitate its consequent in every possible world unless necessitarian grounding is separately assumed. U may necessarily exist while freely, intentionally, probabilistically, or otherwise contingently actualizing derivative states.

Therefore the present bridge does not yet generate modal collapse.

However, later attempts to derive perfect rational choice or a unique "best" actual world could reintroduce collapse. This remains a watch item.

## 9. Disposition

### Derived from current TRT
- actuality is distinct from possibility;
- L3 does not entail that anything is actual;
- derivative actualization requires A within the TRT model;
- a brute-contingent ultimate actualizer is not internally contradictory.

### Additional principle identified
**CGP: Contingent actuality requires a sufficient actuality-determining ground/reason.**

### Conditional theorem
UA(U) + CGP + modal exhaustiveness + proper-ground discipline -> N(U).

### Epistemic status
- TRT-alone derivation of necessity: **FAILED / HIGH confidence**
- Conditional derivation using CGP: **MEDIUM-HIGH**
- CGP as independently established metaphysical principle: **UNCERTAIN**
- Claim that CGP is more economical than unrestricted PSR: **MEDIUM-HIGH**

## 10. Next research target

Test CGP itself.

Three attacks are required:

1. **Brute contingency:** Is "U just happens to exist" coherent, and if coherent, does coherence suffice as a defeater?
2. **Quantum objection:** Do indeterministic outcomes provide counterexamples to CGP, or do they still possess sufficient grounds for the actuality of an outcome without a sufficient reason for that particular outcome?
3. **Self-grounding/autonomous fact:** Can U be contingent yet autonomously explanatory without becoming necessary?

Only after those attacks should necessity be promoted in the dependency ledger.
