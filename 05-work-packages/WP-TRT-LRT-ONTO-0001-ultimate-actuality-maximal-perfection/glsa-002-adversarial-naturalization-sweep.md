# GLSA-002: Adversarial Naturalization Research Sweep

**WP:** WP-TRT-LRT-ONTO-0001
**Date:** 2026-09-19
**Status:** literature sweep v0.1
**Type:** B/H adversarial research
**Question:** Has a mature nonsemantic account successfully derived intrinsic semantic content without importing semantic/normative structure?

## Executive result

The literature contains serious, technically developed naturalization programmes. IAS-thin therefore cannot be dismissed by assertion.

However, the sweep does not reveal a generally accepted reduction from thin causal/informational structure to determinate intrinsic semantic content. The recurring pressure points are:
- reference/content determination;
- misrepresentation/error;
- function/normativity;
- indeterminacy;
- the distinction between information/indication and representation.

The strongest naturalistic route is teleosemantics/informational teleosemantics, not bare covariance.

## 1. Causal/informational semantics

Dretske's programme begins from informational/causal relations and attempts to move from indication/information toward representation.

Primary works identified:
- Fred Dretske, *Knowledge and the Flow of Information* (1981).
- Fred Dretske, *Explaining Behavior: Reasons in a World of Causes* (1988).

Key problem:
indication is too strong in one direction and too weak in another. If R indicates C in the strict informational sense, C obtains; misindication is impossible. Representation, however, must permit misrepresentation. Dretske therefore supplements indication with function.

TRT relevance:
bare I-thin/covariance does not close GLSA. The naturalistic account itself introduces a function layer to get error conditions.

## 2. Fodor / asymmetric dependence

Fodor's causal programme addresses the disjunction problem: if dogs and abnormal causes both cause "dog" representations, why is the content DOG rather than DOG-OR-ABNORMAL-CAUSE?

Primary work identified:
- Jerry Fodor, *A Theory of Content and Other Essays* (1990), developing asymmetric causal dependence.

The strategy enriches causal structure with higher-order dependency relations.

TRT relevance:
this is a genuine reduction candidate, not a trivial correlation theory. But reliable misrepresentation remains a documented pressure point in the contemporary literature. A theory must explain representations whose content has no successful core causal relation to an instantiated referent.

## 3. Millikan / biosemantics

Primary works identified:
- Ruth Garrett Millikan, *Language, Thought, and Other Biological Categories* (1984; MIT Press edition 1987).
- Millikan, "Biosemantics," *Journal of Philosophy* 86(6), 1989, 281-297.

Teleosemantics grounds content in proper function and historical selection. Consumer systems and their proper functions supply correctness conditions, allowing a representation to malfunction/misrepresent.

This is the strongest answer to the misrepresentation problem in the naturalization family.

TRT relevance:
teleosemantics does not derive semantics from bare physical covariance. It requires:
- historical selection;
- proper function;
- producer/consumer or related functional architecture;
- normal/proper performance conditions.

The key audit question becomes whether proper function/normative correctness is fully reducible to thin nonsemantic structure or constitutes semantic/normative thickening.

## 4. Neander / informational teleosemantics

Primary contemporary defense:
- Karen Neander, *A Mark of the Mental: In Defense of Informational Teleosemantics* (MIT Press, 2017).

Neander explicitly targets **original intentionality** for nonconceptual sensory-perceptual representations.

TRT relevance:
this is a high-value adversarial source because it directly contests the claim that original intentionality requires a prior Mind. It must be treated as a serious IAS route, not as derivative symbol semantics.

Research action:
read Neander closely before promoting any "semantics requires Mind" theorem.

## 5. Functional/inferential-role accounts

Functional theories determine content partly through causal, computational, or inferential relations among representations and possibly external states.

TRT relevance:
these theories can enrich IAS beyond simple covariance, but they face the same typed question:
- are inferential relations merely structural transitions;
- or do they already presuppose correctness/rational significance to count as semantic rather than syntactic role?

No blanket rejection is warranted.

## 6. Normativity of content

Contemporary normativity literature distinguishes:
- norms engendered by meaning/content;
- norms that determine/constitute meaning/content.

Some normativist approaches argue that nonnormative dispositions underdetermine content and that only rational/normative dispositions determine meaning.

TRT relevance:
if content-determining normativity is irreducible, GLSA thickens toward L_norm/I_sem.
If teleosemantics successfully naturalizes the relevant norms as biological proper function, IAS survives.

This is a major unresolved fork.

## 7. Phenomenal intentionality counter-programme

A substantial literature argues that causal-informational-teleological relations fail to determine the fine-grained content of representation and locates intentionality in intrinsic phenomenal properties instead.

TRT relevance:
this supports the claim that external structural relations may underdetermine intrinsic content, but it does not directly support Infinite Transcendent Mind. It is evidence against easy IAS reduction, not evidence for the full bridge.

## 8. Original versus derived intentionality

The strongest naturalization programmes explicitly seek original intentionality, so TRT must not argue:

"all nonmental representation is obviously derived from human interpretation."

That would be historically/philosophically inaccurate.

The live question is:

> Have teleological/informational accounts actually reduced original intentionality to nonsemantic structure, or have they relocated semantic/normative work into function, normal conditions, selection history, and consumer architecture?

This is the correct adversarial formulation.

## 9. Current GLSA assessment

### What is supported

1. Bare covariance/information is insufficient for representation because it lacks a robust account of misrepresentation.
2. Mature naturalization theories recognize this and add causal asymmetry, function, teleology, selection history, consumer roles, or inferential structure.
3. Content determination and misrepresentation remain active philosophical problems.
4. There is no consensus result that intrinsic semantic content has been reduced without remainder to thin nonsemantic physical/structural facts.

### What is not supported

1. "Naturalized semantics is impossible."
2. "Intentionality deductively requires a mind" from this literature alone.
3. "Teleosemantics is circular" without a detailed argument.
4. "Function is secretly intentional" merely because ordinary language uses purposive vocabulary.
5. "Original intentionality is unique to conscious human minds."

## 10. IAS impact

IAS-thin survives, but its semantic route is no longer a generic "emergence" placeholder.

Its strongest form is now approximately:

[
IAS + causal/informational structure + proper-function/selection history + consumer architecture
ightarrow I_{sem}.
]

TRT must test whether:
- proper function can be grounded non-normatively;
- content remains determinate under alternative mappings;
- misrepresentation is fully explained;
- truth-aptness follows;
- original intentionality is achieved rather than redescribed.

## 11. Bridge status

GLSA-001 SGAP remains intact.

The literature does not currently license promotion to:

[
SemanticActualityightarrow SemanticCapacity(U)
]

as a theorem.

Nor does it defeat the Mind bridge.

Current status:

[
IAS_{thin}ightarrow I_{sem}
]

is a serious research programme with unresolved burdens, not a demonstrated impossibility and not a settled reduction.
