# Origin Essentialism Bounded Stress Test and Enumeration Stop Rule

**WP:** WP-TRT-LRT-ONTO-0001
**Date:** 2026-09-23
**Status:** bounded adversarial note v0.1

## 1. Governance constraint

Representable information is effectively unbounded. The research programme therefore tests objection classes, not an open-ended enumeration of representable instances.

**Enumeration Stop Rule (ESR):**

A new representable case is admitted only if it:
1. threatens a load-bearing L/I/A relation;
2. proposes a genuinely new irreducible primitive;
3. falsifies an adopted category distinction; or
4. supplies a counterexample not reducible to an already tested class.

Otherwise the case is classified under the existing type and no further instance-level analysis is required.

Representational infinity is a domain property, not a mandate for exhaustive enumeration.

## 2. Why origin essentialism receives one bounded pass

Origin essentialism is retained as a final stress test because it may challenge the claim that constitutive identity can be represented informationally without a primitive modal edge.

The question is narrow:

> Can historical/generative origin participate in numerical identity through a non-modal determination relation?

## 3. Literature boundary

The literature does not establish a consensus theorem of origin essentialism.

Kripke motivates the view that radically different origin would yield a different individual. Forbes and others develop constitutional/origin arguments. Robertson argues that major arguments for origin essentialism fail, especially under tolerance. Penelope Mackie offers a branching/open-future explanation of the intuition while rejecting the stronger necessity thesis in favor of a weaker tenacity-of-origin position. Ballarin develops a historical/productivist account in which production history individuates historical objects and treats origin as the source of essentialist necessity.

Therefore TRT/LRT must not import "origin is necessary" as a premise.

## 4. Non-modal candidate

Let Prod(o,x) mean:

o is the actual generative/production history whose terminus is x.

Let HistID(o,x) mean:

the referential and diachronic identification of x is fixed through continuity with production history o.

Candidate constitutive edge:

DEP_origin(o,x):= Prod(o,x) AND HistID(o,x).

This edge is historical/causal and informational. It contains no modal operator.

It licenses the descriptive claim:

> x is the individual generated through o.

It does not by itself license:

> x could not have originated otherwise.

The latter remains a modal conclusion requiring argument.

## 5. Identity result

For an actual historical individual x, origin can therefore enter G_c(x) as provenance-bearing information without assuming origin essentialism.

G_c(x) may contain:
- ID: referential identity of x;
- DEP: actual production history o -> x;
- STR: continuity relations internal to o;
- TYPE: relevant organism/artifact kind information.

This is enough to represent actual historical identity.

It is not enough to prove transworld origin necessity.

Thus:

OriginInfo(x) != OriginNecessity(x).

## 6. Effect on CCAC

Suppose x' is represented with a different production history o'.

Two questions must be separated:

1. Is x' a coherent individual generated by o'?
2. Is x' numerically identical to actual x?

Constraint closure may answer (1) positively while leaving (2) dependent on the adopted identity criterion.

Therefore origin essentialism does not currently yield:

Coh_c(x') AND not D(x').

At most it may yield:

D(x') AND x' != x.

That is not a counterexample to CCAC. It is an identity classification.

## 7. Stronger productivist proposal

Ballarin's historical/productivist approach suggests that historical entities may be individuated by their processes of production.

TRT/LRT can represent this as a candidate identity rule:

For historical x, ProductionTrace(x) participates in IdFix(x).

But this rule must remain a domain hypothesis until independently defended. It cannot be generalized to all entities.

## 8. Tolerance problem

Origin theories face the tolerance question: if slight variation in origin is allowed, repeated small variations threaten to bridge to radically different origins.

This shows that origin is not presently suitable as a crisp universal identity primitive without further theory.

For TRT/LRT this is sufficient reason not to make origin a new co-primitive.

## 9. Disposition

Origin essentialism does not presently force a fourth primitive.

What survives is narrower:

- historical/generative origin is representable information;
- actual production relations can enter G_c as DEP/ID provenance;
- whether those relations are essential to transworld numerical identity is a separate modal/identity thesis;
- disagreement about that thesis does not defeat the R/D/X or L/I/A architecture.

**Result:** NO NEW PRIMITIVE ESTABLISHED.

## 10. Stop decision

The representability/essence/origin branch has now tested the major objection classes needed for the present proof burden.

Further instance enumeration is suspended under ESR.

Reopen only if a candidate case supplies:
- a contradiction-free constitutive graph independently shown unactualizable;
- a constitutive relation that cannot in principle be represented informationally;
- a demonstrated modal fact irreducible to L/I/A;
- or a failure of the IF/CAN/WOULD/IS distinctions.

## 11. Consolidated research state

The bounded result is:

R: representable informational structures.

D: candidate domain of constitutively coherent/actualizable structures.

X: actualized structures.

L constrains informational specification and closure.

I supplies differentiation, content, identity-relevant and constitutive information.

A distinguishes actuality from mere actualizability.

The principal open conjecture remains:

Coh_c(x) ?=> D(x).

No independent M(x) has been established.

The programme should now return to the larger LRT derivation rather than continue enumerating representable modal cases.
