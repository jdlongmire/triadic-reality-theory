# NGT-001: Necessary Ground Theorem

**WP:** WP-TRT-LRT-ONTO-0001
**Date:** 2026-09-19
**Status:** theorem attempt v0.1
**Type:** B/D bridge analysis

## Research constraint

This theorem attempt may not use:
- PSR/CGP;
- Kalam;
- perfect-being axioms;
- Gödelian positive-property machinery;
- theological premises;
- divine necessity by definition.

The question is internal to the TRT typed transcendental/ground architecture.

## 1. Target

Given a constitutive package G required for determinate actuality, what modal status must an **essential adequate ultimate ground** U of G possess?

Target:

[
EssentialUltimateGround(U,mathcal G)
stackrel{?}{Longrightarrow}
square U.
]

The word **essential** is load-bearing and must itself be analyzed rather than smuggled into the conclusion.

## 2. Typed premises

### P-level hard core

For any determinate actuality D:

[
Dightarrowmathcal G_P
]

where minimally:

[
mathcal G_P=langlemathcal L,mathcal I,mathcal Aangle.
]

CPIT establishes pair-failure/irreducibility relative to D.

### D-level consequences

FAA/CUB/CPGT establish that any proposed ultimate ground must account for the complete constitutive workload and its co-unity.

### B-level ground candidate

Let:

[
Ground(U,mathcal G)
]

be a bridge hypothesis.

U is not a Type-P presupposition merely by being proposed.

## 3. Distinguish conditional necessity from simpliciter necessity

TRT presently has:

[
square[Dightarrowmathcal G].
]

This does not by itself give:

[
squaremathcal G
]

if a null world/no-D condition is metaphysically possible.

Therefore the immediate theorem cannot simply infer necessary U from necessary G unless necessity of G simpliciter is separately established.

Two theorem forms must be distinguished.

### NGT-C: conditional necessary ground

[
square[Dightarrow EssentialGround(U,mathcal G)]
]

may support:

[
square[Dightarrow U].
]

### NGT-S: simpliciter necessary ground

[
EssentialUltimateGround(U,mathcal G)
ightarrowsquare U.
]

NGT-S is stronger and requires handling null actuality.

## 4. Escape route A: G without U

Suppose there is a possible determinate actuality w in which:

[
mathcal G(w)land
eg U(w).
]

Then either:
1. G is ungrounded in w;
2. G is grounded by U' in w.

If U was claimed to be the **essential** ground of G across determinate actuality, case 1 contradicts that essential-ground claim.

Case 2 becomes ground substitution.

Thus:

[
EssentialGround(U,mathcal G)
ightarrow
square[Dightarrow U]
]

is close to analytic if "EssentialGround" explicitly means invariant ground wherever D/G obtains.

But this yields only conditional necessity relative to D.

**Result:** NGT-C survives.

## 5. Escape route B: ground substitution

Suppose:

[
Ground(U,mathcal G,w_1)
]

but:

[
Ground(U',mathcal G,w_2)
]

with U absent in w2.

Then U is not the invariant essential ground of G. The explanatory role is occupied by a higher-order ground schema:

[
GroundRole(_,mathcal G)
]

whose realizers vary by world.

Questions:
- What makes U and U' adequate realizers of the same ground role?
- Is the role itself more fundamental than either?
- What grounds the invariance of the role/function?
- Does variable realization merely move ultimacy from U/U' to the trans-world role/structure?

Ground substitution is coherent in principle. It defeats **U's** necessity unless Necessary Ground Identity (NGI) is independently established.

However, it does not remove the need for a non-derivative ground-role adequate to G in every D-world.

**Result:** individual-ground necessity not yet established; necessary ground-role is stronger.

## 6. Escape route C: ground pluralism

Suppose:

[
g_Lightarrowmathcal L,quad
g_Iightarrowmathcal I,quad
g_Aightarrowmathcal A.
]

CPIT/CUB require:

[
C(g_L,g_I,g_A)
]

to account for their necessary co-operation in D.

Pluralism therefore yields a compound ground system:

[
U_C=langle g_L,g_I,g_A,Cangle.
]

The relevant ground candidate becomes the whole coordinated system, not any isolated member.

Ground pluralism does not defeat the need for an adequate ground; it changes its internal ontology.

It also does not by itself prove one simple bearer.

**Result:** pluralism survives, but CUB prevents primitive-count evasion.

## 7. Escape route D: null actuality

Suppose a possible world w0 has no determinate actuality D.

Then:

[
Dightarrowmathcal G
]

is vacuously satisfied at w0 and does not require U there.

Therefore:

[
square[Dightarrow U]
]

does not entail:

[
square U.
]

This is the strongest blocker to NGT-S.

Earlier null-world analysis did not prove absolute nullity impossible.

**Result:** NGT-S does not currently follow from the conditional transcendental core alone.

## 8. Can the modal domain itself close the null-world gap?

Candidate move:

A "possible null world" is still represented as a modal alternative and therefore presupposes representability/logical distinction in the evaluating modal order.

But this establishes meta-level G, not necessarily U inside w0.

Using it to infer U(w0) risks the same metalanguage/object-language slide previously rejected.

**Result:** blocked.

## 9. Necessary Ground Role Theorem

A stronger result survives without solving null actuality.

Define:

[
UGR(mathcal G)
]

as the adequate non-derivative ground-role required wherever determinate actuality obtains.

Then:

[
oxed{
square[Dightarrow UGR(mathcal G)]
}
]

follows from:
- the transcendental core;
- FAP;
- TASR;
- CUB.

This is a **Necessary Ground Role Theorem (NGRT)**, conditional on D.

It does not yet identify a numerically identical U across worlds.

## 10. Essential Ground Invariance

To reach:

[
square[Dightarrow U]
]

for one U, add:

**EGI, Essential Ground Invariance:**

The ultimate ground of the constitutive package is numerically/essentially invariant across all D-worlds.

Then:

[
NGRT+EGI
ightarrow
square[Dightarrow U].
]

EGI is not yet derived.

Possible routes:
- uniqueness of adequate ground;
- identity of essence across realizers;
- impossibility of ground substitution without a higher common ground;
- Mind/source-unity argument.

These are next-stage obligations.

## 11. From conditional to simpliciter necessity

Even with EGI:

[
square[Dightarrow U]

does not yield:

[

### Route N1

necessary actuality.

Not yet established.

## 13. Why this is useful

The negative result prevents a modal overreach.

TRT can reach a **necessarily required ground wherever determinate actuality obtains** before it can reach a **being that exists in every possible world**.

That distinction is exactly where the ontological convergence bridge becomes substantive rather than redundant.

The perfect-being/modal track may later supply or independently converge on the missing simpliciter necessity, but it cannot be backfilled into NGT-001.

## 15. v0.2 correction: ANNAL removes the null-world blocker

The canonical R/D/X distinction now resolves the earlier null-world issue.

Let N be the represented content "absolute nothingness":

\[
N:=\neg\exists x\,X(x).
\]

ANNAL establishes:

\[
R(N)\land\neg D(N)\land\neg X(N).
\]

Therefore absolute nothingness is not a member of the metaphysically actualizable determinate-world domain W_D.

Hence:

\[
\boxed{\square_D D}.
\]

Combine with NGRT:

\[
\square_D[D\rightarrow UGR(\mathcal G)]
\]

to obtain:

\[
\boxed{\square_D UGR(\mathcal G)}.
\]

Thus an adequate non-derivative ground-role is necessary across the full actualizable determinate domain.

The remaining quantifier problem is:

\[
\square_D\exists U\,Ground(U,\mathcal G)
\not\Rightarrow
\exists U\,\square_D Ground(U,\mathcal G).
\]

Accordingly, the critical next target is EGI/uniqueness: whether the adequate ground-role can have numerically distinct realizers across actualizable worlds.

The earlier statement that "null actuality is the strongest blocker to NGT-S" is withdrawn.
