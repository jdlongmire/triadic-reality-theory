# Selection Bridge: From Determinate Actuality to Will/Agency

**WP:** WP-TRT-LRT-ONTO-0001  
**Date:** 2026-09-19  
**Track:** convergence / mind-ground model  
**Target:** S -> rational selection -> will/agency?  
**Status:** first-pass analysis complete

## Question

If multiple mutually incompatible admissible contents are available to an ultimate rational informational active Mind, and one derivative physical actuality obtains, what must the selection relation S be?

Can S remain impersonal? Does rational selection require reasons? Does reason-responsive selection warrant will or agency?

No freedom doctrine, libertarianism, decree, moral goodness, or omnipotence is assumed.

## 1. Selection requirement

Let D contain mutually incompatible physically instantiable alternatives p and q:

Admissible(p).
Admissible(q).
not CoActualizable_phys(p,q).

If one determinate physical actuality obtains, then the mapping from D to chi_phys is non-total with respect to mutually exclusive alternatives.

Therefore some discriminator is required:

S: D -> selected-for-domain-instantiation subset.

This is a role statement. It does not yet identify S's ontology.

## 2. Candidate models of S

### S0: Brute selection fact
p is instantiated rather than q with no further selector, rule, reason, probability structure, or ground.

This is coherent as a stopping claim but supplies no explanation. Under BBR it receives no explanatory credit merely from being called brute.

### S1: Deterministic impersonal rule
A rule R maps the admissible state to exactly one selected state.

Questions:
- What grounds R?
- Why this rule rather than another?
- Does R presuppose L/I/A?
- Is R part of T or an additional primitive?

If R is downstream physical law, it cannot explain initial physical instantiation without circularity.

If R is ground-level, H_S must add/derive it.

### S2: Stochastic impersonal selection
A probability measure P over D yields an outcome.

This requires:
- differentiated alternatives I;
- probability structure;
- rule for measure assignment;
- actuality of an outcome.

Stochasticity can model non-necessitation but does not by itself explain why this measure or why any outcome is actual.

### S3: Optimization / value-sensitive rule
A criterion V ranks or constrains admissible alternatives.

This introduces:
- an ordering/evaluation;
- a standard of better/worse or fit;
- a relation between reasons/values and selection.

If V is objective and ground-level, its ontology must be accounted for.

### S4: Rational intentional selection
A rational subject apprehends alternatives as alternatives, has reasons/ends, and intentionally selects an instantiation.

This is the candidate will/agency model.

## 3. Does selection alone imply agency?

No.

S0-S3 are conceptual alternatives.

Therefore:

S -/-> agency.

**Status:** HIGH.

## 4. Does rational selection imply reasons?

Need a distinction.

A selection made **by a rational subject as rational** is not merely an event occurring in that subject. To count as rational selection, the selection must be appropriately responsive to reasons, ends, standards, or intelligible considerations.

Thus:

RationalSelection(M,p) -> ReasonResponsive(M,p)

is close to constitutive.

But:

SelectionByRationalBeing(M,p) -/-> RationalSelection(M,p).

A rational being could, conceptually, undergo a random or non-rational selection event.

Therefore rationality of the selector does not automatically rationalize every selection.

## 5. Reason-responsiveness and agency

Suppose:
- M apprehends p and q as alternatives;
- M apprehends reasons/ends bearing on p and q;
- the selection tracks those reasons as M's selection;
- the resulting designation is attributable to M rather than merely occurring within M.

Then the structure contains:
- alternatives;
- intentional content;
- evaluative/reason relation;
- authorship/attributability;
- selection.

This is substantially what is ordinarily meant by purposive agency.

Define minimal agency:

**Agency_min(M,S):** M is the source to which a reason-responsive selection among apprehended alternatives is attributable.

Then:

IntentionalAlternativeApprehension
+ ReasonResponsiveSelection
+ SourceAttribution
-> Agency_min.

This is partly definitional, so the empirical/metaphysical burden lies in establishing the antecedents, especially source attribution.

## 6. Agency and will

Define minimal will without importing libertarian freedom:

**Will_min:** a rational agent's capacity for intentional selection/action in light of apprehended ends/reasons.

Then:

Agency_min + stable capacity for such selection -> Will_min.

This does not establish:
- libertarian freedom;
- ability to choose irrationally;
- moral perfection;
- temporal deliberation;
- changing mental states.

It establishes only a minimal volitional category.

## 7. Does an ultimate Mind require S4 rather than S0-S3?

Not deductively from Mind alone.

But compare explanatory fit.

### S0 with M∞
An ultimate rational Mind exists, apprehends alternatives, but which physical world obtains is a brute fact unrelated to its rational activity.

This weakens the claim that M∞ grounds chi_phys; the decisive physical-instantiation fact remains outside Mind's explanatory role.

### S1 with M∞
An impersonal deterministic rule determines physical actuality independently of Mind.

Then either:
- R is external to M∞, compromising ultimacy; or
- R is intrinsic to M∞, in which case the question becomes whether R expresses Mind's rational nature/act.

### S2 with M∞
An impersonal probability measure selects outcomes.

Again the measure is either external, additional, or intrinsic to M∞. If intrinsic, its relation to Mind must be explained.

### S3 with M∞
Value-sensitive optimization fits rationality more closely but introduces value/normativity. If the evaluative standard is external, ultimacy is threatened; if intrinsic, the selection begins to resemble reason-responsive agency.

### S4 with M∞
Selection is intrinsic to the same rational informational active subject proposed as ultimate ground. This offers the most direct unification of:
- apprehension of alternatives;
- reasons/ends;
- selection;
- instantiation bridge.

This is an abductive/unification advantage, not a deductive proof.

## 8. External-constraint test

For any selection principle R governing M∞:

If R is ontologically external and prior to M∞, M∞ is not ultimate in the relevant respect.

If R is intrinsic to M∞, then selection is constrained by M∞'s nature/reasons rather than an independent external selector.

This produces a useful dilemma for a truly ultimate Mind:

Selection governance is either:
1. external -> ultimacy debt;
2. brute/unrelated -> explanatory debt;
3. intrinsic -> supports reason/nature-responsive selection.

Option 3 does not yet prove will, but it is the strongest fit with H_M∞.

## 9. Modal collapse

If M∞ is necessary and its rational nature necessarily determines one unique best selection, then:

Box M∞ + unique necessary rational selection -> Box chi_phys

may threaten contingency.

Therefore a viable Mind-ground model needs one of:
- multiple rationally permissible selections;
- reasons that incline without necessitating;
- contingent volitional determination;
- another non-collapsing selection structure.

No option is adopted yet.

This is where freedom becomes a genuine research issue rather than an imported doctrine.

## 10. Selection and perfection

Do not infer:

rational selection -> perfect selection.

Perfection remains downstream. However, if later axiological work establishes perfect goodness and unlimited rationality, S becomes a locus where those attributes would constrain action.

## 11. Comparison with H_S

H_S must account for S in addition to L/I/A/C if determinate physical actuality selects among alternatives.

Its options are likewise brute fact, deterministic rule, stochastic rule, optimization, or another selector.

Therefore selection is not a problem created by the Mind hypothesis. It is a general actuality-discrimination problem.

H_M∞ has a candidate internal locus for S: the same subject that apprehends the alternatives.

That is an explanatory advantage only if intentional/reason-responsive selection is independently defensible.

## 12. Disposition

- Determinate instantiation among incompatible alternatives requires a discriminator S: **MEDIUM-HIGH**.
- S -> agency: **NO**.
- Rational selection -> reason-responsiveness: **HIGH by constitutive analysis**.
- Reason-responsive attributable selection -> minimal agency: **MEDIUM-HIGH**, with source attribution as key burden.
- Minimal agency + capacity -> minimal will: **MEDIUM-HIGH as category analysis**.
- Ultimate Mind -> S4 intentional selection: **NOT DEDUCTIVELY ESTABLISHED**.
- S4 provides stronger unification under H_M∞ than external/brute S: **MEDIUM abductive claim**.
- Libertarian freedom: **OPEN**.
- Modal collapse under unique necessary selection: **LIVE RISK**.

## 13. Next research target

The next discriminator is **source attribution**:

> What makes a selection an act of the Mind rather than an event merely occurring within or alongside the Mind?

This requires analysis of agent causation, reasons as causes/grounds, and whether an ultimate subject can be the non-derivative source of selection without an external determining rule.

A parallel task should test the modal-collapse problem: whether rational agency permits multiple rationally admissible choices without defect.
