# GLSA-003: Millikan 1989 Biosemantics Close Read

**WP:** WP-TRT-LRT-ONTO-0001
**Date:** 2026-09-19
**Status:** primary-source adversarial analysis v0.1
**Type:** B/H literature audit

## Source

Ruth Garrett Millikan, "Biosemantics," *The Journal of Philosophy* 86(6), June 1989, 281-297. DOI/JSTOR: 10.2307/2027123.

Primary-text access verified through a scan of the journal article. Secondary interpretation is separated below.

## 1. Millikan's target

Millikan is explicitly attempting a naturalist account of mental representation, especially the problem of false representation.

Her opening diagnosis is important: causal/informational theories need a principled way to identify the **content-fixing circumstances** under which a representation's represented is connected to its production.

She rejects simple statistical normality as arbitrary. The content-fixing conditions must be non-arbitrarily determined.

This is directly relevant to GLSA: bare covariance/information is not Millikan's own solution.

## 2. Core move: teleology/proper function

Millikan moves to conditions of proper/well-functioning representation-producing and representation-consuming systems.

The naturalization strategy is historical:
- functions are tied to the history that explains why a reproduced biological device/type persists;
- representational content is connected to the proper function of systems that use/consume representations;
- normal conditions are not mere statistical averages but conditions relevant to successful proper functioning.

Thus the semantic bridge is not:

covariance -> meaning.

It is closer to:

selected/reproduced biological organization
+ proper function
+ producer/consumer coordination
+ normal conditions
-> representational content.

## 3. Consumer emphasis

A major Millikan move is that representation must be understood through what consumes/uses the representation, not only through what causes it.

The represented conditions are tied to what must obtain for the consumer's proper functions to be performed in the historically relevant way.

TRT significance:
this is stronger than simple causal semantics because content is fixed partly by the role representation plays in an organized system.

But it introduces a richer architecture:
- producer;
- representation;
- consumer;
- proper function;
- success conditions;
- historical reproduction/selection.

IAS-thin must therefore contain more than thin covariance.

## 4. Misrepresentation

The teleological framework is designed to allow a representation token to occur when the represented condition fails to obtain.

That is possible because content is fixed by proper function/history rather than by an exceptionless current covariance.

This is a genuine strength relative to strict indication theories.

TRT consequence:
we cannot claim that naturalistic semantics simply cannot explain error. Millikan supplies a substantive error model.

## 5. The normativity issue

Proper function supports a distinction between:
- successful/proper performance;
- malfunction/error.

The naturalization burden is therefore displaced onto the ontology of proper function.

Millikan's companion 1989 paper, "In Defense of Proper Functions," explicitly defends a historical theoretical definition of function rather than a conceptual analysis.

The central adversarial question is:

> Does selected-effect/proper-function history fully naturalize the relevant correctness norm, or does semantic/normative content remain in the specification of what counts as the proper effect, consumer success, and normal condition?

This cannot be answered by noting that the vocabulary sounds purposive.

## 6. TRT typed-presupposition audit

Millikan's biological machinery is Type H: a proposed account inside determinate actualized reality.

It already presupposes the TRT hard-core roles:
- identity/differentiation of organisms, traits, producers, consumers, histories;
- logical relations;
- actual biological events and reproduction.

That does **not** invalidate biosemantics as an intra-domain explanation.

The anti-smuggling issue arises only if biological teleosemantics is promoted to an ultimate ground-level explanation of I_sem while the historical/functional machinery itself depends on semantic/normative capacities it has not reduced.

Therefore the correct question is reduction, not "biology presupposes L/I/A, so biosemantics fails."

## 7. Ground-level relevance

Millikan can potentially show:

nonsemantic physical/biological history
-> original biological/mental representation.

If successful, this gives IAS a route from thin ground structure to semantic derivative actuality without semantic capacity in the ultimate ground.

That would weaken GLSA's Mind bridge.

But this route is derivative and historical. The ground-level extrapolation requires:
- an account of how proper function originates in a universe before biological selection;
- proof that all intrinsic semantic content is of the teleofunctional kind;
- no irreducible semantic/normative residue in the functional definitions.

Millikan's 1989 article does not by itself establish those broader metaphysical claims.

## 8. Potential circularity tests

Do not accuse Millikan of circularity merely because she uses:
- function;
- purpose-like vocabulary;
- consumers;
- normal conditions.

Instead test:

### C1. Function specification
Can proper function be specified entirely through reproduction/selection history without semantic notions?

### C2. Consumer success
Can "successful use" be specified without reference to what the representation means?

### C3. Normal conditions
Can normal conditions be fixed non-arbitrarily without content-dependent specification?

### C4. Content determinacy
Does the historical-functional story uniquely determine one content rather than many extensionally/behaviorally compatible contents?

### C5. Truth-aptness
Does the account yield proposition-like truth conditions, or primarily biological correctness/incorrectness?

These are the substantive pressure points.

## 9. Strong result for TRT

Millikan defeats one weak TRT argument:

**Rejected:** "Misrepresentation is impossible on naturalism."

Biosemantics offers a coherent naturalistic model in which misrepresentation is malfunction relative to proper function.

But Millikan does not yet defeat SGAP because the naturalization burden moves to:
- proper function;
- content determinacy;
- normal conditions;
- consumer success;
- scope from biological representation to full truth-bearing intentionality.

## 11. Current verdict

### Millikan succeeds at:
- providing a nontrivial naturalistic content theory;
- explaining how misrepresentation can occur without requiring current perfect covariance;
- grounding semantic correctness in a historical-functional framework;
- making consumer use central to content.

### Still open:
- whether proper function is fully nonnormative at the base;
- unique content determination;
- breadth from biological representation to all intrinsic intentionality;
- proposition-level truth-aptness;
- whether the account naturalizes original intentionality without semantic residue.

### TRT consequence

IAS remains live and stronger after the close read.

No deductive Mind bridge may proceed by asserting that error/misrepresentation requires Mind.

The next adversarial source should be Neander 2017 because she explicitly develops informational teleosemantics as an account of original intentionality and directly addresses several of these pressure points.
