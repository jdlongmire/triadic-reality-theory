# Co-Primitive Ground Theorem Candidate (CPGT)

**WP:** WP-TRT-LRT-ONTO-0001
**Date:** 2026-09-19
**Status:** theorem candidate v0.1

## 1. Inputs

Canonical category backbone:

R = Representable.
D = Actualizable.
X = Actualized.

R ⊇ D ⊇ X.

Theorem legs:

**DRT:** Box[X -> I_R].

**DAT:** Box[X -> L_D], with L_D containing at minimum the exact same-respect L3 determinacy constraints.

**AAT:** Box[X -> A_X].

Where multiple mutually incompatible alternatives in D compete for one domain actuality, selection/discrimination S is required.

C denotes the co-unity of the roles in one reality-generating order.

## 2. Co-Primitive Necessity Result

From DRT, DAT, and AAT:

**CPN:**

Box[X -> (I_R AND L_D AND A_X)].

This is the first assembled co-primitive theorem at the role level.

It does not yet establish:
- Box I_R simpliciter;
- Box L_D simpliciter absent actuality;
- Box A_X simpliciter;
- one bearer;
- Mind.

## 3. Selection extension

If:
- D contains p and q;
- p and q are mutually incompatible in actuality domain d;
- X_d is determinate;

then a discriminator S_d is required to account for which member(s) of D are instantiated in X_d.

Conditional:

MultipleIncompatibleActualizable(D,d)
AND Determinate(X_d)
-> S_d-role.

Thus the extended actuality workload is:

G = <I_R,L_D,A_X,S,C>.

S is conditional on genuine alternative plurality. It is not required merely to state the basic three-leg CPN.

## 4. Co-unity

The three theorem legs are not independent descriptions of unrelated worlds.

For any x in X:
- x is representable/differentiated;
- x satisfies logical determinacy;
- x is actual.

These roles co-occur in the same actuality.

Call:

C_X(I_R,L_D,A_X).

At minimum C_X is a fact of co-instantiation.

The stronger claim that C is one ontologically simple essence is NOT yet derived.

## 5. Ground requirement

If an ultimate ground g is proposed for X, FAA/FAP requires:

UltimateGround(g,X)
->
AdequatelyGrounds(g,I_R,L_D,A_X,S,C)

where S is included when alternative plurality obtains.

This is the **Co-Primitive Ground Requirement**.

It does not yet entail that such a ground is Mind.

## 6. Transcendence constraint

Derivative physical systems in X cannot be ontologically prior grounds of the conditions required for X without circularity.

By DSIR, LEE, DEE, and AEE:

Ground(g,G) AND Adequate(g)
-> g is not merely a derivative physical system whose existence already presupposes G.

Call this candidate:

**TC, Transcendence Constraint:**

AdequateUltimateGround(g,G)
-> NonDerivative(g) AND OntologicallyPriorToDerivativePhysicalReality(g).

"Transcendent" here means ontological priority/non-derivation, not spatial exteriority.

## 7. Rival theorem branch

Two live same-level branches remain:

### H_S
One nonmental transcendent structural ground E_S derives/unifies G.

### H_M
One transcendent Mind M_T intrinsically unifies rational form, content, act, selection, and subject-unity.

CPGT v0.1 does not decide between them.

## 8. Mind-identification target

Strong target:

forall g [
AdequateUltimateGround(g,G)
->
MindLike(g)
].

This is NOT YET PROVEN.

To prove it, every nonmental same-level model must either:
- fail at least one FAP function;
- add independent primitives that defeat claimed unity/economy;
- or instantiate independently defined mind-like markers.

MindLike must not be defined as merely "satisfies G."

## 10. Strongest current conclusion

> Any determinate actualized physical reality necessarily instantiates differentiated representability, logical determinacy, and actuality/act. Where mutually incompatible actualizable alternatives exist, determinate domain actuality additionally requires discrimination among them. Any proposed ultimate ground must account non-circularly for these functions and their co-instantiation; it cannot appeal solely to derivative physical systems or emergence processes that already presuppose the functions being grounded. This establishes a transcendent/non-derivative ground requirement if an ultimate ground is posited, but it does not yet establish that the ground is Mind.
