## Source

Karen Neander, *A Mark of the Mental: In Defense of Informational Teleosemantics* (MIT Press, 2017).

Primary/publisher materials verified:
- MIT Press/Oxford Academic book record and chapter abstracts.
- Duke author/book description.

Critical secondary checks:
- Manolo Martinez, Notre Dame Philosophical Reviews (2019).
- Mendelovici & Bourget, *Philosophical Review* 128(3) (2019).
- Justin Garson, *Philosophy of Science* 85(4) (2018).

## 1. Scope discipline

Neander does **not** claim a complete naturalization of all intentionality.

Her target is deliberately modest:
- original intentionality;
- referential-intentional content;
- sensory-perceptual/nonconceptual representation.

Conceptual thought, belief, and desire are outside the primary explanandum.

This materially limits what GLSA may infer from the book.

## 2. The challenge to TRT

Neander directly targets a key possible Mind bridge:

> Can original intentionality arise in neurobiological systems without prior semantic interpretation by another mind?

Her answer is yes for the targeted class, through informational teleosemantics.

If successful, the TRT inference:

derived original intentionality -> semantic capacity in ultimate ground

is weakened.

Therefore Neander is a stronger adversary than accounts limited to derived symbol semantics.

## 3. Core architecture

Neander combines:
- proper biological function;
- causal-information relations;
- second-order similarity/state-space structure.

Her producer teleosemantics emphasizes the proper function of representation-producing systems.

A sensory-perceptual producer has the function of producing internal state changes caused by and structurally related to relevant distal features.

The content is therefore constrained by:
- what the producer is for;
- causal relations to represented features;
- structural similarity among representational and represented domains.

This is substantially richer than covariance.

## 4. Error and malfunction

Neander explicitly requires:
- error-permitting representation;
- malfunction-permitting function.

This avoids identifying representation with factive information.

A system can misrepresent because its representational producer can malfunction while still having the relevant proper function.

TRT consequence:
misrepresentation alone cannot establish irreducible Mind.

## 5. Methodology-to-metaphysics move

Neander argues partly from explanatory practice in cognitive science:
- cognitive science posits nonconceptual representations;
- dysfunction explanations presuppose malfunction-permitting function;
- information-processing explanation uses representational content.

Her chapter 4 explicitly includes a "From Methodology to Metaphysics" step.

This is a load-bearing point for adversarial review.

The fact that a representational/functional vocabulary is explanatorily fruitful in cognitive science does not automatically establish that its semantic properties reduce metaphysically to the naturalistic base.

The methodological argument must therefore be distinguished from the reduction claim.

## 6. Content determinacy

Neander does not ignore content-indeterminacy objections. Chapters 7-9 are explicitly devoted to six content-determinacy challenges.

This defeats another weak criticism:

"teleosemantics has no answer to why R means C rather than Q."

She has an extended answer programme.

In particular, distal-content problems are addressed rather than assumed away.

TRT must engage the actual solutions, not merely state the indeterminacy objection.

## 7. Producer versus consumer

Neander's producer teleosemantics differs from Millikan's more consumer-oriented account.

The content-fixing emphasis lies more heavily on what the producer is biologically supposed to detect/indicate under proper-function conditions.

This potentially reduces dependence on downstream purposive consumer interpretation.

That makes Neander especially relevant to GLSA because it is a stronger candidate for non-interpreter-dependent original content.

## 8. Information terminology

A critical review notes that Neander's "information" is thin, essentially causal rather than Shannon-theoretic.

This actually aligns with TRT's need to keep I_R distinct from Shannon information.

But it means the semantic work is not being done by "information" alone. It is performed by the package:

causation + proper function + structural similarity + representational role.

TRT should not criticize the theory as though Neander claims raw Shannon information yields semantics.

## 10. Critical pressure: scope

Even if Neander succeeds fully, the demonstrated target is sensory-perceptual nonconceptual content.

It does not thereby naturalize:
- conceptual propositional thought;
- reasons-as-reasons;
- abstract logical content;
- mathematical intentionality;
- normative inference;
- world-level selection;
- subject-sourcehood.

Therefore success would defeat a universal claim that **all original intentionality requires prior Mind**, but would not automatically defeat the broader MindLike bridge based on the full thick package.

## 11. Critical pressure: function

Neander relies centrally on proper function.

The naturalization therefore inherits the philosophy-of-biology burden:
- what makes a function proper?
- can selected-effect/history fully naturalize malfunction/correctness?
- can function be specified without semantic/normative circularity?

Neander explicitly argues that proper functions are naturalistically acceptable and scientifically employed.

TRT cannot reject this merely because function language sounds normative.

The issue requires independent function ontology analysis.

## 12. Critical pressure: everyday intentionality

Mendelovici & Bourget argue that:
- Neander's theory yields incorrect predictions in some cases;
- her methodological argument does not succeed;
- it is unclear whether the account connects with the everyday phenomenon of intentionality that motivates the project.

This is a serious critical result but secondary, not a primary-text conclusion.

TRT should record it as a live objection, not adjudicated fact.

## 13. Critical pressure: methodological sufficiency

A scientific theory can legitimately posit representation for explanatory purposes even if semantic properties are not metaphysically reducible to the physical/functional properties cited.

Thus:

ExplanatoryUsefulness(Representation)
-/->
Reduction(Semantics,PhysicalFunction).

Neander needs the metaphysical bridge, not only methodological indispensability.

Her book explicitly recognizes and attempts this bridge, so this is a test point rather than a dismissal.

## 14. GLSA impact

### Claims TRT must not make

- "Original intentionality has no naturalistic theory."
- "Misrepresentation cannot be naturalized."
- "Content determinacy is ignored by teleosemantics."
- "Neander merely assumes an external interpreter."
- "Information alone is supposed to do all semantic work."

### Claims still defensible

- Neander's demonstrated scope is deliberately limited.
- The account depends on a rich functional/causal/structural package.
- Proper-function naturalization remains load-bearing.
- Methodological representational practice does not alone prove metaphysical reduction.
- Success for nonconceptual sensory content would not establish reduction of reasons, propositional truth-bearing thought, or sourcehood.

## 15. Current verdict

Neander significantly strengthens IAS and prevents TRT from using a simple original/derived intentionality dichotomy as a deductive Mind bridge.

But GLSA is not defeated.

The semantic bridge must now be partitioned:

### GLSA-S1
Can nonconceptual sensory-perceptual original intentionality be naturalized?

Neander provides a serious affirmative model.

### GLSA-S2
Can propositional/truth-bearing conceptual content be naturalized from the same thin base?

OPEN.

### GLSA-S3
Can reasons-as-reasons and rational normativity be naturalized?

OPEN.

### GLSA-S4
Can source-attributable intentional agency be naturalized?

OPEN.

The MindLike bridge should focus increasingly on the joint thick package rather than sensory representation alone.
