## 4. Four substitution models

### Model E1: accidental common adequacy

U1 and U2 are fundamentally different kinds of reality that independently happen to satisfy F_G.

This is logically conceivable.

But "happen to satisfy" cannot itself function as an explanation of invariant ground adequacy. If no deeper common basis exists, world-relative adequacy is primitive at the modal level.

Status: coherent stopping model; explanatory debt remains.

### Model E2: shared ground-essence

There is an invariant essence E_G such that:

[
Instantiates(U_1,E_G,w_1)
]

[
Instantiates(U_2,E_G,w_2)
]

and E_G explains why each grounds G.

Then the explanatory invariant is E_G rather than the accidental identity of either realizer.

Question:

Is E_G itself merely abstract/inert, or is it the actual ground-nature instantiated by each U?

If E_G is only a description/classification, it does not replace the realizers.

If E_G has grounding efficacy, it begins to look like the deeper invariant ground.

Status: promising route to invariant ground-nature, not yet one numerically identical U.

### Model E3: ground-role as invariant

No shared essence is posited; only the role:

[
UGR(mathcal G)
]

is invariant and different entities realize it.

This preserves:

[
square_Dexists U,Ground(U,mathcal G)
]

without:

[
exists Usquare_D Ground(U,mathcal G).
]

The role itself may be a modal abstraction rather than an efficacious ground.

Status: coherent unless role-realization requires deeper invariant ontology.

### Model E4: one trans-world ground

The same U grounds G across all W_D:

[
exists Usquare_D Ground(U,mathcal G).
]

This is EGI's target, but cannot be selected merely for elegance.

## 5. Does ultimacy defeat substitution?

Suppose U1 and U2 each have everything required to be ultimate in their worlds.

World-relative ultimacy does not logically prohibit different ultimate grounds in different worlds.

Thus:

[
Ultimate(U,w)
]

does not by itself entail:

[
square_D U.
]

A trans-world identity principle is still needed.

**Result:** ultimacy alone does not prove EGI.

## 6. Does necessary G defeat substitution?

G's invariant constitutive role constrains every adequate ground to the same functional profile.

But:

same necessary explanandum
-/->
same numerical explainer

without an additional identity principle.

**Result:** necessary G narrows the ground class but does not by itself prove one U.

## 7. Differentiation analysis

If U1 and U2 are distinct, some basis of distinction is required at least at the level of identity:

[
U_1
eq U_2.
]

Possible bases:

### Intrinsic difference
U1 has property F and U2 lacks F.

If F affects adequacy, then the grounds may not actually instantiate the same full ground profile.

If F is irrelevant to adequacy, numerical plurality remains possible without affecting G.

### World-index difference
U1 exists in w1; U2 exists in w2.

This can distinguish realizers without an intrinsic difference available inside one world.

### Primitive numerical difference
Distinctness is primitive.

This cannot be rejected without adopting a stronger identity principle.

Therefore differentiation does not yet force uniqueness.

**Result:** CUB strengthens adequacy but not EGI directly.
