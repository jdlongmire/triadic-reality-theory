# The L-I-A Storyline: Logic, Information, and Action

Here’s the storyline.

“Ontological” just means “about what is real,” as opposed to what we think about it. An ontological constraint is therefore a constraint reality itself has to obey. It is not merely a rule we made up. It is a condition on what can actually be.

Start with logic. A coin-face cannot be both heads and not-heads at the same time and in the same respect. That is the law of non-contradiction in ordinary language. If contradiction were allowed all the way down, distinctions would collapse. “Heads” and “not-heads” could both obtain in the same respect, and determinate description would disappear.

Think of the laws of logic as something like the physics engine of existence. A game designer can build many different worlds, but anything rendered as a determinate world still has to satisfy the engine’s constitutive constraints. Logic is not literally software or code. The analogy captures the constraint: reality does not get to opt out of the conditions required for anything to be determinately what it is.

Then there is information. There is a difference between something being sayable, something specifying a coherent pattern, and that pattern actually being realizable. I can utter the words “square circle,” but the phrase does not specify a coherent object that any amount of power could build. A genuine specification has determinate content, and realization adds further conditions.

Sheet music makes the distinction easier to see. The score represents a pattern. A performable score is one whose specification can actually be realized under the relevant constraints. The music sounding in the room is the performance itself. Score, performability, and performance are related, but they are not the same thing. A score can remain forever unperformed.

That brings us to action. Action accounts for actuality. A specification does not make itself obtain merely by being specified. Logic can constrain a pattern, and information can distinguish what the pattern is, while neither fact alone entails that the pattern is actual. In the musical analogy, the written score does not cause the concert simply by existing. Performance introduces actuality.

Put the three together:

**Logic constrains what can be. Information specifies what it is. Action accounts for its actuality.**

Or, in the musical shorthand:

**Score → performable score → performance**

There is an important direction to the reasoning. Start with what is actual. Anything actual must satisfy the conditions required for it to be realizable, coherent, and sufficiently specified. The reverse does not automatically follow. Something can be described without being coherent. Something coherent can remain unrealized. Something realizable can remain unactualized. Conceivability alone does not manufacture reality.

Christian theology then asks a deeper grounding question. It does not identify this threefold structure with God. The Logos is the uncreated Son, not Logic, Information, Action, an operator, or some component inside the model. Scripture identifies the Logos as the one through whom created reality exists and in whom it holds together. On that account, the logical, informational, and actual character of creation is grounded in Him.

The model describes features of created reality. The Logos is not contained by the model.
