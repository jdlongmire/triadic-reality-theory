# Rational Permissibility Test

**WP:** WP-TRT-LRT-ONTO-0001  
**Date:** 2026-09-19  
**Track:** convergence / mind-ground model  
**Target:** perfect rationality and non-necessitating selection  
**Status:** first-pass analysis complete

## Question

Can a perfectly rational agent face two or more alternatives such that each is fully rationally permissible and no rational defect follows from selecting either?

If yes, necessary perfect rationality need not uniquely determine derivative physical actuality.

## 1. Rationality is not identical to optimization

Define minimal perfect rationality:

PR(M): M never violates logic, acts on false apprehension, ignores a decisive reason, or selects an option that is irrational relative to the complete relevant reasons.

This does not yet entail:

OPT(M): for every choice set there exists exactly one maximally rational option and M necessarily selects it.

OPT is a stronger principle and must not be smuggled into PR.

Therefore:

PR -/-> unique selection

unless uniqueness follows from the reason structure of the specific choice.

## 2. Equal-permissibility case

Let p and q satisfy:

Admissible(p).
Admissible(q).
not CoActualizable_phys(p,q).

Suppose complete reasons R yield:

Permissible_R(p).
Permissible_R(q).

and neither:

R -> ought p over q

nor:

R -> ought q over p.

Then selecting p violates no rational requirement; selecting q violates no rational requirement.

Therefore multiple rationally permissible alternatives are conceptually coherent unless one adds:

**URP, Unique Rational Resolution Principle:**
For every complete rational choice situation, exactly one option is uniquely rationally required.

URP is not contained in logic alone.

## 3. Simple demonstration

Consider a perfectly rational agent selecting between two alternatives identical with respect to every reason-relevant feature, while the alternatives remain numerically distinct.

If numerical distinctness alone creates no rational superiority, then:

Reasons(p) = Reasons(q).

A requirement to select p rather than q would need an additional differentiating reason.

Absent that reason, either selection can be rationally permissible.

Potential objection: Leibnizian identity of indiscernibles may collapse p and q if all properties are identical.

Reply: the demonstration need not rely on absolutely indiscernible entities. It is sufficient that p and q differ in properties irrelevant to the relevant rational end while being equally satisfactory with respect to all decisive reasons.

## 4. Incommensurable or satisficing cases

Multiple rational permissions can also arise if:
- alternatives instantiate different goods that are not totally ordered;
- several options meet every rational requirement;
- the end underdetermines the means;
- reasons constrain a permissible set rather than rank every member.

Thus rationality can function as constraint:

R -> {p,q,r}_permitted

without functioning as a total choice algorithm:

R -> exactly p.

This structure closely resembles TRT's own distinction between admissibility and actuality.

## 5. Important analogy to TRT

L constrains I into an admissible set:

I --L--> D.

L does not itself select which admissible state becomes actual.

Likewise rationality may constrain an agent's alternatives:

Options --Reasons/Rationality--> PermissibleSet.

Rationality need not itself select one member.

Then volitional selection S can operate within the rationally permissible domain:

S(PermissibleSet) -> selected p.

This is structurally analogous to:

A(D) -> actual state.

The analogy does not prove will, but it shows that rational constraint and selection are conceptually separable without irrationality.

## 6. Perfect rationality and AOD

If PR constrains a set of permissible alternatives but does not uniquely necessitate one, then AOD becomes coherent in form:

1. M∞ perfectly apprehends all relevant alternatives and reasons.
2. Rationality excludes impermissible alternatives.
3. More than one alternative remains fully permissible.
4. M∞ is source of selection among the permissible alternatives.
5. Selection is intentional and reason-compatible, though not uniquely reason-determined.

This avoids:
- irrationality;
- external selector;
- brute event with no subject/reasons;
- unique necessitation.

It does not yet prove AOD is metaphysically instantiated.

## 7. "Why p rather than q?" objection

The demand for a uniquely differentiating sufficient reason assumes:

**UDR:** every rational selection must have a reason sufficient to distinguish the selected option from every unselected permissible option.

UDR is stronger than:

**RR:** rational selection must be intelligible and compatible with sufficient reasons for acting.

Under RR, the agent can have sufficient reason to choose from a permissible set without a reason that uniquely necessitates one member.

Thus:

Reason to choose p

need not mean:

Reason necessitating p over every q.

Whether UDR is true is a substantive PSR/agency thesis, not a law of logic.

## 8. Does this make selection arbitrary?

Distinguish:

**Arbitrary_1:** contrary to reasons / reasonless.

**Arbitrary_2:** not uniquely determined by comparative reasons.

AOD selection among equally permissible alternatives may be non-arbitrary_1 while arbitrary_2 in the technical sense.

Calling it "irrational" would conflate the two.

The agent's sourcehood can supply determination where reasons supply permissibility.

## 9. Perfect goodness complication

If later perfect goodness is established, some choice sets may contain a uniquely morally best option.

Then perfect goodness may necessitate exclusion of inferior options.

But it does not follow that every possible world-selection has a unique morally best member.

A future axiological track must test:
- whether there is a unique best possible world;
- whether no best world exists;
- whether many worlds are equally/permissibly good;
- whether creation itself is morally optional.

No conclusion is imported here.

## 10. Modal result

If multiple perfectly rational selections are possible:

Box PR(M∞)

does not entail:

exists p Box S(M∞,p).

Therefore:

Box M∞ + Box PR(M∞)

is compatible with:

Diamond S(p) AND Diamond S(q)

for mutually exclusive derivative instantiations.

This blocks the specific modal-collapse route from necessary rationality to necessary physical actuality.

It does not by itself prove libertarian freedom or contingency of every act.

## 11. Result for will

The route now becomes:

perfect apprehension
-> rational constraint of alternatives
-> nonempty permissible set
-> source-attributable selection S
-> Will_min.

If the permissible set has cardinality > 1, will has genuine underdetermined selection space without defect.

This is a stronger conceptual model of volition than simply labeling S "will."

## 12. Countermodels and objections

### O1. URP
Perfect rationality always uniquely determines the best option.

Response: requires proof of total ordering + unique maximum for every choice set.

### O2. Strong PSR/UDR
Every actual selection requires a uniquely differentiating sufficient reason.

Response: substantive metaphysical principle; not logic. If adopted, AOD is threatened and modal collapse must be revisited.

### O3. Chance
If reasons do not determine selection, outcome is random.

Response: false dichotomy unless agent-sourcehood is incoherent. AOD is precisely the third candidate and requires separate evaluation.

### O4. Timelessness
Selection among alternatives implies temporal deliberation/change.

Response: not necessarily. Explanatory distinction among alternatives and selection need not imply temporal sequence. A timeless volitional act is conceptually distinct from temporal deliberation, though its coherence requires later analysis.
