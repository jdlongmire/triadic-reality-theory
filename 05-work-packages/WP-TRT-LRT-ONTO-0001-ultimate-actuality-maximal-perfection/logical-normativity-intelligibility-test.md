# Logical Normativity and Intelligibility Test

**WP:** WP-TRT-LRT-ONTO-0001  
**Date:** 2026-09-19  
**Track:** convergence / H_S vs H_M∞  
**Target:** L-thin -> L-thick?  
**Status:** first-pass conceptual analysis

## Question

Is ultimate L merely descriptive constraint on what can obtain, or does logical consequence contain irreducibly normative/intelligible structure?

If logical normativity is fundamental, can an impersonal structural ground account for it without adding independent normative primitives?

## 1. Separate three senses of L

### L_constitutive
Identity/noncontradiction-type conditions constitutive of determinate actuality.

### L_consequence
Objective consequence relations:

Gamma |= p

or, proof-theoretically, p follows from premises/rules.

### L_normative
Standards governing rational inference:

If an agent accepts premises Gamma and recognizes that p follows, there is a rational norm against affirming Gamma while rejecting p without defeating reason.

These must not be collapsed.

TRT's hard core most clearly commits to L_constitutive. LRT may develop stronger consequence structure. L_normative requires separate warrant.

## 2. Descriptive consequence is not yet normative obligation

From:

Gamma entails p

it does not trivially follow:

Agent M ought to believe p.

Norms of belief may depend on:
- whether M believes Gamma;
- whether M recognizes the entailment;
- epistemic conditions;
- defeaters;
- resource limitations.

Therefore:

L_consequence -/-> full doxastic obligation.

This blocks an easy move from logic to mind.

## 3. But consequence is not physical regularity

Logical consequence differs categorically from:

"systems of type X usually transition to Y."

If p follows validly from Gamma, a physical system failing to instantiate p does not make the inference invalid. Conversely, repeated physical transition does not establish logical validity.

Thus logical consequence is not reducible merely to observed physical regularity.

Under TRT this is expected because L is ontologically prior to derivative physical systems.

## 4. Correctness norm

Even if full doxastic "ought" is downstream, logic contains a thinner normative/correctness dimension:

An inference either preserves validity/correctness under the relevant logical relation or it does not.

Call:

Correct_L(Gamma,p).

This is not moral normativity. It is a standard of valid consequence.

Question:

Is Correct_L merely another descriptive structural property, or does "correct" add irreducible normativity?

A structuralist can answer:

Correct_L(Gamma,p) iff p stands in the designated consequence relation to Gamma.

Then correctness reduces to structure.

This is coherent if the consequence relation itself is primitive/defined without appeal to rational agents.

## 5. Plural logics pressure

Different formal systems support different consequence relations.

Therefore one cannot infer from "there is a formal consequence relation" to "there is one uniquely normative logic" without additional argument.

TRT's L3 claim is stronger: certain logical constraints are constitutive of actuality rather than optional calculi.

This suggests two levels:
- formal-logical plurality in representable systems;
- ontological logical constraint on actualizable reality.

The normativity question concerns primarily the latter.

## 6. Ontological normativity

If L_constitutive determines what is admissible for actuality:

Violation(L) -> not Actualizable.

This is stronger than human inference norms. L does not merely advise; it constrains ontological admissibility.

Call this **modal/ontological normativity** only with caution. "Normativity" may mislead because no agent is commanded.

A cleaner term may be:

**necessitating constraint** or **constitutive constraint**.

Thus the pure TRT result may remain non-agentive:

L constitutes admissibility.

No mind follows from this alone.

## 7. Intelligibility

A different question is whether L is intrinsically intelligible/rational rather than merely formal.

Logical relations are relations of reasons among propositions when apprehended by a rational subject.

But the relation may exist whether or not any finite subject apprehends it.

Two models:

### LS
Logical relations are primitive objective structures; minds discover/apprehend them.

### LM
Logical relations are intrinsic to ultimate rational Mind; finite minds participate in/apprehend that rational order.

Both explain objectivity differently.

The existence of objective consequence alone does not decide between them.

## 8. Explanatory burden for LS

LS owes:
- why logical structure exists necessarily;
- why it constrains actuality;
- how logical truths/consequence relate to informational possibilities;
- why finite rational minds can apprehend it.

But those are explanatory questions, not contradictions.

Calling logic "brute" invokes BBR and gains no explanatory credit. Calling it emergent violates EPR if emergence presupposes L.

Therefore LS's strongest position is that logical structure is primitive/necessary by its own nature.

That claim must be compared with LM, not dismissed.

## 9. Explanatory burden for LM

LM owes:
- why Mind is independently warranted;
- why rational Mind entails the relevant logical structure rather than merely conforms to it;
- whether logical truths depend on Mind in a way that avoids arbitrariness;
- whether Mind could think contradictions true;
- how necessity of logic relates to necessity of Mind.

A viable LM should say logicality is intrinsic to Mind's nature, not selected by arbitrary will.

Then:

Box M∞ -> Box L

would be a candidate essence relation, not a decree model.

This is not yet derived.

## 10. Euthyphro-style logic dilemma

Is logic:
1. valid because Mind wills it; or
2. recognized by Mind because it is independently valid?

The unified-nature model proposes a third form:

3. logical rationality is intrinsic to the necessary nature of Mind.

Then neither arbitrary volition nor an external law is prior.

But this only has explanatory value if "necessary rational Mind" is independently warranted and not defined as "the thing that has necessary logic."

## 11. Does L-thick get us to Mind?

Not deductively.

Even objective, necessary consequence relations can be modeled Platonically/structurally.

Thus:

L_objective + L_necessary -/-> Mind.

However, if combined with:
- intrinsic semantic content I_thick;
- reason-responsive action A;
- unity C;

the Mind model may unify features that structuralism otherwise treats separately.

This reinforces the Triadic Thickening Hypothesis rather than producing a standalone logical argument.

## 12. Key result

The strongest warranted characterization of fundamental L is presently:

**L is constitutive, objective, and non-emergent constraint on actualizability.**

Whether it is intrinsically normative in a subject-involving sense is not established.

This is enough to rule out:
- physical emergence of L;
- reduction to observed regularity.

It is not enough to derive Mind.

## 13. H_S vs H_M∞ update

H_S:
- can coherently treat L as primitive necessary constitutive structure;
- owes its unity with I/A and its necessity;
- receives no explanatory credit from brute/emergence labels.

H_M∞:
- can model L as intrinsic rational nature of one Mind;
- potentially unifies logicality with content and act;
- owes independent warrant for Mind and the essence relation Mind -> L.

No winner from L alone.

## 14. Disposition

- L_constitutive: **HIGH within TRT**.
- L_consequence objective: **HIGH as logical category; exact formalization varies**.
- L_consequence -> doxastic ought: **NOT DIRECTLY**.
- Logic reducible to physical regularity: **NO within TRT dependency order**.
- Fundamental L intrinsically subject-normative: **NOT ESTABLISHED**.
- Primitive necessary structural L coherent: **YES as live H_S model**.
- Intrinsic rational-Mind L coherent: **YES as live H_M∞ model**.
- L alone -> Mind: **NO**.
- Joint thick L/I/A/C remains the stronger discriminator: **YES**.
