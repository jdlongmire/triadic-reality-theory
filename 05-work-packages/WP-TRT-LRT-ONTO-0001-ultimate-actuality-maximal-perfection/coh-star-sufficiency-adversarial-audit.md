# Coh* Sufficiency Adversarial Audit

**WP:** WP-TRT-LRT-ONTO-0001
**Date:** 2026-09-23
**Status:** adversarial research v0.1
**Target:** D(x) ?<=> Rep(x) AND Coh*(x)

## 1. Burden

The candidate thesis is:

D(x) iff Rep(x) AND Coh*(x),

where Coh*(x) means that the sufficiently complete informational specification of x contains no contradiction under the governing logical constraints.

A counterexample must satisfy all three:

1. x is determinately specified;
2. x is contradiction-free under every constitutive relation included in that specification;
3. x is nevertheless metaphysically unactualizable for a reason not reducible to omitted information or an external condition set Y.

If no such counterexample survives, a separate intrinsic M(x) remains unsupported.

## 2. Essentialist objections

Claim: some descriptions are logically consistent yet metaphysically impossible because they violate an object's essence.

Test: if the essential property is genuinely constitutive of x, then the complete representation of x must contain it. Adding its negation produces incoherence in the completed specification.

If the alleged essence is not contained in or derivable from the specification, the critic owes an independent account of why it is constitutive.

Result: essence does not yet establish M. It either enriches I_x or introduces a separately argued ontological thesis.

**Disposition:** NO COUNTEREXAMPLE YET.

## 3. Necessary a posteriori cases

Example class: "water is not H2O" under Kripkean identity claims.

If "water" rigidly designates the substance H2O, then a complete specification containing water and non-H2O violates an identity relation. If "watery stuff" is intended instead, the description may be coherent but concerns a different substance.

The modal force appears to depend on identity/reference facts that belong in the complete specification.

**Disposition:** NO COUNTEREXAMPLE YET. This case pressures the adequacy of I_x, not Coh*.

## 4. Mathematical independence and undecidability

A proposition may be undecidable from a chosen formal system without being ontologically indeterminate.

Failure of proof is epistemic/formal-system relative:

not Provable_T(p) AND not Provable_T(not-p)

does not entail:

not Coh*(p).

Coh* must therefore not be defined as "provable consistent by us" or by any fixed incomplete calculus.

**Disposition:** NO METAPHYSICAL COUNTEREXAMPLE. Important epistemic limitation.

## 5. Causal and dispositional modality

Objection: a state may be coherent yet lack any causal pathway by which it could occur.

Response: this establishes non-reachability from a specified initial condition or causal regime. Encode that regime as Y:

Real(x | Y).

Failure under Y does not establish intrinsic nonactualizability of x.

To turn causal non-reachability into unrestricted impossibility requires a necessary causal principle, which must be independently defended.

**Disposition:** RELATIONAL, NOT INTRINSIC.

## 6. Laws of nature

A state violating the actual laws of physics may be physically impossible in our world.

Represent this as:

not Compat*(x, Y_actual-laws).

Unless the laws themselves are metaphysically necessary, this does not establish:

not D(x).

**Disposition:** WORLD-RELATIVE.

## 7. Aristotelian potency

Potentiality may be invoked to say that only states grounded in real potencies are possible.

But "real potency" cannot simply mean "capacity for actualization" without restating D in different vocabulary.

If potency is grounded in the nature of a bearer, then bearer-nature and its capacities enter Y or the complete specification.

**Disposition:** DOES NOT YET PRODUCE INDEPENDENT M.

## 8. Thomistic act/potency

A Thomistic objection may distinguish logical possibility from real possibility, requiring potency in an existing or creatable essence.

This is a substantive metaphysical theory. It cannot be imported into the pure TRT track without argument.

Further, for creatio ex nihilo, creaturely potency cannot be required as an antecedent substrate in God. The relevant question becomes whether the essence is noncontradictorily creatable, which returns pressure to complete specification plus divine power.

**Disposition:** SERIOUS EXTERNAL RIVAL ACCOUNT; NO INTERNAL DEFEATER YET.

## 9. Impossible-world semantics

Impossible-world frameworks deliberately permit representations at which contradictions or nonclassical relations obtain.

This reinforces R != D. It does not show that a fully coherent representation is unactualizable.

**Disposition:** SUPPORTS REPRESENTABILITY DISTINCTION; NO COUNTEREXAMPLE.

## 10. Counterfactuals of creaturely freedom

Let Y fully specify a creature and circumstances, with alternative actions a and b each individually coherent:

Coh*(Y AND a)
and
Coh*(Y AND b).

This may establish:

CAN(a | Y)
and
CAN(b | Y).

It does not establish either:

Y WOULD-> a

or:

Y WOULD-> b.

Therefore Coh* may suffice for CAN while remaining insufficient for WOULD.

This is not a counterexample to D. It is evidence that counterfactual truth is a distinct relation.

**Disposition:** CAN/WOULD DISTINCTION CONFIRMED.

## 11. Divine nature and impossible divine acts

Statements such as "God lies" or "God ceases to be God" can look grammatically representable and superficially noncontradictory.

Once the relevant divine attributes are treated as essential, the complete specification contains the relations generating the contradiction.

This case therefore turns on whether the essential attributes have been independently established.

**Disposition:** SPECIFICATION-DEPENDENT.

## 12. Haecceity and transworld identity

Could x be fully qualitatively specified yet fail to be the numerically same individual across a modal comparison?

This exposes a possible limitation in informational individuation. Coh* needs a typed treatment of identity conditions and reference, not merely predicate consistency.

It does not yet demonstrate an unactualizable coherent state, but it warns that "complete specification" cannot be reduced to a finite list of qualitative predicates.

**Disposition:** OPEN FORMALIZATION ISSUE.

## 13. Self-reference and semantic paradox

Liar-style or Russell-style constructions can appear locally grammatical while producing contradiction only after semantic closure.

A genuine Coh* test must include dependency closure over the relations constitutive of the representation.

Therefore:

Coh*(x) != surface syntactic consistency.

**Disposition:** STRENGTHENS CLOSURE REQUIREMENT.

## 14. Infinite specifications

Some candidates may require infinitely many relations to determine their structure.

"Complete specification" therefore cannot mean an explicitly enumerated finite description. It must mean that all truth-relevant constitutive constraints are fixed, whether finitely expressible or not.

This raises an epistemic issue for finite reasoners but does not by itself create an ontological M.

**Disposition:** EPISTEMIC LIMITATION, NOT DEFEATER.

## 15. The strongest surviving objection

The strongest challenge is not a known counterexample. It is the possibility that there are **primitive modal facts**:

Coh*(x) AND not D(x),

where nothing about x's informational specification explains the failure of actualizability.

But asserting such a fact merely posits M unless an example or independent modal ontology is supplied.

Thus the burden is symmetrical:

- TRT/LRT must not define coherence into possibility by fiat.
- The critic must not define primitive impossibility into existence by fiat.

**Disposition:** OPEN METAPHYSICAL RIVAL, not evidence against Coh* sufficiency.

## 16. Refinement: closure rather than omniscient enumeration

Replace "complete specification" with **constraint closure**.

Let Spec(x) be the informational specification of x.

Let Cl_L(Spec(x)) be the closure of that specification under all constitutive identity, definitional, mathematical, relational, and logical consequences relevant to x.

Define:

Coh*(x) iff bottom is not in Cl_L(Spec(x)).

This avoids requiring a finite agent to enumerate every proposition. Coh* is an ontological/semantic property of the specification under closure, while our ability to determine Coh* remains epistemically limited.

For conditional compatibility:

Coh*(x,Y) iff bottom is not in Cl_L(Spec(x) union Spec(Y)).

This is cleaner than invoking an unexplained M.

## 17. Revised candidate theorem

**Constraint-Closure Actualizability Conjecture (CCAC):**

For any determinately representable state x:

D(x) iff bottom is not in Cl_L(Spec(x)).

Conditional form:

Real(x | Y) iff bottom is not in Cl_L(Spec(x) union Spec(Y)).

The right-to-left directions remain conjectural.

The left-to-right directions are strongly supported by DAT and the canonical category backbone.

## 18. Falsification condition

CCAC is falsified by a case x for which:

1. Spec(x) is determinate;
2. its full constitutive closure is contradiction-free;
3. no omitted Y explains the failure;
4. x is independently demonstrable as metaphysically unactualizable.

No such case has been established in this audit.

## 19. Result

The adversarial pass did not identify a successful intrinsic M(x).

The main improvements are:

- replace vague "complete specification" with constraint closure;
- distinguish ontological coherence from our ability to prove coherence;
- keep physical, causal, and law-relative impossibility indexed to Y;
- keep CAN distinct from WOULD;
- treat primitive modal facts as a rival hypothesis rather than an assumed defeater.

## 20. Epistemic disposition

R/D/X distinction: HIGH.

D(x) -> Coh*(x): MEDIUM-HIGH within TRT/DAT.

Coh*(x) -> D(x): MEDIUM as research conjecture, not theorem.

Conditional Coh*(x,Y) -> Real(x|Y): MEDIUM as research conjecture.

CAN != WOULD: HIGH as category distinction.

No independent M found: HIGH as a result of this audit, limited to the candidate classes tested.

"Every fully specified, contradiction-free state is metaphysically possible."

The literature search must distinguish logical possibility, broadly logical possibility, metaphysical possibility, essence-based modality, and nomological possibility rather than treating them as synonyms.
