# Literature Audit: Constraint Closure and Metaphysical Possibility

**WP:** WP-TRT-LRT-ONTO-0001
**Date:** 2026-09-23
**Status:** literature-grounded adversarial note v0.1
**Target:** Constraint-Closure Actualizability Conjecture (CCAC)

## Research question

Can the literature supply a clear case in which a determinately specified state is contradiction-free under all constitutive constraints yet is metaphysically impossible for some further reason?

Candidate CCAC:

D(x) ?<=> bottom not-in Cl_L(Spec(x)).

## 1. Modal taxonomy

Contemporary literature commonly distinguishes logical, metaphysical, and physical/nomological possibility. A common ordering treats physical possibilities as a subset of metaphysical possibilities and metaphysical possibilities as a subset of logical possibilities, while acknowledging that the characterization of metaphysical possibility is controversial.

This immediately warns against identifying thin formal consistency with metaphysical actualizability.

CCAC therefore cannot use "logical consistency" in the narrow proof-theoretic sense. Its only plausible form uses constitutive constraint closure over the informational specification.

## 2. Fine's challenge

Kit Fine's "Essence and Modality" argues against reducing essence to metaphysical necessity and proposes the reverse explanatory direction: metaphysical necessities flow from the natures or essences of things.

For CCAC this is potentially important.

If essence is additional modal structure external to Spec(x), then Finean essentialism threatens CCAC.

If the essence/nature of x is constitutive information about what x is, then it belongs in Spec(x), and closure over that information can capture the Finean constraint.

Thus Fine does not straightforwardly provide a counterexample to CCAC. He does provide a major warning: Spec(x) must be hyperintensional enough to encode constitutive nature, not merely a set of extensionally equivalent propositions.

## 3. Correia and essence-source accounts

Fabrice Correia develops the Finean programme by treating metaphysical necessity as grounded in essence, while also proposing essence-based treatments of conceptual and logical necessity.

This strengthens the pressure on any account that treats L3 as the whole content of modality.

For TRT/LRT the natural response is not to add "essence" as an unexplained fourth primitive. It is to ask whether essence is informationally expressible as constitutive constraint.

Research target:

Essence(x) -> ConstitutiveInfo(Spec(x)) ?

If yes, Finean constraints may be absorbed by I + L through closure.

If no, CCAC requires revision.

## 4. Mackie's objection

Penelope Mackie argues that the Finean reduction faces difficulty because the required notion of real definition may not itself be isolable without appeal to metaphysical modality.

This is highly relevant to the no-free-lunch rule.

If "constitutive information" is defined using "what x must be," CCAC has merely hidden modal vocabulary inside Spec(x).

Therefore CCAC needs a non-modal account of constitutive specification, or must explicitly acknowledge a primitive modal component.

## 5. Bovey's circularity challenge

Gaétan Bovey argues that the thesis that essence is the source of all metaphysical necessity faces a circularity problem concerning the necessity of essences themselves.

This reinforces the same methodological point: replacing primitive modality with primitive essence does not automatically discharge the explanatory burden.

TRT/LRT should therefore resist both moves:

1. primitive M(x) labeled "metaphysical possibility";
2. primitive Essence(x) that performs the same modal work without independent grounding.

## 6. Kripkean necessary a posteriori cases

Kripkean examples such as water/H2O are often used to distinguish metaphysical from merely epistemic or superficially conceptual possibility.

These cases do not yet defeat CCAC if identity/reference facts are included in Spec(x). "Water is non-H2O" then fails closure because the rigidly designated substance identity is part of the constitutive information.

The cost is important: Spec(x) must include identity conditions that may be discovered empirically. Constraint closure is ontological and need not coincide with what a finite reasoner can know a priori.

Therefore:

epistemically conceivable != Coh*(x).

## 7. Literature result

The literature surveyed supplies strong reasons to reject:

thin logical consistency = metaphysical possibility.

It does not yet supply a decisive counterexample to the stronger CCAC formulation:

full constitutive informational closure without contradiction = actualizability.

The central unresolved issue is whether "full constitutive informational closure" can be specified without importing primitive metaphysical modality.

That is now the critical burden.

## 8. Refined CCAC

Replace the earlier formulation with:

**CCAC-2**

For a determinately represented x, let Spec_c(x) contain all constitutive information fixing what x is, while excluding merely accidental world conditions.

Let Cl_L be consequence/constraint closure under the logical relations governing that information.

Then:

D(x) ?<=> bottom not-in Cl_L(Spec_c(x)).

Conditional form:

Real(x | Y) ?<=> bottom not-in Cl_L(Spec_c(x) union Spec(Y)).

The right-to-left directions remain conjectural.

## 9. Anti-circularity condition

CCAC-2 is admissible only if Spec_c can be characterized without:

- "properties x necessarily has";
- "properties x has in every possible world";
- "properties x could not lack";
- "properties required for x to be metaphysically possible."

Those definitions presuppose the modal relation CCAC is intended to explain.

Candidate non-modal vocabulary for further work:

- identity conditions;
- real definition;
- constitutive relations;
- structural determination;
- informational invariants;
- dependency relations.

Each requires independent scrutiny.

## 10. Current verdict

No literature-grade falsifier of CCAC-2 has yet been identified.

However, the literature does defeat any naive version in which Coh* means ordinary formal consistency.

The viable hypothesis is narrower and stronger:

> Metaphysical actualizability may correspond to contradiction-free closure of the complete constitutive informational specification, provided constitutive information itself can be characterized without circular modal primitives.

This is not yet a theorem.

## 11. Epistemic status

HIGH: logical, metaphysical, and nomological modalities must be distinguished.

HIGH: ordinary formal consistency is insufficient as a literature-grounded account of metaphysical possibility.

HIGH: Finean essentialism is a serious rival/source theory of metaphysical modality.

MEDIUM: Finean essence may be representable within TRT/LRT as constitutive information.

UNCERTAIN: constitutive information can be characterized non-modally with enough strength to support CCAC-2.

UNCERTAIN: CCAC-2 sufficiency.

## 12. Next work

1. Formalize Spec_c using non-modal identity/constitutive vocabulary.
2. Test whether Finean real definition can be translated into informational constraint without loss.
3. Construct explicit Kripkean identity cases and verify closure behavior.
4. Search for alleged metaphysical impossibilities that remain consistent even after essence/identity information is included.
5. Keep the result in the protective/research layer until a non-circular Spec_c definition survives adversarial review.
