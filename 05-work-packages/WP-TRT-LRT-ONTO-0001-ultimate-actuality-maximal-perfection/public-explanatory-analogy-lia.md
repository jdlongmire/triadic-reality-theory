# Public Explanatory Analogy: Logic, Information, and Action

**WP:** WP-TRT-LRT-ONTO-0001  
**Date:** 2026-09-28  
**Status:** canonical explanatory note v0.1  
**Type:** pedagogical exposition; non-proof artifact

## Accessible narrative

Here’s the storyline. “Ontological” just means “about what is real,” as opposed to what we think about it. So an ontological constraint is a rule reality itself has to obey. It is not merely a rule we made up. It is a constraint on what can actually be.

Start with logic. A coin-face cannot be both heads and not-heads at the same time and in the same respect. That is the law of non-contradiction in ordinary language. If contradiction were allowed all the way down, distinctions would collapse. “Heads” and “not-heads” could both obtain in the same respect, and determinate description would disappear.

Think of the laws of logic as something like the physics engine of existence. A game designer can build many different worlds, but anything rendered as a determinate world still has to satisfy the engine’s constitutive constraints. Logic is not literally software or code. The analogy captures the constraint: reality does not get to opt out of the conditions required for anything to be determinately what it is.

Then there is information. There is a difference between something being sayable, something specifying a coherent pattern, and that pattern actually being realizable. I can utter the words “square circle,” but the phrase does not specify a coherent object that any amount of power could build. A genuine specification has determinate content, and realization adds further conditions.

Sheet music makes the distinction easier to see. The score represents a pattern. A performable score is one whose specification can actually be realized under the relevant constraints. The music sounding in the room is the performance itself. Score, performability, and performance are related, but they are not the same thing. A score can remain forever unperformed.

That brings us to action. Action accounts for actuality. A specification does not make itself obtain merely by being specified. Logic can constrain a pattern, and information can distinguish what the pattern is, while neither fact alone entails that the pattern is actual. In the musical analogy, the written score does not cause the concert simply by existing. Performance introduces actuality.

Put the three together and the intuition becomes straightforward: **Logic constrains what can coherently be. Information specifies differentiated content. Action accounts for actuality rather than mere possibility.**

Or, in the musical shorthand:

**Score → performable score → performance**

Christian theology then asks a deeper grounding question. It does not identify this threefold structure with God. The Logos is the uncreated Son, not Logic, Information, Action, an operator, or some component inside the model. Scripture identifies the Logos as the one through whom created reality exists and in whom it holds together. On that account, the logical, informational, and actual character of creation is grounded in Him. The model describes features of created reality. The Logos is not contained by the model.

## Purpose

Provide an accessible explanation of the Logic-Information-Action framework without weakening the category distinctions established in the formal TRT/LRT corpus.

This note is explanatory. The analogies do not constitute proofs and must not be used to strengthen claims beyond the formal artifacts.

## 1. Logic: the engine analogy

An ontological constraint is a constraint on what can obtain in reality, rather than merely a rule for how an observer should think.

The law of non-contradiction is the simplest entry point. Under the classical qualification, the same subject cannot both possess and lack the same predicate at the same time and in the same respect.

A useful analogy is a game's physics engine. A designer may specify many different worlds, but anything rendered as a determinate state must satisfy the constitutive constraints of the engine.

The analogy is limited. Logic is not literally software, code, a physical mechanism, or a contingent rule set. The point is the constraint relation.

**Public shorthand:** Logic constrains what can coherently be.

## 2. Information: score and performability

Information supplies differentiated content: the determinate "what" under consideration.

A critical distinction must be preserved between linguistic expressibility and coherent representability. One can utter the words "square circle," but this does not thereby establish a coherent state capable of realization. In the formal corpus, representability is deliberately weaker than coherence.

The musical analogy gives a useful staged model:

1. **Score:** specified or represented content.
2. **Performable score:** content that satisfies the relevant logical and realization constraints.
3. **Performance:** the content actually instantiated through action.

A score may contain marks that cannot coherently be performed. A coherent score may remain unperformed. Performance introduces actuality.

This maps pedagogically onto the more precise corpus distinctions:

**Rep(x) → Coh(x) → Real(x | Y) → X(x)**

only as a sequence of questions, not as unconditional forward implications. The formally warranted direction remains from actuality back through its necessary conditions:

**X(x) → Real(x | Y_actual) → Compat(x, Y_actual) → Coh(x) → Rep(x)**

**Public shorthand:** Information specifies differentiated content and makes the question of realization determinate.

## 3. Action: from possibility to actuality

Action supplies the act/actuality role. It must not be reduced in introductory exposition to physical motion or temporal change.

A photograph-versus-film analogy can help with dynamic cases, but it is incomplete because TRT's primitive Action includes actuality more broadly. A thought can be actual as a mental act without producing physical motion.

The stronger TRT point is:

**specification ⇏ obtaining**

Logic plus Information can characterize admissible or differentiated content. They do not, merely by doing so, entail that the content is actual.

**Public shorthand:** Action accounts for actuality rather than mere specification or possibility.

## 4. Combined public formulation

The most compact pedagogical formulation is:

> Logic constrains what can coherently be. Information specifies differentiated content and the conditions under which realization can be considered. Action accounts for actuality rather than mere possibility.

Or, using the musical analogy:

> Score → performable score → performance.

This is pedagogical compression. The formal model retains the distinctions among representability, coherence, conditional realizability, and actualization.

## 5. Theological boundary: the Logos

The L-I-A triad must never be identified with the Logos.

The Logos is the uncreated Son of God. He is not Logic, Information, or Action treated as an abstract primitive; not an operator; not a vector, state, field, law, or member of a mathematical ontology; and not contained within the created explanatory framework.

Within the Christian theological extension, the claim is one of grounding and dependence: created reality is logically ordered, informationally differentiated, and actual because creation exists through the Logos.

A suitable public landing is:

> Logic constrains what can coherently be. Information specifies what can be represented and, where coherent under the relevant conditions, what can be realized. Action accounts for actuality rather than mere possibility. Together these describe something striking about created reality: it is logically ordered, informationally structured, and dynamically actual.
>
> Christianity goes one step deeper. It does not identify that structure with God. Scripture identifies the ultimate ground of created intelligibility and actuality with the uncreated Logos, the Son through whom all things were made and in whom all things hold together. The Logos is therefore not one more element inside the L-I-A structure. The structure exists because creation exists through Him.

## 6. Guardrails

Do not use the public analogy to assert any of the following without the relevant independent argument:

- that coherence is sufficient for metaphysical realizability;
- that representability entails coherence;
- that specification entails actuality;
- that every instance of Action is temporal change;
- that the L-I-A triad itself is God or the Logos;
- that the analogy proves the personal-ground or Christian-identification bridge;
- that "square circle" names a coherent possible state merely because the phrase is linguistically expressible.
