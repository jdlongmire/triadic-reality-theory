# Intentionality and Reasons-as-Reasons Audit

**WP:** WP-TRT-LRT-ONTO-0001  
**Date:** 2026-09-19  
**Track:** convergence / H_S vs H_M∞  
**Target:** can intentional aboutness and reasons-as-reasons be purely impersonal structure?  
**Status:** first-pass conceptual audit

## Question

Can intrinsic intentionality and reasons-as-reasons be fully represented as impersonal structural relations, or does their character require a subject?

This is a same-level ontological comparison. Derivative computers, organisms, brains, or physical systems may illuminate implementations but cannot by themselves establish the ground-level independence of intentionality from the co-primitives.

## 1. Thin relation versus intentional relation

A thin structural relation has form:

R(x,y).

Examples include:
- covariance;
- causal dependence;
- spatial relation;
- formal mapping;
- transition relation.

An intentional relation has the form:

About_M(c,o)

where content c is about object/state o for/in subject M.

The question is whether M is eliminable:

About(c,o) = some purely structural R(c,o).

If yes, H_S can absorb intentionality.
If no, subjecthood is irreducible.

## 2. Reference underdetermination

A bare correlation can connect c with many candidate targets:
- immediate cause;
- distal cause;
- functional role;
- class of similar causes;
- future use;
- observer-assigned interpretation.

Structure may constrain reference but does not trivially identify one relation as **the content's referent**.

This underdetermination does not prove subjecthood. It creates an explanatory burden for impersonal structural semantics.

## 3. Misrepresentation

Intentional content can apparently be false or mistaken:

M represents p while not-p obtains.

A simple covariance model struggles because when p is absent the representing state may still occur.

To call that state a misrepresentation requires a standard distinguishing:
- what the state actually tracks now;
- what it is supposed to represent.

This introduces normativity/function.

Structural accounts can respond with teleology, functional role, historical selection, inferential role, or primitive semantic relations.

Therefore misrepresentation pressures thin structuralism but does not deductively establish Mind.

## 4. Reasons versus causes

A cause explains why an event occurred in a causal sequence.

A reason-as-reason is content apprehended under an intelligible relation such as:
- evidence for;
- justification for;
- goal served by;
- consideration counting in favor of.

The same proposition may causally affect a system without functioning as a reason for that system.

Thus:

Cause(r,S) -/-> ReasonFor_M(r,S).

Reasons-as-reasons involve normative/intelligible relations among contents and the subject's apprehension of them as bearing on action or belief.

## 5. Can impersonal structure contain reasons?

Candidate structuralist responses:

### RS1. Primitive normative structure
"Counts-in-favor-of" is an objective primitive relation among propositions/actions.

This can preserve impersonalism but adds a primitive normative relation beyond thin L/I/A unless derived from them.

### RS2. Inferential-role reduction
A reason is whatever occupies the appropriate role in a network of inferential transitions.

Challenge: distinguishes rule-conforming transitions from apprehension of a consideration **as** a reason only if normativity is already encoded in the role.

### RS3. Functional/teleological reduction
Reasons reduce to states functioning to guide behavior toward goals.

Challenge: goal, function, correctness, and guidance must themselves be grounded without circularly importing intentional/normative vocabulary.

### RS4. Dispositional reduction
To take r as a reason is to possess dispositions to infer/act under r-like conditions.

Challenge: disposition may model behavior while leaving open whether the content is apprehended as justificatory.

None is dismissed; each owes a non-circular reduction.

## 6. The "as" structure

A recurring feature is aspectual apprehension:

M apprehends r **as** evidence for p.
M apprehends q **as** an end.
M apprehends p **as** preferable under R.

The "as" relation appears to bind:
- content;
- interpretation;
- normative role;
- unified point of view.

This is a strong candidate marker of subjecthood.

Call:

Aspect_M(c,k): M apprehends content c under aspect/role k.

An impersonal reduction must explain Aspect without merely replacing M with an unexamined interpretation function whose own semantic status is assumed.

## 7. First-person unity is not yet required

The present argument does not need phenomenal consciousness, qualia, or a human-style first-person perspective.

Minimal subjecthood requires only:

Sbj(M): one locus in which content is intentionally related, rational relations are apprehended, and acts are attributable.

This is deliberately thinner than consciousness.

Therefore an objection based solely on whether ultimate Mind has human-like phenomenology misses the current target.

## 8. Same-level H_S burden

For H_S to absorb thick intentionality, it must show that one impersonal structural essence can yield:
- determinate reference;
- truth/falsity or correctness conditions;
- misrepresentation;
- reasons-as-reasons;
- aspectual apprehension;
- source-attributable action;

without presupposing a subject or adding independent semantic/normative primitives that recreate the explanatory-cost problem.

If it succeeds, H_M∞ loses a major advantage.

## 9. H_M∞ fit

A subject model naturally represents:

About_M(c,o).
Aspect_M(c,k).
ReasonFor_M(r,S).
Act_M(S).

because the same M is the locus of:
- content;
- interpretation;
- rational relation;
- action.

This is an explanatory fit, not proof that M∞ exists.

The Mind hypothesis gains only if these relations are independently real and resist impersonal reduction.

## 10. Co-primitive connection

The thick triadic pattern now becomes:

I_thick: content with determinate aboutness/reference.

L_thick: rational/normative relations among contents, including validity/evidence/reasons.

A_thick: subject-attributable act responsive to those contents/reasons.

C: one locus unifying content, rational relation, and act.

This is not obtained by redefining the original thin co-primitives. It is a candidate thickening that requires independent warrant.

## 11. Does intentionality prove a subject?

If intentionality is defined as "a subject's aboutness," yes trivially, but that would be circular.

Use a neutral definition instead:

IntrinsicIntent(c,o): c has determinate aboutness toward o that is not merely assigned by an external interpreter.

Question: can IntrinsicIntent be instantiated without any subject-locus?

**Disposition:** no deductive impossibility has yet been shown. However, the impersonal account owes substantial additional semantic/normative structure.

## 12. Explanatory-cost result

H_S options:

1. reduce intentionality/reasons to thin structure;
2. add primitive semantic/normative relations;
3. deny intrinsic intentionality/reasons at the ultimate level.

Option 1 requires successful reduction.
Option 2 increases primitive burden.
Option 3 remains viable unless independent argument shows thick features belong at the ultimate level.

H_M∞:

Mind supplies a candidate single locus for semantic, normative, and act relations, but still owes warrant that the ultimate co-primitives are thick in this way.

## 13. Disposition

- Correlation -> determinate reference: **NOT ESTABLISHED**.
- Cause -> reason-as-reason: **NO**.
- Misrepresentation introduces normative burden: **YES**.
- Aspectual "as" structure is a strong subjecthood marker: **MEDIUM**.
- Intrinsic intentionality deductively requires subject: **NOT YET PROVEN**.
- H_S can reduce thick intentionality without extra primitives: **UNESTABLISHED**.
- H_M∞ naturally unifies thick semantics/normativity/action: **YES as model fit, not existence proof**.
- Thickening of ultimate I/L/A: **STILL OPEN**.

## 14. Next target

The remaining question is upstream:

> Do the co-primitives at the ultimate level need to be thick, or can thin L/I/A be metaphysically complete?

The strongest route is the **intelligibility/normativity test**:
- logical relations are not merely regularities;
- truths and valid consequences appear objective;
- if ultimate L is genuinely normative rather than merely descriptive, determine whether an impersonal structural ground can account for that normativity without additional primitives.

This should be worked next.
