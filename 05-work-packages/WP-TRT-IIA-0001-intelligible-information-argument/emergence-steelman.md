# Emergence Steelman and Causal-Adequacy Test

Status: ACTIVE RED TEAM
Date: 2026-10-01

## Strong emergentist position

A sophisticated emergentist need not claim that complexity magically creates mind.
The stronger claim is that organized physical systems instantiate higher-level
causal/functional properties not possessed by isolated components. Consciousness,
representation, semantic behavior, and rational cognition may supervene on or be
realized by particular organizations of neural or computational processes.

The fact that neurons individually lack beliefs does not show that neural systems
cannot instantiate beliefs. IIA therefore rejects the simple composition objection.

## Tests IIA may legitimately impose

E1 Mechanism: identify the organization and dependencies alleged to realize the
higher-level property.

E2 Content: explain what makes state C about X rather than Y, or state explicitly
that aboutness is primitive.

E3 Truth: explain how the system obtains conditions under which C is correct or
incorrect about an external domain.

E4 Normativity: distinguish a causally produced inference from an inference that is
valid, justified, or rationally required.

E5 Error: explain misrepresentation rather than merely unexpected behavior.

E6 Generalization: explain successful reasoning beyond the selection/training domain.

E7 Causal relevance: explain whether semantic/rational properties themselves do
causal work or are epiphenomenal descriptions of lower-level causes.

## Naturalistic candidate bridges

The audit must engage:
- teleosemantics: content from biological function/history;
- informational semantics: content from lawful/informational dependence;
- functional-role semantics: content from inferential/system role;
- predictive processing: representation from generative error-minimizing models;
- computational functionalism: cognition from implementation-independent functional organization;
- evolutionary reliabilism: truth tracking as an adaptive consequence plus cultural refinement.

## Burden symmetry

IIA cannot demand mechanistic decomposition from emergentism while allowing
"rational ground" to terminate explanation without scrutiny. H_RG must explain why
a rational ground predicts or makes intelligible the particular source/receiver
correspondence, and what is gained beyond redescribing it.

## Interim result

Emergence remains a live alternative. Merely naming emergence is insufficient;
merely noting that constituent parts lack semantic properties is also insufficient.
The dispute turns on comparative bridge adequacy.
