# Intelligible Information Argument — Core Specification v1.0

Status: CANDIDATE BASELINE
Date: 2026-10-01
Work package: WP-TRT-IIA-0001

## 1. Purpose

IIA addresses a joint explanandum: a mind-independent world exhibits stable,
recoverable structure, while finite rational agents can form representations of
that world that are semantically about it, assessable as true or false, governed
by rational norms, and capable of supporting successful novel prediction.

The argument is abductive. It does not infer a sender from communication vocabulary
and does not derive semantics from Shannon information.

## 2. Typed layers

Let:

- W = mind-independent world/domain.
- S(W) = stable structure instantiated by W.
- I_S = Shannon/statistical information available through lawful dependence.
- C = representational content that purports to be about W.
- T = truth relation between C and W.
- N = rational/normative relations among contents and inferences.
- K = knowledge, requiring at minimum appropriately grounded truth plus epistemic
  warrant under the adopted account.

Typing constraint:

```text
S(W) -> I_S -> C -> T/N -> K
```

Arrows mark explanatory/causal bridges to be accounted for, not entailments.

Non-identity constraint:

```text
I_S != C != T != N != K
```

No claim of ontological independence follows merely from non-identity.

## 3. Empirical and epistemic observations

O1. Physical and other mind-independent domains exhibit stable regularities.
O2. Finite agents can recover information about those regularities.
O3. Agents form representations that purport to describe states beyond immediate
    sensory contact.
O4. Such representations can succeed or fail with respect to external states.
O5. Rational evaluation distinguishes valid/invalid inference and warranted/
    unwarranted belief, not merely frequent/infrequent state transitions.
O6. Mathematical and theoretical representations sometimes enable successful
    prediction of previously unobserved phenomena.

## 4. Bridge questions

BQ1: What makes structure in W recoverable by R?
BQ2: What makes an internal state contentful or about W rather than merely correlated?
BQ3: What grounds truth conditions for C?
BQ4: What grounds normative relations among inferences?
BQ5: What makes receiver processes reliably truth-tracking rather than merely
     behaviorally useful?
BQ6: Why is the source domain sufficiently stable and tractable for these processes
     to succeed?

Any competing ontology may answer some questions by taking their targets as
primitive. IIA must count such stopping points symmetrically.

## 5. Minimal argument

P1. The conjunction X = {O1...O6} obtains to a substantial degree.

P2. X contains multiple typed explanatory transitions; statistical dependence alone
    does not, without bridge principles, entail semantic content, truth, rational
    normativity, or knowledge.

P3. An adequate worldview-level explanation of X should account for both source-side
    intelligibility and receiver-side rational/semantic adequacy, while identifying
    any primitives or brute stopping points.

P4. A common rational-ground hypothesis H_RG proposes that source intelligibility
    and receiver rationality share an ultimate rational provenance.

P5. H_RG is evidentially favored by X only if, after comparison with serious
    alternatives, it explains more of X with equal or lower unsupported explanatory
    cost and without merely relocating the unexplained features into H_RG.

C. Therefore X is at most abductive evidence for H_RG in proportion to H_RG's
   comparative explanatory performance.

## 6. Inferential ceiling

Even a successful IIA v1.0 directly warrants, at most, a common rational ground.
Further conclusions require bridges:
- rational ground -> mind;
- mind -> personal agency;
- personal agency -> necessity/aseity;
- necessary personal ground -> classical theism;
- classical theism -> specifically Christian revelation.

IIA must never collapse these steps.

## 7. Falsification / downgrade conditions

IIA should be weakened or abandoned if:
- the typed distinctions collapse under a successful reductive account;
- naturalistic models explain source tractability plus receiver semantics,
  truth-tracking, and normativity with no greater primitive burden;
- H_RG simply re-labels X rather than explaining it;
- the world/receiver correspondence is shown to be fully expected under a competing
  model once selection effects are correctly conditioned;
- the argument depends on an equivocation between statistical and semantic information.
