## Material result

Dretske is a more important antecedent than the seed ledger initially indicated.
He explicitly treats source and receiver as information-theoretic labels for known
and knower and treats the channel as central to epistemology. Therefore TX/RX
architecture itself is not novel.

Polkinghorne is likewise a direct antecedent for the two-ended fit: human reason
and external rational order are linked, on his account, by common origin in divine
Rationality. Therefore "world intelligibility + mind intelligibility -> Creator"
is not novel.

The remaining candidate distinctiveness of IIA is the explicit integration of:

1. Dretske-style source/channel/receiver epistemology;
2. the Shannon/statistical versus semantic-information boundary;
3. Wigner/Polkinghorne world-mind intelligibility;
4. truth-aptness and rational normativity as properties of successful cognitive uptake;
5. an abductive comparison requiring one ontology to explain both source-side
   intelligibility and receiver-side semantic/rational adequacy.

This currently supports, at most, a candidate classification of DISTINCT_SYNTHESIS.
It does not support DISTINCT_ARGUMENT yet.

## Primary-source anchors

- Shannon, Claude E. (1948), "A Mathematical Theory of Communication",
  Bell System Technical Journal 27. Shannon explicitly brackets semantic aspects
  from the engineering problem.
- Bar-Hillel, Yehoshua and Rudolf Carnap (1953), "Semantic Information",
  British Journal for the Philosophy of Science 4(14):147-157. They explicitly
  warn against transferring statistical-information results into semantic and
  pragmatic domains without justification.
- Wigner, Eugene P. (1960), "The Unreasonable Effectiveness of Mathematics in
  the Natural Sciences", Communications on Pure and Applied Mathematics 13.
- Dretske, Fred I. (1981), Knowledge and the Flow of Information. Dretske develops
  source/channel/receiver relations as an epistemological framework.
- Polkinghorne, John (1991), primary article located in Perspectives on Science
  and Christian Faith. He explicitly frames "reason within" and "reason without"
  as linked by common origin in the Rationality of the Creator.
