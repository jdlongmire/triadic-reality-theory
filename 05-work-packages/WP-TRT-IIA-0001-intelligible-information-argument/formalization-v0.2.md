# Intelligible Information Argument — Formalization v0.2

Status: ACTIVE DRAFT
Date: 2026-10-01
Work package: WP-TRT-IIA-0001

## Typed vocabulary

Let:

- W = mind-independent world/domain.
- S(W) = stable, discoverable structure instantiated by W.
- R = finite rational receiver/agent.
- I_S(W;R) = Shannon/mutual information available through lawful dependence between W and R.
- C_R(W) = representational content in R that is about W.
- T(C_R,W) = truth relation between R's content and W.
- N_R = rational norms governing inference among contents.
- K_R(W) = knowledge of W acquired by R.

No identity is assumed among I_S, C_R, T, N_R, and K_R.

## Empirical/epistemic data

E1. Some finite agents successfully discriminate and predict mind-independent states.
E2. Mature science constructs representations whose empirical success substantially
    exceeds immediate survival-scale discrimination.
E3. Mathematical structures developed with varying degrees of independence from
    particular empirical targets can successfully model physical domains.
E4. Cognitive success is evaluated under truth and rational-inference norms, not
    solely by statistical covariance.

## Bridge constraints

B1. I_S can characterize lawful dependence/recoverability but does not by itself
    entail semantic content, truth, or rational normativity.

B2. An explanation of K_R(W) that appeals to semantic content must specify how
    C_R(W) obtains its aboutness and truth conditions, or explicitly take them as
    primitive.

B3. An explanation of receiver reliability must be distinguished from an explanation
    of why W instantiates the kind of stable and mathematically tractable structure
    that is reliably recoverable.

B4. No intentional transmitter may be inferred from source/channel/receiver
    terminology alone.

## Explanandum

X = conjunction of:
  X1: W has stable rationally/mathematically tractable structure;
  X2: R can acquire information about W;
  X3: R forms content about W that can be true or false;
  X4: R can evaluate and extend that content under rational norms;
  X5: the resulting representations sometimes support successful novel prediction.

IIA concerns the joint probability/explanatory status of X, not any component alone.

## Competing hypothesis classes

H_RM: Rational-ground model. W and finite rational capacities ultimately share a
      rational ontological ground.

H_SR: Structural realism. W possesses objective structure; cognition increasingly
      tracks it.

H_ER: Evolutionary reliabilism plus naturalistic semantics. Reliable cognition and
      content arise through selection, learning, biological function, and culture.

H_CF: Computational/functional emergence. Semantic/rational capacities are realized
      by sufficiently organized physical information processing.

H_BF: Brute-fact model. The joint correspondence X has no deeper explanation.

These classes can overlap; later work must construct mutually discriminating versions.

## Abductive form

A1. X obtains to a nontrivial degree.

A2. A satisfactory explanation of X should account for source-side tractability,
    receiver-side epistemic capacity, and the bridge from physical/statistical
    dependence to semantic and norm-governed cognition, or state explicitly which
    elements are primitive.

A3. H_RM offers a unified candidate explanation: source-side intelligibility and
    receiver-side rationality derive from a common rational ground.

C_abd. To the extent H_RM explains X with fewer unsupported bridge principles and
       greater explanatory integration than H_SR, H_ER, H_CF, and H_BF, X is
       abductive evidence favoring H_RM.

## What does NOT follow

The present argument does not deductively establish:
- an intentional sender from Shannon theory;
- that semantic information is immaterial;
- that physical systems cannot realize semantics;
- that evolution cannot produce reliable cognition;
- that the rational ground is personal;
- divine necessity, omniscience, moral perfection, Trinity, or Christianity.

Those require independent bridges.

## Principal research burden

The key question is comparative, not merely rhetorical:

Does H_RM genuinely reduce explanatory commitments, or does it relocate the same
problems into an unexplained rational ground?

Until that question is answered against steelmanned alternatives, IIA remains an
argument schema rather than an established theistic argument.
