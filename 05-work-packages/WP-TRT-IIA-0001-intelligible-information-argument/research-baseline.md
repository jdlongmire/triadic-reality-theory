# Intelligible Information Argument (IIA) — Initial Research Baseline

Status: ACTIVE RESEARCH NOTE
Date: 2026-10-01
Work package: WP-TRT-IIA-0001

## Working explanandum

Reality is not merely causally encountered. Rational agents recover stable,
mathematically expressible and logically constrained structure from it, form
truth-apt representations of that structure, and successfully use those
representations to predict and manipulate states of the external world.

The IIA asks what best explains the joint fact that:

1. mind-independent reality is intelligibly structured;
2. information about that structure is recoverable by finite agents; and
3. those agents can apprehend recovered structure semantically, as truth-bearing
   knowledge rather than merely correlated physical states.

## Provisional architecture

```text
mind-independent reality
        |
        | recoverable structure / physical correlation
        v
   informational relation
        |
        | cognitive uptake
        v
 rational receiver
        |
        | semantic interpretation + rational evaluation
        v
 truth-apt knowledge
```

TX/RX is a systems-engineering heuristic. It must not silently import a conscious
sender into the premises.

## Shannon boundary

Shannon information quantifies statistical structure relevant to communication,
coding, entropy, and recoverability. It does not by itself establish semantic
meaning, truth, intentionality, or rational normativity.

Accordingly, IIA must not argue:

> Shannon channel -> transmitter -> conscious transmitter -> God.

The research question begins where purely quantitative information is insufficient
to exhaust the observed epistemic phenomenon.

## Minimal abductive skeleton, v0.1

P1. Mind-independent reality exhibits stable structure recoverable by rational agents.

P2. Rational agents can convert interaction with that structure into semantically
contentful, truth-apt representations.

P3. The correspondence is sufficiently robust to support successful novel
prediction, mathematical modeling, and counterfactual reasoning about reality.

P4. A complete ontology should account for both the intelligibility of the source
domain and the semantic/rational adequacy of receivers, rather than merely
stipulating either side.

P5. Purely syntactic/statistical information does not, without additional bridge
principles, entail semantic content, truth-aptness, intentionality, or rational
normativity.

C1. The joint source/receiver correspondence constitutes an explanandum exceeding
a merely Shannon-level description.

Abductive hypothesis H: a prior rational ground is a candidate unified explanation
of both mind-independent intelligibility and finite rational receptivity.

The inferential work is to determine whether H has greater explanatory adequacy
than serious alternatives. No stronger conclusion is licensed at this stage.

## Initial antecedents

- Claude E. Shannon: mathematical communication theory.
- Eugene P. Wigner: unreasonable effectiveness of mathematics.
- John Polkinghorne: reason within and reason without sharing a proposed origin in divine rationality.
- Luciano Floridi and semantic-information literature: truth, meaning, and limits of merely quantitative information.
- Bar-Hillel and Carnap / Dretske: semantic-information antecedents to audit.
- Plantinga: rational reliability and theism/naturalism comparisons to distinguish from IIA.

## Novelty hypothesis

Candidate novelty is not any ingredient individually. Candidate novelty, if
sustained, lies in explicit integration into a single
source/recoverability/semantic-truth/receiver-adequacy argument and in treating the
two-ended correspondence as the explanandum.

Current novelty status: UNESTABLISHED.

## Required defeaters

The package must steelman structural realism, evolutionary reliabilism,
teleosemantics, computational functionalism, emergentism, selection/anthropic
explanations, brute-fact intelligibility, and anti-realist or constructivist
accounts of mathematical fit.

## Success criterion

A successful WP need not vindicate IIA. It succeeds if it determines, with
traceable sources, whether IIA is a distinct argument, a distinct synthesis, a
reformulation, or an unsuccessful inference, and identifies the strongest
conclusion actually supported.
