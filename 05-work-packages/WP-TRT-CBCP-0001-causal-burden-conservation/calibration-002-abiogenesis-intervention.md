# CBCP Adversarial Calibration 002: Abiogenesis and Investigator-Supplied Causation

**Work package:** WP-TRT-CBCP-0001  
**Status:** Initial adversarial calibration  
**Date:** 2026-10-04

## Purpose

Test the Causal Burden Conservation Principle (CBCP) in the difficult middle case of experimental prebiotic chemistry.

The calibration must avoid two opposite errors:

1. dismissing genuine demonstrations of abiotic chemical generative capacity merely because investigators designed the experiment; and
2. transferring evidential credit from chemistry demonstrated under investigator-supplied constraints to a historical pathway in which those constraints have not independently been shown to arise.

The issue is not whether investigators are present. All experiments involve investigators. The issue is whether investigator actions materially supply causal resources or pathway constraints that the historical hypothesis itself must explain.

## Primary calibration sources

### Miller 1953

Stanley Miller circulated a specified mixture of methane, ammonia, water, and hydrogen through an apparatus with electrical discharge and detected amino acids.

Reference:

- Miller, S. L. (1953), "A Production of Amino Acids Under Possible Primitive Earth Conditions," *Science* 117(3046): 528-529. DOI: 10.1126/science.117.3046.528.

CBCP credits this experiment with demonstrating that specified organic products can arise abiotically from specified feedstocks under the tested energetic and environmental conditions.

It does not by itself establish the complete historical provenance of those conditions, nor a pathway from the products to an autonomous living system.

### Patel et al. 2015

Patel, Percivalle, Ritson, Duffy, and Sutherland experimentally showed that precursors relevant to ribonucleotides, amino acids, and lipids can arise from a common cyanosulfidic reaction network. Key steps use ultraviolet light, hydrogen sulfide, and Cu(I)-Cu(II) photoredox chemistry.

Reference:

- Patel, B. H. et al. (2015), "Common origins of RNA, protein and lipid precursors in a cyanosulfidic protometabolism," *Nature Chemistry* 7: 301-307. DOI: 10.1038/nchem.2202.

CBCP credits this as substantial evidence that a broader family of biologically relevant precursor chemistries can share common abiotic causal resources. This is stronger than demonstrating isolated synthesis routes independently.

### Sutherland 2017

Sutherland describes the larger target as proceeding from simple feedstocks and energy sources to a living system through extensive synthesis and coordinated assembly over numerous steps governed by environmental factors and inherent chemical reactivity. He states that demonstrating such a process would show how life can start from the inanimate, while also stating that the field was not yet close to achieving that end.

Reference:

- Sutherland, J. D. (2017), "Studies on the origin of life — the end of the beginning," *Nature Reviews Chemistry* 1, 0012. DOI: 10.1038/s41570-016-0012.

This is especially useful for CBCP because it distinguishes progress on component chemistry from discharge of the full integrated generative burden.

### Richert 2018

Clemens Richert explicitly addresses human intervention in prebiotic chemistry and argues that the extent of intervention required to obtain experimental results is worth reporting.

Reference:

- Richert, C. (2018), "Prebiotic chemistry and human intervention," *Nature Communications* 9, 5177. DOI: 10.1038/s41467-018-07219-5.

The methodological importance is independent of any design argument: intervention is a causal variable that should be visible in the evidential record.

### Ritson et al. 2018

Flow chemistry has been used specifically to improve environmental realism by mimicking aspects of early-Earth surface chemistry.

Reference:

- Ritson, D. J. et al. (2018), "Mimicking the surface and prebiotic chemistry of early Earth using flow chemistry," *Nature Communications* 9, 1821. DOI: 10.1038/s41467-018-04147-2.

CBCP therefore must not freeze the field at earlier intervention-heavy experiments. Experiments that replace researcher-mediated transitions with independently plausible environmental processes genuinely discharge portions of the historical burden.

## Causal graph

The broad abiogenesis claim can be decomposed schematically:

```text
geochemical environment
-> available feedstocks
-> activated/biologically relevant monomers
-> polymers / reaction networks
-> compartmentalized systems
-> coupled replication + metabolism + compartmentalization
-> heritable variation
-> open-ended Darwinian evolution
-> autonomous living system
```

This is intentionally schematic. CBCP does not require that historical abiogenesis followed this exact sequence. It requires that whatever sequence is proposed expose its material causal transitions.

## The intervention problem

An experimental pathway can be represented as:

```text
starting chemistry
+ physical law
+ environmental conditions
+ investigator interventions I
-> experimental product E
```

The historical hypothesis seeks something closer to:

```text
starting chemistry
+ physical law
+ historically warranted environment H
-> E
```

The experiment supports the historical pathway to the degree that the causal work performed by `I` is either:

1. irrelevant to production of `E`;
2. replaceable by independently evidenced features of `H`; or
3. shown experimentally to arise from the proposed environment without equivalent directed intervention.

The mere presence of an investigator does not invalidate an experiment. Conversely, a chemically successful experiment does not automatically establish a prebiotic pathway if the investigator supplies material transition constraints that the historical environment has not been shown to provide.

## Intervention classes

CBCP should distinguish at least four classes.

### Class 0: observational/measurement intervention

Actions used only to measure, sample, or characterize the reaction without materially producing the target state.

These generally impose little historical causal burden.

### Class 1: environment-setting intervention

The investigator establishes temperature, pressure, radiation, pH, mineral surfaces, wet-dry cycles, or feedstock composition intended to represent an independently motivated natural environment.

The burden is to establish that the corresponding environmental conditions are historically/geochemically plausible. Once independently warranted, the experiment can legitimately demonstrate the causal capacity of chemistry under those conditions.

### Class 2: pathway-enabling intervention

The investigator changes conditions between stages, isolates products, concentrates reactants, removes inhibitors, adds reagents at selected times, or transfers products into a new environment.

These actions may materially enable the pathway. The historical model bears a burden to identify natural processes capable of supplying equivalent sequencing, separation, concentration, cycling, or transport.

### Class 3: outcome-directed intervention

The investigator selects successful products or pathways because they move the system toward the desired biological target, while unsuccessful branches are discarded or bypassed.

This creates the strongest attribution problem. If outcome-directed selection is necessary for traversing the demonstrated pathway, the experiment has established chemical possibility under intelligent selection more strongly than it has established the corresponding unguided historical pathway.

These classes are methodological, not accusations of experimental impropriety.

## CBCP audit

### What current experiments positively establish

The literature provides genuine positive evidence for multiple non-biological generative capacities:

- simple feedstocks can yield amino acids under specified conditions;
- photochemical and redox networks can produce biologically relevant precursor molecules;
- common reaction networks can connect precursor chemistry relevant to multiple cellular subsystems;
- environmental analogues such as irradiation, flow, mineral interaction, and cycling can perform causal work previously supplied more directly by laboratory handling;
- self-assembly and self-replication phenomena exist in chemical systems.

These results count positively for natural chemical causal adequacy at the transitions actually demonstrated.

### What does not automatically follow

The following inference is invalid without additional bridging evidence:

```text
chemistry generated component X under experimental conditions
therefore
the complete historical pathway to autonomous life is demonstrated
```

Likewise:

```text
the complete pathway is unresolved
therefore
mind generated the first life
```

Both violate CBCP.

## Burden transfer test

For every laboratory step, ask:

```text
Who or what supplied this constraint?
```

If the answer is the investigator, ask:

```text
Does the historical model independently identify a natural process that supplies an equivalent constraint?
```

If yes, test that proposed natural process.

If no, mark the transition as an unresolved historical burden rather than either:

```text
"the experiment proves abiogenesis"
```

or:

```text
"the intervention proves design"
```

## Mind-cause implication

Investigator intervention provides direct evidence that mind can generate and navigate constrained chemical pathways:

```text
mind
-> experimental specification
-> controlled constraints
-> chemical dynamics
-> target product
```

This establishes mind as a demonstrated generative contributor to the experimental system.

It does **not** establish that mind was historically required for the origin of life. That attribution requires evidence that the relevant constraints cannot be supplied, or are substantially less expected to be supplied, by the competing historical pathway.

Thus:

```text
mind-mediated experimental success
!=
historical proof of intelligent causation
```

but also:

```text
chemical transformation inside a mind-configured pathway
!=
demonstration that the whole pathway is unguided
```

## Emergence and provenance

Experimental prebiotic chemistry strongly evidences this conditional form:

```text
specified lawful chemistry
+ specified conditions
-> emergent chemical products/organization
```

That is genuine evidence for emergence.

It does not by itself evidence:

```text
undirected process
-> laws + relevant initial conditions + boundary conditions
```

The provenance of the lawful generative regime is a separate causal question. CBCP forbids transferring operational evidence across that boundary without an explicit bridge.

## Time

Time contributes opportunities for specified reactions and processes. It does not supply missing pathway constraints.

For a proposed transition, additional time has explanatory value only if the specified dynamics make the target state reachable and if the opportunity structure shows that added duration materially increases the relevant probability.

```text
time + demonstrated pathway -> potentially increased opportunity
time + missing pathway -> still a missing pathway
```

## Comparative disposition

**Natural chemical pathway:** receives substantial positive credit for demonstrated precursor synthesis, reaction networks, self-organization, and other experimentally established transitions.

**Complete abiogenesis pathway:** remains a composite historical claim whose burden is not discharged by success at component transitions alone.

**Mind pathway:** has demonstrated generative capacity to specify and navigate chemical pathways in experiments, but that fact alone does not establish mind as the historical cause of first life.

**Design inference:** must therefore be comparative and discriminating, not a deduction from the incompleteness of abiogenesis research.

## CBCP result

CBCP survives this calibration provisionally and produces a more discriminating appraisal than either polemical alternative.

> **Credit chemistry for every transition chemistry demonstrably crosses. Credit environmental models when independently plausible natural conditions replace investigator-supplied causal work. Record remaining transitions as unresolved. Do not transfer the success or failure of one causal edge to another.**

And:

> **Investigator intervention is neither an automatic defeater of prebiotic chemistry nor an irrelevant detail. Its epistemic significance is proportional to the causal work it performs in making the target pathway traversable.**
