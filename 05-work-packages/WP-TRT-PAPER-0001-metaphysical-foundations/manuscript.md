# Triadic Reality Theory: A Metaphysical Foundation for Quantum and Classical Physics

**James D. Longmire**  
ORCID: 0009-0009-1383-7698  
**Status:** Initial argument draft, not publication-ready (2026-10-30).  
**Research work package:** WP-TRT-PAPER-0001.

## Abstract
Classical and quantum physics employ different state descriptions and mathematical dynamics while relying upon intelligible distinctions, constrained physical possibilities and determinate empirical records. Triadic Reality Theory (TRT) proposes three irreducible co-primitives as candidate necessary metaphysical conditions of determinate actuality: ontological logical constraint (L₃), unbounded informational representability (I∞), and fundamental action (A), defined as state transition without presupposing physical time. The proposal distinguishes representability, logical admissibility, actualizability and actuality. It argues that neither state-space structure nor dynamical equations, considered merely as formal descriptions, settle the ontological question of why physical actuality obtains. Classical and quantum mechanics are examined as compatible instantiations of the triadic architecture, with particular attention to superposition, measurement, decoherence and the classical limit. Compatibility is distinguished from derivation and confirmation. The analysis identifies substantial unresolved obligations, including the move from conditions of description to ontological necessity, the status of change in static ontologies, and the derivation of quantum probability and specific dynamics. TRT is presented as a falsifiability-governed research programme whose metaphysical thesis remains contestable and whose physics-facing models require independent mathematical and empirical appraisal.

**Keywords:** metaphysics of physics; quantum foundations; classical mechanics; logical realism; information; action; decoherence; actualization.

## 1. Introduction: the grounding question
A successful physical theory specifies mathematical structures and empirical correspondences. Classical mechanics represents systems through states and dynamical evolution; quantum mechanics employs state vectors or density operators, observables and probability assignments. Both presuppose that experimental claims can be made and evaluated. Neither formal success alone establishes which metaphysical entities are fundamental.

The proposed question is narrower than a derivation of quantum theory: what, if anything, must be true of reality for determinate physical states and changes to obtain? TRT answers with a triad. This paper distinguishes a weak representational observation, that physical theories can be organized into constraints, states and dynamics, from the stronger metaphysical thesis that logical constraint, differentiation and action are irreducible constituents of actuality. The latter requires argument independent of the convenience of the former.

## 2. The typed ontology
Let R denote representable configurations, C the subset admissible under L₃, D the actualizable configurations, and X the actual domain. The canonical commitments are D ⊆ C ⊆ R and X ⊆ D. The reverse inclusion C ⊆ D is not established. In particular, logical consistency does not by itself demonstrate physical possibility.

L₃ names Identity, Non-Contradiction and Excluded Middle as candidate ontological constraints on determinate actuality. I∞ denotes unbounded informational representability; a representable contradiction need not be actualizable. A denotes primitive state transition/change, schematically A: sᵢ → sⱼ, without taking physical time as an input to its definition. Ordered transitions may furnish a basis for derivative temporal succession. The shorthand χ ≡ A(I∞ | L₃) expresses the triad but does not establish the sufficiency of L₃ for D.

## 3. The necessity argument and its limits
The strongest initial argument for L₃ concerns the identity and non-contradiction of determinate actual states. A purported record that both obtains and fails to obtain in the same respect cannot function as a determinate record without further semantic distinctions. Yet this point does not alone demonstrate that classical logic exhausts physical ontology. Non-classical logics and interpretations of quantum propositions require separate examination, especially where excluded middle is invoked.

Informational differentiation is required for there to be distinguishable states. Whether this requires an independently existing infinite informational domain, rather than a structural feature of concrete reality, is a further metaphysical claim. Likewise, A is motivated by the difference between a list of possible configurations and actual change. A block-universe ontology challenges the claim that transition must be fundamental rather than relationally represented within a complete structure.

**Proposition (conditional, argued):** If determinate actuality requires coherent identity, distinguishable content and irreducible ontic transition, then the three functional roles L₃, I∞ and A are jointly required. **Limit:** The conditional does not establish the independence of its premises or that each role is an ontological primitive. These remain research obligations.

## 4. Classical physics as an instantiation
A classical model can be expressed through a state space S, constraints on admissible states, and a dynamical law mapping initial conditions to trajectories. In Hamiltonian mechanics a state may be represented as a point of phase space, with evolution generated by a Hamiltonian under appropriate assumptions. This gives an intelligible mapping from classical physics to differentiation, constraint and transition. It does not prove that the three are ontologically irreducible or that Hamiltonian dynamics follows from TRT.

Classical determinacy should not be conflated with the logical law of excluded middle. A proposition about an outcome can be bivalent while the theory's dynamics remains stochastic. Conversely, a deterministic mathematical flow can be defined without establishing the truth of a particular metaphysical theory of time.

## 5. Quantum physics as an instantiation
Quantum models represent alternatives through states in Hilbert space and assign measurement statistics through additional mathematical structure. Superposition does not by itself imply an actual contradiction of the form P and not-P in the same respect. Entanglement prevents the simple factorization of certain composite states. Bell-type results constrain families of local hidden-variable models under specified assumptions. None of these facts uniquely selects TRT.

The mapping to the triad is therefore interpretive: distinguishable quantum alternatives illustrate informational structure, consistency conditions constrain admissible descriptions, and unitary evolution or measurement interactions are candidate downstream specifications of change. It remains open whether the primitive A can explain measurement outcomes without importing a particular interpretation or probability rule.

## 6. Decoherence and the classical limit
Environmental decoherence can suppress interference terms in a reduced density matrix relative to relevant system-environment interactions. This supports an account of the practical stability of certain records and classical-looking observables. Decoherence by itself does not establish a single outcome ontology. Nor does classical behavior follow merely from L₃. A TRT explanation of the classical limit must specify the dynamics, the relevant approximation regime, and the emergence of robust records.

## 7. Research obligations and rival accounts
A substantive TRT programme must confront at least four alternatives: structural realist interpretations that deny the need for independent informational substance; block-universe accounts that regard change as derivative; instrumentalist accounts that refuse the ontological inference; and quantum interpretations with different accounts of definite outcomes. TRT earns explanatory advantage only if it provides independent necessity arguments, resolves an open explanatory burden, or yields a discriminating consequence through a formal auxiliary model.

## 8. Conclusion
TRT offers a compact candidate ontology for determinate physical reality. Its immediate value is a disciplined articulation of the distinctions between representation, logical admissibility, actualizability and actuality, together with an explicit challenge to ground their relationship. Classical and quantum theories can be read through this architecture. Whether the architecture is necessary, irreducible and physically fruitful remains open to philosophical counterargument, formal development and severe empirical tests.
