# TRT Fundamental Laws of Logic Physical Model

## Governing Hypothesis, Falsification Criterion, and Experimental Research Program

> **Status:** Governing research statement
>
> **Framework:** Triadic Reality Theory (TRT)
>
> **Purpose:** Re-establish the primary empirical objective and prevent subsidiary investigations
> from displacing the central hypothesis.
>
> **Authority:** JD Longmire, 2026-08-29. This statement governs subsequent work in
> `WP-TRT-LCC-0001` and `OPN-009`.

TRT-FLL is a **physical model** proposing that the Fundamental Laws of Logic constitute physically
operative constraints on which informational states can actualize in physical reality. It is not
primarily a philosophy-of-logic project and does not test whether logical rules are valid inside a
chosen formal language. It proposes that $L$ constrains the physically admissible state space.

The model is

$$
\boxed{\chi\equiv\mathcal A(I\mid L)},
$$

where

$$
L=\text{global admissibility constraint},\qquad
I=\text{informational state},\qquad
\mathcal A=\text{primitive action},\qquad
\chi=\text{physical actualization}.
$$

Bivalent actualization is an experimentally exposed consequence of this physical ontology.

## 1. Governing Objective

The central empirical claim under investigation is:

> The three Fundamental Laws of Logic constrain physical informational actualization such that no
> irreducibly non-bivalent informational state can become actual in physical reality.

The three laws are:

1. **Identity:** A thing is itself. A proposition $P$, evaluated as an actualized informational
   state, must retain determinate identity with respect to the proposition, context, time, and
   relevant respect under consideration.
2. **Non-Contradiction:** $P$ and $\neg P$ cannot both be actual in the same respect, context, and
   actualization.
3. **Excluded Middle:** For an actualized proposition $P$, either $P$ or $\neg P$ obtains.

The resulting physical hypothesis is:

$$
\boxed{
\forall \chi,\quad
\chi\text{ is a physical informational actualization}
\Rightarrow
\chi\text{ satisfies Identity, Non\text{-}Contradiction, and Excluded Middle}.
}
$$

For a proposition $P$ instantiated as physical information by an actualization $\chi$, the
corresponding operational consequence is:

$$
\boxed{V(P_\chi)\in\{T,F\}.}
$$

TRT therefore predicts that physical actualization is bivalent.

Here $P_\chi$ is not merely a proposition in a formal calculus. It represents information
instantiated by the physical actualization $\chi$. The claim concerns what physical systems can
actually instantiate.

This claim concerns **actualized information**. It does not require that every conceivable quantum
proposition possess a context-independent, predetermined truth value prior to actualization. That
distinction is essential.

## 2. What TRT Is Not Claiming

The hypothesis must not be expanded into stronger claims that are unnecessary to TRT and have
already encountered substantial experimental or theoretical difficulty. TRT does not require:

- local hidden variables;
- classical determinism;
- counterfactual definiteness;
- global noncontextual value assignments;
- predetermined measurement outcomes;
- classical realism in the Bell sense;
- macrorealism in the Leggett--Garg sense;
- the absence of quantum superposition;
- the absence of contextuality;
- the absence of entanglement; or
- the absence of stochastic measurement outcomes.

A quantum system may therefore possess a superposed state, exhibit contextual behavior,
participate in nonlocal correlations, or lack a predetermined measurement value without violating
the TRT hypothesis.

The relevant question arises at actualization:

> When physical information becomes actual, can the resulting informational state be irreducibly
> non-bivalent?

TRT predicts that it cannot.

## 3. Physical Ontology and Admissibility Constraint

Excluded Middle and semantic bivalence are distinguishable concepts within the broader literature
on logic. TRT therefore must not define physical bivalence into existence. Its stronger formulation
is ontological: $L$ is already a primitive component of the physical model, functioning as the
global constraint on admissibility.

Thus

$$
\boxed{
\chi\equiv\mathcal A(I\mid L)
}
$$

is the physical model, and the exclusion rule is

$$
\boxed{I_{\mathrm{non\text{-}bivalent}}\notin\Omega_{\mathrm{actualizable}}.}
$$

An FLL-violating informational state is not merely undesirable within a description. The model
predicts that it cannot enter the physically actualized state space.

Consequently, the hypothesis incurs empirical risk. If physical reality produces an irreducibly
non-bivalent informational actualization, the hypothesis is false.

## 4. Candidate Non-Bivalent Actualizations

For a proposition $P$, consider the four-value space

$$
V(P)\in\{T,F,B,N\},
$$

where:

- $T$: $P$ is actual;
- $F$: $\neg P$ is actual;
- $B$: both $P$ and $\neg P$ are actual;
- $N$: neither $P$ nor $\neg P$ is actual.

TRT permits $\{T,F\}$. It forbids irreducible physical actualization of $\{B,N\}$.

A genuine $B$-state would constitute a physical informational glut and violate the relevant
Non-Contradiction constraint. A genuine $N$-state would constitute a physical informational gap
and violate the relevant Excluded-Middle constraint.

Identity is indispensable because apparent contradictions can otherwise be manufactured by
changing the proposition, context, time, reference frame, measurement basis, or relevant respect.
The experimental proposition must therefore be fixed before a candidate $B$ or $N$ event can count
against the hypothesis.

## 5. Falsification Criterion

The TRT-FLL Physical Model is falsified if there exists a reproducible physical actualization
$\chi$ and proposition $P_\chi$ such that

$$
\boxed{V(P_\chi)\in\{B,N\}}
$$

under controlled conditions preserving the identity of the proposition and its relevant context.
The result must survive ordinary competing explanations.

Equivalently,

$$
\boxed{
\exists\chi:\ I_\chi\in I_{\mathrm{non\text{-}bivalent}}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mathrm{TRT\text{-}FLL\ falsified}.
}
$$

In particular, a candidate falsification cannot merely consist of:

- detector failure or detector inefficiency;
- an unobserved event or incomplete measurement;
- ambiguous instrumentation or coarse-graining;
- conflation of different propositions or measurement contexts;
- temporal change between observations;
- ordinary stochastic uncertainty or epistemic ignorance;
- premeasurement quantum superposition or classical mixtures;
- post-selection artifacts;
- semantic relabeling of conventional outcomes; or
- expansion of an ordinary outcome alphabet.

These exclusions are not protective devices for making the hypothesis unfalsifiable. They specify
the phenomenon being claimed. The target is an **irreducibly non-bivalent physical informational
actualization**. If such an event is demonstrated, TRT must accept the result.

## 6. The Four-Valued Rival-Model Result

The initial attempt to construct a physically normalized four-valued rival produced a useful
negative result. Suppose a measurement instrument is assigned four outcomes $\{T,F,B,N\}$.

If all four outcomes are represented as normalized, physically distinguishable measurement
records, then $B$ and $N$ become ordinary members of an expanded outcome space. The physical
statement “Outcome $B$ occurred” is itself an ordinary bivalent proposition concerning the
measurement record. Labeling that outcome a logical glut does not establish that the underlying
physical information simultaneously actualized $P$ and $\neg P$. Likewise, labeling another
detector state $N$ a logical gap does not establish that neither $P$ nor $\neg P$ obtained.

A semantic interpretation has been attached to an ordinary physical outcome. Unless that
interpretation generates an independently measurable constraint unavailable to an ordinary
normalized outcome model, it lacks contrastive physical content.

Conversely, refusing to complete the candidate $B/N$ states as normalized outcomes prevents the
construction from supplying the complete likelihood required for experimental comparison.

This establishes a conditional negative result:

> A simple normalized expansion of the quantum measurement outcome space does not, by itself,
> produce physically non-bivalent actualization.

It does not establish that all conceivable non-bivalent physical theories are impossible. The open
problem therefore remains open.

## 7. Relation to Quantum Foundations

Modern physics has repeatedly tested propositions adjacent to the TRT hypothesis. Bell-type
experiments have placed severe constraints on local hidden-variable explanations. Kochen--Specker
results constrain global noncontextual assignments of predetermined binary values. Contextuality
experiments probe increasingly strong forms of those constraints. Leggett--Garg tests challenge
conjunctions associated with macrorealism and noninvasive measurability. Quantum eraser,
delayed-choice, weak-measurement, entanglement, and related experiments have challenged classical
intuitions concerning state, causation, measurement, and information.

These results must not be weakened or redescribed to preserve TRT. They should be accepted at full
strength and classified precisely according to what they demonstrate.

The central historical observation motivating the present research program is:

> Experiments have repeatedly falsified stronger classical assumptions surrounding physical
> reality without thereby demonstrating an irreducibly non-bivalent physical informational
> actualization.

Bell violations do not produce a detector record simultaneously $+1$ and $-1$ in the same
measurement respect. Kochen--Specker does not demonstrate a proposition physically actualizing
both truth values or neither; it constrains global, context-independent predetermined values.
Superposition does not by itself constitute simultaneous contradictory actualization; it describes
the quantum state prior to the relevant measurement actualization. Contextuality demonstrates that
measurement outcomes cannot generally be understood through a single global noncontextual
valuation structure. It does not automatically establish a physically actualized logical gap or
glut.

This distinction defines the experimental boundary TRT is investigating.

## 8. The Empirical Research Question

The primary research question is:

> Can any physical experiment produce an irreducibly non-bivalent informational actualization?

It must not be replaced by whether quantum mechanics can be represented classically, whether all
quantum observables possess predetermined bivalent values, or whether a non-classical mathematical
logic can describe quantum systems.

The empirical question concerns what becomes actual:

$$
\boxed{\text{Can physical reality actualize }B\text{ or }N?}
$$

That is the experimental target.

## 9. Research Program

Future work is governed by this target. The immediate task is the
[FLL Empirical Falsification Ledger](../3-prediction/fll-empirical-falsification-ledger.md), covering
the strongest historical and contemporary experimental programs that approach the boundary.

For each experiment or theorem, the ledger records:

1. the proposition tested;
2. the target assumption;
3. what was actually measured;
4. whether the result bears directly on Identity, Non-Contradiction, or Excluded Middle;
5. any plausible $B$ or $N$ interpretation;
6. whether that interpretation reduces to superposition, contextuality, detector behavior,
   epistemic uncertainty, ordinary outcome refinement, or another bivalent account;
7. the verdict: FLL falsified, candidate unresolved, FLL survives this test, or orthogonal; and
8. confidence and source quality.

The ledger distinguishes theoretical impossibility results from laboratory observations.

## 10. Role of Rival Models

Rival-model construction remains useful but subordinate to the governing objective. Its purpose is
to sharpen falsification by asking:

> What physical observation would force us to conclude that $B$ or $N$ actually occurred?

A successful rival must produce a measurable prediction differing from every admissible bivalent
model. If it merely renames conventional outcomes, it has not supplied the required contrast. If
it produces a genuinely distinct likelihood, correlation, event structure, or physical record
that cannot be represented as bivalent actualization, it becomes experimentally significant.

The representation problem is an instrument of the falsification program. It is not the research
objective itself.
