# Action, Ontological Transition, and Dynamic Stability: Reconciliation Assessment

**Status:** Proposed research analysis; not an adopted hard-core revision.
**Date:** 2026-10-25
**Target:** TRT co-primitive A; candidate Dynamic Stability Principle (DSP).
**Provenance:** Reconciles the canonical September 29 normalization with the October 11 discussion of persistence and continuous actualization. Historical statements are retained as proposals, not retroactively declared proved.

## Verified canonical baseline

- `README.md`, `1-hypothesis/hard-core.md`, and `1-hypothesis/paper/TRT-v0.10.md` define A as primitive fundamental state transition/change, without presupposing physical time, motion, force, energy, work, or actuality.
- `05-work-packages/WP-TRT-CORE-0001-co-primitive-normalization/package.yaml` records the completed normalization.
- The typed architecture is R --(L3 necessary filter)--> D --(A)--> X/chi. L3 coherence is not proved sufficient for D.
- No claim of DSP's derivation is warranted from these canonical texts.

## The tension

The hard core sketches `A: s_i -> s_j` as transition between informationally distinguishable states. The later candidate DSP proposes that physically invariant configurations remain dependent on A. If `s_i = s_j` at the same ontological level and transition requires distinct relata, the persistence claim cannot be inferred from the transition definition. Calling physical stability 'continuous action' does not resolve the inference.

## Typed distinction proposed for investigation

Let O denote fundamental ontological states, P physical configurations, and pi: O -> P an observational/physical projection. A is a primitive relation on O. A nontrivial transition `A(o_i,o_j)` may satisfy `o_i != o_j` while `pi(o_i) = pi(o_j)`. Thus a physically invariant configuration can coexist with ontological change **if** such underlying distinct states and transitions exist. The projection construction demonstrates logical consistency, not their existence.

A distinct hypothesis is that the same ontological state requires ongoing grounding or sustaining action. That requires a *sustenance/grounding relation* G, or an explicitly broader definition of A. Neither follows solely from the strict transition predicate. Avoid assuming that ontological persistence must consist of an infinite sequence of distinct transitions.

## Candidate DSP and formal status

Candidate modal thesis: `□(Actual(x) -> DependsOnAction(x))`. The original shorthand `□(chi -> A)` is insufficiently typed: chi is a domain/status and A is a primitive relation, so the implication lacks clear predicates. The typed thesis remains a **conjecture**, with the accessibility relation and necessity modality unspecified.

Possible options:

1. **Strict-transition option:** preserve A as transition between distinct ontological states; formulate DSP conditionally on a demonstrable sequence of distinct underlying states. No unconditional persistence theorem.
2. **Broader-action option:** revise the primitive to include transition and ontological sustenance; this is a material hard-core change requiring a separate approved normalization and new irreducibility/circularity analysis.
3. **Grounding-relation option:** preserve A and posit a separate dependent grounding relation G; establish whether G is derivative of A rather than an unacknowledged fourth co-primitive.

**Recommendation:** retain the canonical strict-transition definition provisionally. Treat DSP as a tier-2 hypothesis and test option 1 first. Do not rewrite the hard core or promote DSP to an axiom without explicit approval and formal justification.

## Severe objections and proof obligations

1. **Identity:** define distinguishability at O and distinguish ontological identity from physical observational equivalence.
2. **Non-temporality:** specify ordering without embedding clock time in the definition of A; avoid presupposing the very temporal succession to be derived.
3. **Persistence:** prove or explicitly posit why a static physical configuration entails ongoing ontological transitions.
4. **Regress:** assess whether every sustaining transition requires another, and whether a well-founded or non-well-founded order is intended.
5. **Modal typing:** define the scope of □, Actual, DependsOnAction, and the status of chi.
6. **Irreducibility:** demonstrate that any sustaining role is neither a disguised definition of actuality nor reducible to L3 and I-infinity.
7. **Physical discrimination:** identify possible models and failure conditions; physical invariance alone cannot evidence hidden ontological transitions.
8. **Compatibility:** preserve R/D/X typing and the open L3-to-D sufficiency obligation.

## Acceptance criteria for future work

- Record a formal transition relation and observational projection with a countermodel showing that physical invariance alone does not entail ontological transition.
- Formalize each DSP variant with its assumptions explicitly listed; distinguish consistency proofs from ontological derivations.
- Conduct an adversarial review for circularity, temporal smuggling, and unfalsifiable accommodation.
- Only after review, propose bounded updates to paper, traceability, and Lean formalization, with CI evidence.
- Preserve `WP-TRT-CORE-0001` as completed historical normalization; do not silently revise it.
