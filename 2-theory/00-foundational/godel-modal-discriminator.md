# Gödel's universe as a TRT modal discriminator

**Work package:** WP-TRT-GMD-0001  
**Status:** Foundational worked case; does not alter the TRT hard core  
**Primary source confidence:** HIGH

## Purpose

Kurt Gödel's 1949 rotating cosmological solution provides a precise case for testing TRT's distinction among representability, logical admissibility, theory-relative admissibility, actualizability, and actuality.

Gödel exhibited an exact cosmological solution of Einstein's field equations with global causal structure permitting closed timelike curves. The result establishes that the Einstein field equations, with the matter content and cosmological constant of the model, do not by themselves exclude that causal structure. It does not establish that the Gödel universe is physically instantiated, that our universe has its global properties, or that a closed timelike curve is physically constructible in our universe.

Primary source: Gödel (1949), *An Example of a New Type of Cosmological Solutions of Einstein's Field Equations of Gravitation*, *Reviews of Modern Physics* 21, 447. DOI: 10.1103/RevModPhys.21.447.

## TRT mapping

Let \(g_G\) denote the Gödel spacetime specification.

### 1. Representability

\[
g_G \in R.
\]

The metric, source terms, and causal structure are mathematically specifiable. This establishes representability only.

### 2. L₃-admissibility

No contradiction follows merely from the presence of closed timelike curves. Subject to the usual mathematical treatment of the solution:

\[
\mathrm{Adm}_{L_3}(g_G).
\]

TRT already requires:

\[
x\in D \Rightarrow \mathrm{Adm}_{L_3}(x),
\]

while leaving the converse unproved.

### 3. Theory-relative admissibility

Introduce a theory-relative predicate rather than a new ontological domain:

\[
\mathrm{Adm}_{GR}(g_G).
\]

Here \(\mathrm{Adm}_{GR}(x)\) means that \(x\) satisfies the relevant equations and stipulated model conditions of general relativity. This is stronger than bare representability and distinct from TRT's metaphysical actualizability predicate.

Therefore:

\[
\mathrm{Adm}_{GR}(g_G) \nRightarrow g_G\in D.
\]

An exact solution belongs to a theory's solution space. Solutionhood alone does not prove that the represented state can be instantiated in reality.

### 4. Actualizability

Whether the complete Gödel state belongs to TRT's actualizable domain remains a separate question:

\[
g_G\in D;?
\]

TRT does not infer this from GR-admissibility.

### 5. Actuality

Actuality requires obtainment:

\[
g_G\in X;?
\]

Gödel's formal result does not answer this question. No inference from exact solutionhood to actuality is licensed.

## Why the proposed R₂a/R₂b ladder is not canonicalized

A pedagogical ladder

\[
R_0\to R_1\to R_{2a}\to R_{2b}\to R_3
\]

can usefully distinguish representability, coherence, law-admissibility, actualizability, and actuality. It becomes misleading if the arrows imply that every theory-admissible state advances toward metaphysical actualizability.

TRT therefore retains the canonical typed architecture:

\[
R \xrightarrow{L_3\;\text{necessary filter}} D \xrightarrow{A} X(\chi),
\]

and overlays theory-relative predicates on \(R\):

\[
x\in R,\qquad \mathrm{Adm}_{L_3}(x),\qquad \mathrm{Adm}_{T}(x).
\]

For Gödel:

\[
g_G\in R\land \mathrm{Adm}_{L_3}(g_G)\land \mathrm{Adm}_{GR}(g_G),
\]

while neither

\[
g_G\in D
\]

nor

\[
g_G\in X
\]

follows merely from those facts.

## Constraint does not entail instantiation

The case motivates the TRT principle:

\[
\boxed{\mathrm{Adm}_{T}(x)\nRightarrow \mathrm{Obtains}(x)}
\]

for a theory \(T\), absent an additional bridge principle.

This is the important result. Equations constrain a solution space. They do not, solely by being satisfied, select which member of that space obtains.

The same distinction protects the compact TRT identity:

\[
\boxed{\chi\equiv\mathsf{A}(I_\infty\mid L_3)}
\]

from being read as though constraint plus representability automatically generated actuality. The separate R-to-D-to-X architecture remains necessary because the sufficiency conditions for D are open and because obtainment is not identical to formal admissibility.

## Bearing on A

Gödel's case does not prove TRT's primitive \(A\). It does expose the explanatory question to which \(A\) is addressed.

A complete formal specification plus satisfaction of governing constraints does not entail that the specified state obtains. If a rival ontology can derive obtainment from state-space plus dynamics without presupposing transition or actuality, TRT's irreducibility claim for \(A\) faces a substantive challenge. Until such a reduction is supplied, the gap between admissibility and obtainment remains an explicit TRT burden rather than a hidden inference.

## Result

Gödel 1949 is adopted as a **modal discriminator**, not as evidence that time travel is actual.

It separates five questions:

1. Can the state be represented?
2. Is it logically admissible?
3. Does it satisfy a specified physical theory?
4. Is it genuinely actualizable?
5. Does it actually obtain?

TRT's architecture requires these questions to remain typed and non-equivalent.
