## 1. Research Context

The TRT Fundamental Laws of Logic Physical Model does not arise in a vacuum. Since the development
of quantum mechanics, physicists, mathematicians, and philosophers of physics have repeatedly
investigated whether quantum phenomena require departures from classical logical structure. Some
proposals modify the algebra of propositions. Others reject predetermined bivalent valuation,
introduce additional truth values, weaken Excluded Middle, or permit formally contradictory
properties.

This history is directly relevant because TRT proposes a physical constraint:

$$
\boxed{\text{No irreducibly non-bivalent informational state can actualize in physical reality.}}
$$

Within the model,

$$
\chi\equiv\mathcal A(I\mid L),
$$

where $L$ is Logic as the primitive global physical constraint on admissibility, $I$ is the
informational state, $\mathcal A$ is Primitive Action, and $\chi$ is physical Actualization. The
corresponding prediction is

$$
\boxed{V(P_\chi)\in\{T,F\}}
$$

for every proposition $P$ instantiated by a completed physical informational actualization $\chi$,
holding proposition, context, time, and relevant respect fixed. This is the physical hypothesis and
proposed physical principle.
Its significance becomes clearer against nearly a century of attempts to accommodate nonclassical
logical structures within physical theory.

## 2. Birkhoff and von Neumann: Non-Boolean Quantum Propositions

Birkhoff and von Neumann's 1936 paper, *The Logic of Quantum Mechanics*, asked what logical
structure belongs to physical theories that do not conform to classical logic. Their calculus
models experimental propositions with linear subspaces, conjunction with intersection, disjunction
with closed linear span, and negation with orthogonal complement. The resulting propositional
structure is non-Boolean; unrestricted classical distributivity fails.

That is a foundational challenge to classical propositional organization, but it is not an
experimental discovery of a physical contradiction or truth-value gap. Orthocomplementation still
gives the structural analogues

$$
P\wedge P^\perp=\bot,
\qquad
P\vee P^\perp=\top.
$$

The departure lies especially in distributivity, compatibility, and the organization of
experimental propositions. The adversarial lesson for TRT is exact:

> A physical theory can possess profoundly nonclassical propositional structure without thereby
> demonstrating non-bivalent physical actualization.

## 3. Kochen--Specker: Global Noncontextual Valuation Fails

Kochen and Specker proved that, under the theorem's assumptions, quantum observables cannot all be
assigned globally consistent, context-independent predetermined values satisfying the required
functional relationships. This rules out a stronger global valuation structure; it does not
mathematically or experimentally establish

$$
V(P_\chi)=B
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
V(P_\chi)=N
$$

for a completed physical actualization. TRT therefore must not require every quantum proposition
to possess a predetermined bivalent value before measurement. Its physical prediction begins at
actualization.

The original article is conventionally dated 1967 in *Journal of Mathematics and Mechanics* 17,
59--87. The current Indiana journal archive describes volume 17, issue 1 as 1968; this is a
bibliographic archive discrepancy, not a scientific ambiguity.

## 4. Many-Valued Quantum Mechanics

Pykacz represents physically sound Birkhoff--von Neumann quantum logics as a particular
infinite-valued Łukasiewicz logic. On this account, Born probabilities can serve as truth values for
propositions about future, not-yet-completed quantum experiments:

$$
V(P_{\mathrm{pre}\text{-}\chi})\in[0,1].
$$

This comes close to the TRT boundary, but Pykacz explicitly distinguishes the completed case: after
an experimentally checked dichotomic proposition is resolved, it occurs as true or false. His 2019
online article, published in volume 60 in 2021, describes this as a collapse of the many-valued
logic of a future event onto a two-valued statement about the completed event.

TRT does not presently require $V(P_{\mathrm{pre}\text{-}\chi})\in\{T,F\}$. It predicts

$$
\boxed{V(P_\chi)\in\{T,F\}.}
$$

Many-valued quantum logic can therefore characterize informational potential before actualization
without, by that fact alone, falsifying TRT. A falsification requires an intermediate or otherwise
non-bivalent valuation itself to survive physical actualization.

## 5. Three-Valued and Indeterminate Quantum Propositions

Three-valued approaches use $\{T,I,F\}$, with $I$ an indeterminate proposition. Reichenbach is a
historical antecedent. A verified contemporary example, Takagi (2021),
defines $I$ for probabilities strictly between zero and one and separately derives that a completed
projective observation yields truth value 0 or 1.

The governing adversarial question is not whether an indeterminate semantics can be formulated. It
is:

> Does $I$ characterize an unresolved proposition concerning a possible future measurement, or
> does physical measurement itself actualize an irreducibly indeterminate informational state?

The former is compatible with the TRT-FLL exclusion rule. The latter is a candidate falsifier.

## 6. Paraconsistent Quantum Mechanics: The Strongest Direct Challenge

Da Costa and de Ronde propose a paraconsistent interpretation of quantum superpositions, explicitly
seeking a formal account in which contradictory properties are not removed at the outset. If a
superposition

$$
|\psi\rangle=\alpha|P\rangle+\beta|\neg P\rangle
$$

were physically demonstrated to instantiate $P\wedge\neg P$ in the same actualization, context,
time, and respect, TRT-FLL would face exactly the kind of counterexample it forbids.

The challenge cannot be dismissed by stipulation; it must be separated experimentally into four
possibilities:

1. contradiction in the mathematical representation of a premeasurement state;
2. contradiction in the semantics assigned to that representation;
3. incompatible or co-present potential measurement outcomes; or
4. contradiction in the informational state that physically actualizes.

Only the fourth is the TRT-FLL falsifier. This makes the paraconsistent programme one of the
strongest adversarial programmes for the model. Its present published boundary is also precise:
the 2013 paper calls itself an outline and first step, while the 2015 development formalizes
contradictory powers in a **potential** realm. Neither source derives a distinct post-actualization
record likelihood.

## 7. The Actualization Boundary

Quantum theory has forced abandonment or qualification of global Boolean propositional structure,
noncontextual predetermined valuation, locality under Bell-type assumptions, and several forms of
classical realism. Many-valued approaches permit intermediate values for quantum possibilities;
paraconsistent approaches investigate contradiction in superposition.

Across these distinct programmes, a recurring empirical pattern motivates—but does not establish—
the TRT-FLL physical model:

$$
\text{nonclassical quantum state}
\longrightarrow
\text{measurement}
\longrightarrow
\text{definite physical record}.
$$

The proposed boundary is

$$
\boxed{\text{informational potential may exhibit nonclassical structure}}
$$

while

$$
\boxed{\text{physical informational actualization is bivalent}.}
$$

TRT proposes an ontological explanation:

$$
\chi\equiv\mathcal A(I\mid L),
\qquad
I_{\mathrm{non\text{-}bivalent}}\notin\Omega_{\mathrm{actualizable}}.
$$

This is the proposed physical exclusion principle.

## 8. What Would Falsify It?

None of the following is sufficient by itself:

- a superposition;
- a probability strictly between zero and one;
- failure of global noncontextual valuation;
- violation of a Bell inequality;
- a mathematical logic permitting contradictions; or
- a semantic interpretation describing a quantum state as contradictory.

The decisive result is

$$
\exists P_\chi:\quad V(P_\chi)\in\{B,N\},
$$

where

$$
B=P\wedge\neg P
$$

is an irreducible informational glut, while $N$ is the valuation in which neither $P$ nor $\neg P$
obtains. The $N$ notation denotes a truth-value gap, not the classical proposition
$\neg P\wedge\neg(\neg P)$ evaluated inside the very bivalent calculus under test.

Proposition identity, context, time, and relevant respect must be controlled. The result must
survive reduction to detector inefficiency, ambiguous measurement, distinct contexts, different
propositions, sequential state change, incomplete observation, ordinary additional outcomes,
epistemic uncertainty, or semantic relabeling. If it survives, TRT-FLL is falsified. One verified
case is sufficient.

## 9. Why the Historical Record Matters

The historical development of quantum foundations supplies an adversarial test environment, not
mere background. Birkhoff and von Neumann challenged Boolean structure. Kochen--Specker excluded a
broad class of global noncontextual valuations. Bell-type results excluded local hidden-variable
structures under their assumptions. Many-valued quantum logics relaxed bivalence for unperformed
measurements. Three-valued approaches introduced indeterminacy. Paraconsistent approaches explored
contradiction within quantum superposition.

These programmes differ substantially and must not be conflated. Together they establish that the
logical structure of physical theory has been subjected to sustained theoretical and experimental
pressure. TRT-FLL enters that contest with a narrower prediction. It does not restore Boolean
structure throughout quantum theory, hidden variables, predetermined outcomes, locality, or
noncontextuality. It predicts:

$$
\boxed{\text{Whatever informational structure precedes actualization,}\quad
V(P_\chi)\in\{T,F\}.}
$$

## 10. Candidate New Physical Principle

The prospective novelty is not that the FLL govern a formal language. TRT assigns them a primitive
physical role in $\chi\equiv\mathcal A(I\mid L)$, where $L$ constrains physical admissibility.
Bivalent actualization is therefore predicted to constrain what physical information can become
actual, not merely how an observer describes a record.

> **Fundamental Logical Admissibility Principle:** No informational state violating Identity,
> Non-Contradiction, or Excluded Middle with respect to the same proposition, context, time, and
> relevant respect can become physically actual.

Its consequence and falsifier are

$$
\forall P_\chi,\quad V(P_\chi)\in\{T,F\},
$$

$$
\exists P_\chi,\quad V(P_\chi)\in\{B,N\}.
$$

This is a proposed physical constraint, not merely a philosophical assertion about logic.

## 11. Research Consequence

The immediate research objective is to identify the strongest theoretical and experimental
challenges to bivalent physical actualization and apply the same criterion to each. Priority cases
include paraconsistent superposition, many-valued and intuitionistic quantum-logical approaches,
Kochen--Specker and contextuality, Bell and GHZ, Leggett--Garg, weak measurement,
Wigner's-friend-type experiments, indefinite causal order, delayed choice, and any experimental
proposal explicitly claiming contradictory or truth-value-indeterminate physical states.

The governing question for every ledger entry is:

> Did the experiment produce an irreducibly non-bivalent physical informational actualization?

If yes, TRT-FLL fails. If no, the experiment may still have falsified another classical assumption
while leaving this physical constraint intact. This distinction governs the research programme.

### Candidate first frontier: Wigner's friend and Local Friendliness

This line is unusually relevant because it pressures the status of an already recorded event, not
only an unperformed measurement. It is not yet a TRT-FLL experiment. The Local-Friendliness result
rejects a conjunction including No-Superdeterminism, Locality, and Absoluteness of Observed Events;
its proof-of-principle experiment uses a photon's path as the “observer.” The gating TRT question is:

> Does TRT require Absoluteness of Observed Events across all observers, or only bivalence relative
> to one well-defined physical actualization context?

The latter cannot become an escape by equivocation: proposition identity and the relation between
contexts must be specified. [`WP-TRT-FLL-0002`](../../05-work-packages/WP-TRT-FLL-0002-adversarial-operational-protocol/package.yaml)
will assess this frontier before choosing an apparatus.

## 12. Present Status and Intended Law Claim

TRT-FLL is presently a physical hypothesis and proposed physical principle. Its intended law
content is a universal constraint on physically actualizable informational states:

$$
\boxed{\forall P_\chi,\quad V(P_\chi)\in\{T,F\}.}
$$

Equivalently,

$$
\boxed{I_{\mathrm{non\text{-}bivalent}}\notin\Omega_{\mathrm{actualizable}}.}
$$

This is not a metaphysical assertion that reality ought to conform to logical principles. It is a
claim about what physical reality can and cannot instantiate. The law is exposed to falsification:

$$
\boxed{
\exists P_\chi:\ V(P_\chi)\in\{B,N\}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mathrm{TRT\text{-}FLL\ is\ false}.
}
$$

One verified irreducibly non-bivalent informational actualization is sufficient. The task is not
to establish the law by definition, but to subject the exclusion principle to the strongest
available theoretical and experimental attempts at falsification.

Quantum foundations supplies an unusually mature adversarial environment. Its results must be
accepted at full strength. The narrower question is whether physical reality has been demonstrated
to actualize an informational state that irreducibly violates Identity, Non-Contradiction, or
Excluded Middle. If yes, the hypothesis is false. Purpose-built negative tests capable of exposing
such states can increase its empirical corroboration without becoming deductive proof. Historical
experiments that constrain nearby rival structures leave TRT-FLL unfalsified but do not
automatically corroborate it.

Accordingly, the status is

$$
\boxed{\text{PHYSICAL HYPOTHESIS / PROPOSED PHYSICAL PRINCIPLE}}
$$

with

$$
\boxed{\text{EXPLICIT PHYSICAL FALSIFIER}}
$$

and

$$
\boxed{\text{OPERATIONAL LAW STATUS NOT YET EARNED}.}
$$

Within $\chi\equiv\mathcal A(I\mid L)$, $L$ participates directly in determining admissible
physical actualization. The resulting law is the Fundamental Logical Admissibility Principle:

> No informational state violating Identity, Non-Contradiction, or Excluded Middle with respect
> to the same proposition, context, time, and relevant respect can become physically actual.

The scientific obligation is straightforward: **try to break it**. A properly discriminating
negative test adds empirical support in proportion to its severity. If the hypothesis is broken
once under controlled and reproducible conditions, it is false. Only after the contrastive
operational obligation is met and severe tests survive should law status be advanced.
