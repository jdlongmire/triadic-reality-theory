### Abstract

The success of rational inquiry involves more than regularities in a mind-independent world and more than statistical sensitivity of cognitive systems to those regularities. Finite agents recover structure from the world, form representations that purport to be about it, distinguish true from false representations, reason under norms that distinguish valid from invalid inference, and sometimes use abstract mathematical theories to predict previously unobserved phenomena. This paper formulates the Intelligible Information Argument (IIA) as an abductive argument from that joint source-receiver correspondence. It distinguishes stable world-structure, Shannon/statistical information, semantic content, truth, rational normativity, and knowledge, treating transitions among them as explanatory bridges rather than logical entailments. The argument proposes a common rational ground as a candidate explanation of both source-side intelligibility and receiver-side rational adequacy. It does not infer an intentional transmitter from communication theory, derive semantics from Shannon information, or purport to deduce theism. Its force depends on comparative explanatory performance against serious alternatives.

**Keywords:** intelligibility; information; semantic information; epistemology; rationality; normativity; philosophy of information; philosophy of science; abductive inference

## 1. The phenomenon to be explained

Scientific inquiry presupposes a remarkable conjunction. A world that exists independently of any particular human investigator exhibits stable regularities. Finite agents can interact with that world in ways that preserve information about its states. Those agents form representations that purport to describe objects, events, relations, and laws beyond the representations themselves. Their representations can be correct or incorrect. Their inferences can be valid or invalid, warranted or unwarranted. In mature inquiry, abstract mathematical structures can support successful predictions concerning phenomena not among the observations from which the relevant theories were initially constructed.

Each component can be considered separately. The philosophical pressure arises from their conjunction.

Let X comprise stable and tractable structure in a mind-independent world; recoverability of that structure by finite receivers; representation about that world; truth and error with respect to that world; rationally norm-governed transformation of representations; and successful extension to novel prediction. IIA asks what kind of ontology best explains X as a whole.

Wigner (1960) identified the surprising effectiveness of mathematics in physical science. Polkinghorne (1991) later framed a theological response in terms of the fit between “reason within” and “reason without.” Shannon (1948) explicitly excluded semantic aspects from the engineering problem addressed by mathematical communication theory. Bar-Hillel and Carnap (1953) warned against transferring statistical-information results into semantic and pragmatic domains without justification. Floridi (2005) represents a later truth-centered treatment of semantic information. IIA accepts these boundaries.

The proposed contribution is a synthesis: treat source-side intelligibility and receiver-side epistemic adequacy as one explanandum while retaining explicit type boundaries between statistical dependence, semantic content, truth, rational normativity, and knowledge.

## 2. Terms before inference

Let W denote a mind-independent world or target domain; S(W), stable discoverable structure instantiated by W; R, a finite rational receiver; I_S(W;R), Shannon/statistical information available through lawful dependence between W and states accessible to R; C_R(W), representational content in R that purports to be about W; T(C_R,W), the truth relation between content and W; N_R, rational or epistemic norms governing inference; and K_R(W), knowledge of W acquired by R.

The architecture is:

    W -> S(W) -> I_S(W;R) -> C_R(W) -> T/N -> K_R(W)

The arrows mark explanatory transitions, not entailments. Likewise:

    I_S != C != T != N != K

This non-identity constraint does not establish ontological independence. Physicalist, functionalist, emergentist, dualist, idealist, and other ontologies may propose realization relations among these layers. The burden is to specify the bridge.

A physical system can covary with another without thereby possessing a proposition about it. A representation can possess content while being false. A true representation can be acquired accidentally or without warrant. A causal transition between representations can occur without constituting a valid inference. These concepts perform different explanatory work.

## 3. The Shannon boundary

Shannon's mathematical theory concerns selection, transmission, and recovery of messages under probabilistic constraints. Shannon explicitly notes that messages often have meaning while declaring semantic aspects irrelevant to the engineering problem he formalizes (Shannon, 1948, pp. 379-380). This restriction prevents a mathematical measure of uncertainty or channel capacity from silently becoming a theory of meaning.

Bar-Hillel and Carnap (1953) sharpen the boundary. Statistical information is determined without regard to what symbols designate. Semantic information introduces content. Nothing in Shannon theory licenses the chain “information -> communication -> transmitter -> mind -> God.”

IIA begins after this prohibition is accepted. The question is how a complete ontology accounts for a world whose structure is statistically recoverable and receivers whose interaction with that structure can become semantic, truth-assessable, rationally governed cognition.

## 4. Source and receiver without an illicit transmitter

Systems vocabulary can clarify the problem if its limits remain explicit:

    source domain W
      -> state-sensitive causal relation
      -> receiver R
      -> representation C
      -> inferential transformation N
      -> external validation
      -> warranted output or correction

“Source” means the domain about which information is acquired. It does not mean an intentional encoder. “Receiver” means a system whose accessible states vary with source states and which can use that dependence.

Receiver adequacy is multidimensional: statistical sensitivity, generalization, semantic aboutness, truth tracking, and rationally governed inference. No premise states that statistical sensitivity entails the remaining properties. The relevant correspondence is tractable(W) AND adequate(R,W).

## 5. Observations and bridge questions

IIA begins with six observations: mind-independent domains exhibit stable regularities; finite agents recover information about some of them; agents form representations of states beyond immediate sensory contact; representations can succeed or fail with respect to external states; rational evaluation distinguishes valid from invalid inference and warranted from unwarranted belief; and mathematical/theoretical representations sometimes enable successful novel prediction.

These generate six bridge questions. What makes W recoverable by R? What makes an internal state about W rather than merely correlated with it? What grounds truth conditions? What grounds rational normativity? What makes receiver processes truth-tracking rather than merely behaviorally useful? Why is the source domain sufficiently stable and tractable for these processes to succeed?

An adequate worldview may take some targets as primitive. IIA requires that stopping points be visible and compared symmetrically.

## 6. The minimal Intelligible Information Argument

**P1.** The conjunction X represented by the preceding observations obtains to a substantial degree.

**P2.** X contains multiple typed explanatory transitions. Statistical dependence alone does not entail semantic content, truth, rational normativity, or knowledge without additional bridge principles.

**P3.** A worldview-level explanation of X should account for both source-side intelligibility and receiver-side rational and semantic adequacy, while identifying what it takes as primitive.

**P4.** A rational-ground hypothesis, H_RG, proposes that source intelligibility and receiver rationality share an ultimate rational provenance.

**P5.** H_RG is evidentially favored by X only to the extent that, compared with serious alternatives, it explains more of X with equal or lower unsupported explanatory cost and does not merely relocate the unexplained features into the rational ground.

**C.** Therefore X constitutes abductive evidence for H_RG only in proportion to H_RG's comparative explanatory performance.

The conclusion is intentionally conditional. The argument identifies a candidate explanatory advantage and a burden of proof. It does not secure that advantage by definition.

## 7. Why a common rational ground is a live hypothesis

H_RG proposes a single provenance relation for two otherwise separable facts: reality is rationally tractable and rational agents are capable of tracking that reality. Its potential virtue is explanatory integration.

Polkinghorne (1991) proposes that external intelligibility and internal rationality are linked by common origin in divine Rationality. IIA therefore makes no priority claim for the common-origin idea. Its narrower contribution is analytical: the common-origin proposal is placed after the Shannon boundary and evaluated across a typed source-to-knowledge architecture.

“Reality and mind are rational because their ground is rational” is explanatory only if rational provenance makes features of both source and receiver less surprising, constrains what should be expected, or reduces independent primitives. Otherwise H_RG is a redescription.

## 8. Serious alternatives

### 8.1 Structural realism

A structural realist can maintain that objective structure is a central feature of reality and that successful theories increasingly capture it. This directly addresses much of the source-model fit. IIA cannot dismiss this by asking who “sent” the structure.

Residual questions concern accessibility of structure to rational agents, semantic representation, truth, and normativity. A structural realist may add accounts of each or regard some as primitive. The comparison is between explanatory packages.

### 8.2 Evolutionary reliabilism and naturalistic semantics

Evolution supplies a serious account of why organisms become sensitive to environmentally relevant regularities. Learning and cultural accumulation extend those capacities. Teleosemantic theories attempt to ground content in biological function and history. These proposals directly address receiver adequacy.

The narrower pressure point is whether these mechanisms jointly explain semantic aboutness, error, truth tracking, rational normativity, and abstract theoretical generalization, and whether source-side mathematical tractability is explained or properly treated as background.

This differs from Plantinga's evolutionary argument against naturalism. Plantinga (1993) challenges the rational compatibility of naturalism and confidence in cognitive reliability. IIA does not require the claim that evolution under naturalism makes reliable cognition improbable. It asks comparatively how an entire ontology explains both the tractable world and the truth-assessable receiver.

### 8.3 Computational functionalism and emergence

A sophisticated emergentist may hold that appropriately organized physical systems realize representational and rational properties through causal and functional organization. The fact that individual neurons lack beliefs does not establish that neural systems cannot instantiate beliefs.

The appropriate tests concern mechanism, content, truth conditions, misrepresentation, normativity, generalization, and causal relevance. If a functional account supplies these bridges, IIA must credit it. If “emergence” merely labels the appearance of the target properties, it has not yet explained them. The same standard applies to “rational ground.”

### 8.4 Hybrid naturalism and brute fact

A naturalistic account need not unify X under one mechanism. Physical theory may explain source regularity, evolutionary theory receiver sensitivity, teleosemantics content, functionalism cognition, and social epistemology scientific correction. Plural explanation is not automatically a defect. Unification counts for H_RG only where it yields explanatory constraint rather than verbal economy.

The correspondence may also be a brute fact. An abductive argument cannot deductively eliminate that possibility. It can only expose the stopping point and compare its explanatory cost with competing stopping points.

## 9. Selection effects

Observer-selection reasoning reduces one form of surprise. Observers capable of asking about intelligibility necessarily occupy environments compatible with their existence and cognitive activity.

This does not by itself provide an ontology of semantic content, truth, rational normativity, or mathematical tractability. Conversely, IIA cannot treat observer-world fit as surprising without conditioning on the existence and capacities of observers. The same evidence must be compared under each hypothesis.

## 10. Inferential ceiling

Even if H_RG ultimately outperforms its competitors, the immediate conclusion is limited.

    rational ground
      -> mind
      -> personal agency
      -> necessity / aseity
      -> classical theism
      -> Christian revelation

Each arrow requires additional argument. A common rational ground is not yet a personal mind. A mind is not yet a necessary being. A necessary personal rational ground is not yet the complete God of classical theism. Classical theism does not by itself establish specifically Christian revelation.

A Christian metaphysic can supply further claims from independent arguments and revelation. They cannot be imported into IIA's premises if IIA is to remain independently assessable.

## 11. Defeaters and downgrade conditions

IIA should be weakened or abandoned if a successful reductive theory shows that the typed distinctions do no explanatory work beyond a single lower-level variable; if a naturalistic package accounts for source tractability, semantic content, truth tracking, rational normativity, and receiver reliability with no greater primitive burden than H_RG; if H_RG merely restates that rationality exists at both ends; if proper selection conditioning makes the correspondence no more expected under H_RG than under a competitor; or if the argument depends on an equivocation between statistical and semantic information.

These are substantive vulnerabilities and form part of the research programme.

## 12. Relation to prior arguments

IIA is best classified at present as a distinct synthesis rather than a demonstrated new argument.

Shannon (1948) supplies the quantitative boundary. Bar-Hillel and Carnap (1953) make explicit the danger of importing statistical information into semantic contexts. Wigner (1960) supplies the classic source-side problem of mathematical effectiveness. Dretske (1981) develops an information-theoretic epistemology connecting source, channel, receiver, and knowledge. Polkinghorne (1991) directly anticipates the theological common-origin explanation of external and internal rationality. Plantinga (1993) develops a different challenge concerning cognitive reliability under evolutionary naturalism. Floridi (2005) explicitly connects semantic information with truth.

No originality claim is made here. The candidate contribution is the explicit integration of these concerns into a typed, two-ended abductive framework whose central explanandum is the conjunction of source tractability and receiver semantic/rational adequacy.

## 13. Conclusion

The deepest fact behind successful inquiry is not merely that the world contains patterns, nor merely that organisms react to them. Rational inquiry occurs because a structured world is accessible to finite receivers in ways that support content, truth, error, inference, correction, and prediction. That entire relation is the explanandum.

IIA does not turn information theory into theology. It marks the point at which information theory ceases to answer the relevant question. Statistical recoverability is indispensable to many forms of empirical cognition, but it is not by itself semantic content, truth, rational normativity, or knowledge.

A common rational ground is a serious candidate explanation because it addresses both ends of the correspondence under one provenance claim. Its merit cannot be assumed. It must be earned by comparison with the strongest alternatives and by showing that rational provenance explains rather than renames the phenomenon.

The foundational argument therefore ends conditionally: if common rational provenance yields greater explanatory integration of source intelligibility and receiver rational adequacy without greater unsupported cost, X is evidence for H_RG. If it does not, IIA fails to establish its intended abductive preference.

That is the proper burden of the argument.
