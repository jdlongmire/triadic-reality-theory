**Status:** Research review, noncanonical; author’s derivation not publicly available.

**Reviewer standpoint:** TRT canonical hard core, not an assumption that TRT has been proved.  
**Source:** [filed primary text](paper.md); [original publication](https://struever-institute-for-configurational-science.alexstruever.com/science-of-stability/).  
**Scope:** All 273 source lines audited below. Line numbers are Markdown-file lines, not original webpage pagination. This is a textual audit of the published exposition, not verification of the undisclosed mathematical kernel.

## Executive judgment
The paper articulates a serious methodology for separating ontological, configurational, and evidential questions, and a practical governance architecture for evidence-qualified execution. Its strongest contributions are the insistence on identifiable premises, preservation of measured-entity identity, path-wise feasibility, and remeasurement after execution. Its strongest foundational claims remain **unverified**: the unique necessity of a monoprimitive, the deduction of distinction/binding/propagation/relation from Capacity, and completed ontological closure. The article explicitly withholds the formal closure expression and derivation (lines 91, 107–115, 242–245). Therefore the correct verdict is **insufficient publicly disclosed evidence**, not proof of falsity or demonstrated circularity.

## Key formal challenges

### F1. Monoprimitive requirement risks begging the question (lines 47–63)
The conditional argument assumes a complete connected foundation must derive every relation without any additional independent ontological condition. That is a substantive *criterion*, not a theorem forcing one primitive. A model can posit two jointly necessary co-primitives with a constitutive relation or a structural law. A relational axiom may be irreducible without being an additional *entity*. The paper has not shown that relational primitive structure is incoherent, or that deriving a relation from A and B entails A or B loses primitive status. **Finding:** underjustified uniqueness. **Confidence:** HIGH for textual limitation; UNCERTAIN for full unpublished proof.

### F2. Capacity-to-distinction (lines 65–79)
The definition of Capacity as a condition for the *possibility* and limitation of configuration does not entail the existence of an actual distinction. A countermodel with an undifferentiated capacity satisfies the stated modal description absent an additional differentiation principle. The article offers explanatory definitions and a proposed order, not a public formal entailment. **Finding:** deduction not shown. **Confidence:** HIGH.

### F3. Capacity-to-Action and propagation (lines 73–77, 101–115)
**Capacity without Action is inert** is a compact objection when *Capacity* is construed as modal ability. Formally, `◇T` does not entail `T`; `Permitted(T)` does not entail `Occurs(T)`. Even `CanPropagate(C)` does not imply `Propagates(C)`. If the primitive is defined as already dynamically active, the dynamical commitment is included by stipulation and its independent necessity remains to be defended. This establishes an **unclosed inferential gap in the published exposition**; it does **not** prove the hidden kernel is circular. The author’s own language often concerns *admissible transformation*, which must not be conflated with actual occurrence. **Finding:** major open proof obligation. **Confidence:** HIGH for gap in published article; UNCERTAIN for hidden proof.

### F4. Closure is defined but not demonstrated (lines 81–99, 101–117, 242–245)
The claim that every necessary condition has been derived cannot be independently checked without the kernel, a complete premise inventory, inference rules, a proof of completeness, and an explicit noncircular dependency graph. The expository `Capacity ⊢ Distinction ⊢ Binding ⊢ Propagation ⊢ Relation` is expressly *not* that proof. **Finding:** closure claimed, not auditable. **Confidence:** HIGH.

### F5. Ontological possibility versus actualizability (lines 18–31, 65–79, 101–117)
The paper distinguishes possibility, structural admissibility, and evidence-qualified execution. It does not show that a possible configuration obtains, or that an authorized candidate causes the physical successor. Avoid reading his engineering `permission` as metaphysical actualization. **Finding:** typed transitions require more precision. **Confidence:** HIGH.

### F6. Engineering contributions are independently valuable (lines 119–210)
The domain-mapping schema, observability principle, necessary-premise evidencing, qualified execution boundary, feedback, and conditional disturbance analysis provide a coherent engineering programme. They resemble assurance-case and safety-control disciplines; their practical validity is independent of whether Capacity is the unique ontological primitive. The paper cites no detailed reproducible test dataset, device protocol, or trace in the filed text. **Finding:** constructive methodology, empirical claims not reproducible from article alone. **Confidence:** HIGH.

### F7. Critique of physics is methodologically sound but nonexclusive (lines 211–226)
Empirical adequacy does not uniquely identify ontology. The same underdetermination applies to Science of Stability. A proposed replacement needs formal derivations and discriminating predictions, rather than philosophical superiority alone. **Finding:** valid general caution; no demonstrated displacement of current physics. **Confidence:** HIGH.

### F8. Testability and falsification (lines 227–257)
The four verification layers are well differentiated. However, when the foundational kernel is unavailable, external investigators cannot falsify or corroborate its alleged deductive sufficiency. Failure of one mapped engineering system may implicate mapping rather than ontology. Specify at least one *foundational* failure condition, alongside reproducible domain-level tests. **Finding:** promising audit taxonomy, incomplete public falsifiability. **Confidence:** HIGH.

## Strongest charitable reconstruction
1. Every actual configuration is bounded and distinguishable.
2. Distinguishable configurations require capacity for limitation and relations.
3. A complete ontology should explain binding and propagation rather than assume them.
4. A single sufficiently rich primitive might generate these features.
5. Conditional evidence-bound transitions can be engineered from a qualified domain mapping.

Steps 1–3 are intelligible motivations. Step 4 is a conjectural sufficiency/uniqueness claim in the public paper. Step 5 is a separate engineering programme and cannot retroactively establish step 4.

## TRT comparison and symmetrical burdens
Canonical TRT: `TRT = <L₃, I∞, A>`; `χ ≡ 𝔄(I∞ | L₃)`; representable R, actualizable D, actual X; `D ⊆ R`, and `x∈D ⇒ Adm_L₃(x)`, not the converse. L₃ constrains, I∞ supplies representable differentiation, A supplies primitive non-temporal state transition. TRT **argues** these are irreducible; it has not completed every formal or physical proof.

- **Convergence:** distinction between representability and realization; successor-state reasoning; derivative temporality; anti-conflation of mathematics with physical evidence; severe scrutiny of premises.
- **Difference:** Strüver seeks reduction to one Capacity primitive; TRT treats logical constraint, representability, and Action as irreducible co-primitives.
- **Fairness:** TRT must justify why co-primitives and their co-requirement are necessary and sufficient for determinate actuality; the possible need for further actualizability conditions remains open. TRT cannot declare victory merely because Strüver withholds a proof.
- **Key discriminator:** produce an explicit entailment from capacity-as-possibility to *actual* transition, without adding a dynamical axiom or embedding transition in the definition.

## Questions to submit to Strüver
1. What is the exact definition of Capacity, with its type, modal status, and identity conditions?
2. Which axioms and inference rules derive the first actual distinction from Capacity?
3. Which theorem derives **occurring transition**, as opposed to its permission or possibility?
4. Can a static model satisfy the Capacity axioms? If not, which axiom excludes it?
5. Why is an irreducible relation among co-primitives logically inadmissible rather than simply explanatorily disfavored?
6. Does the completeness proof quantify over all ontological conditions or only those included in the proposed ontology?
7. What independently checkable predictions distinguish the framework from conventional domain-specific assurance/control systems?
8. Can a proof certificate, dependency graph, or independently auditable redacted derivation be released without disclosing protected intellectual property?
