## Abstract

Alexander Wolfgang Strüver's *Science of Stability and Ontological Closure* proposes a reduction of the necessary conditions of physical realizability to a single primitive, *Capacity*. The account claims to derive distinction, binding, propagation, and relation, and to have completed an Ontological Closure Condition that subsequently governs domain-specific mappings and evidence-qualified transitions. This paper evaluates the published exposition using premise reconstruction, modal countermodels, an audit of claimed entailments, and a comparative analysis with Triadic Reality Theory (TRT). Three results emerge. First, Strüver's argument for a uniquely sufficient monoprimitive depends on a methodological criterion that has not independently excluded irreducible relational or co-primitive foundations. Second, the modal availability of differentiation or propagation does not entail an occurring distinction or transition. A capacity understood solely as a dispositional condition leaves the occurrence of any transition unexplained. This modal gap does not by itself prove that a distinct Action primitive is the only possible remedy. Third, the published article expressly omits the mathematical closure condition and its derivation, preventing independent evaluation of the claimed completeness theorem. The article nevertheless offers substantive engineering principles concerning entity-evidence binding, path-dependent feasibility, execution authorization, and post-execution verification. The resulting judgment is conditional: Strüver presents a coherent research agenda and potentially useful applied architecture, while the publicly available argument does not yet warrant its strongest foundational conclusions. TRT is presented as a rival candidate with its own outstanding necessity and sufficiency obligations.

## 1. Introduction

The difference between explaining what a physical system does and identifying what must obtain for any determinate physical system to exist is a legitimate philosophical distinction. Strüver (2026, Research Section 01) foregrounds this distinction and proposes that the necessary conditions of configurational realizability can be deduced from one fundamental *Capacity*. The stated programme is unusually ambitious: a completed ontology would supply invariant structural conditions for otherwise open-ended domain mappings, spanning robotics, enterprise operations, and other configurations.

The appropriate critical question is not whether a single-primitive ontology is conceivable. It is whether the particular reduction has been established by an argument whose premises and inferential rules can be inspected. The article explicitly says its mathematical closure expression and full derivation are not reproduced (Strüver, 2026, Research Sections 05, 06 and 13). Consequently, the object of this review is the **publicly disclosed argument**, not the undisclosed derivation, and its findings must not be misrepresented as a disproof of that unavailable work.

The inquiry is conducted from the standpoint of Triadic Reality Theory (TRT), a competing research programme that argues for the joint irreducibility of ontological logical constraint, unbounded informational representability, and fundamental Action (Longmire, 2026a). This standpoint motivates several questions but does not confer a presumption of correctness on TRT. A symmetrical critique must expose where TRT itself remains underdetermined.

This paper distinguishes: (i) definition, (ii) stipulation, (iii) logically valid consequence, (iv) metaphysical necessity claim, (v) empirical claim, and (vi) engineering design recommendation. Confusion among these categories is especially costly when the object of inquiry is ontological completeness.

## 3. Strüver's foundational proposal reconstructed

Strüver states a three-part methodological completeness criterion: irreducibility, deductive sufficiency, and realizability (2026, Research Section 02). He argues that two independent primitives, if they contribute to a connected reality, require a relation. If this relation is independent, the foundation is incomplete; if derived, their primitive status warrants reconsideration; if absent, the two do not form a connected interacting foundation (Research Section 03). He concludes, *within his method*, that the admissible foundation must reduce to one sufficient starting condition.

That condition is named **Capacity**, described as the irreducible condition from which the possibility and limitation of configuration can be derived (Research Section 04). The publicly displayed dependency sequence is:

**Equation (1)**

$$
\mathrm{Capacity} \vdash \mathrm{Distinction} \vdash \mathrm{Binding} \vdash \mathrm{Propagation} \vdash \mathrm{Relation}
$$

The article explicitly labels this sequence *expository*, not the formal kernel (Research Section 06). It defines ontological closure as accounting for all independent conditions necessary for configurational realizability and admissible transformation in one deductively connected structure (Research Section 05). Domain mapping then supplies measured entities, constraints, and evidence; runtime qualification separates a proposed candidate from permission to execute and from a subsequently observed outcome (Research Sections 07–11).

This reconstruction preserves a charitable distinction in Strüver's position: his published claim primarily concerns the **conditions permitting** transformations. An argument that it fails to prove **every possible transformation occurs** would attack a proposition he need not affirm. The stronger objection concerns whether his proposed foundation can account for **any occurring transition** without an additional dynamical commitment.

## 4. Does connected reality require exactly one primitive?

### 4.1 The conditional argument

Let $P$ and $Q$ denote candidate primitives and $R(P,Q)$ a necessary relation. Strüver's reasoning may be schematized as:

1. A complete foundation must explain every necessary relation without an independent unexplained premise.
2. Connected $P$ and $Q$ require $R(P,Q)$.
3. If $R$ is an independent premise, the pair is incomplete.
4. If $R$ is derived, the independence of $P$ and $Q$ requires reassessment.
5. Therefore a complete foundation must have exactly one primitive.

The conclusion is stronger than the premises. Premise 4 requires *reassessment*, not elimination, of the primitive status of $P$ and $Q$. Moreover, premise 1 adopts a particular account of explanatory completeness. It does not demonstrate that a primitive relation is impossible or that ontological primitiveness is measured solely by the number of starting terms.

### 4.2 Relational counterexample

Consider a theory with primitive kinds $P$ and $Q$ and a necessary structural law $R(P,Q)$. This theory can be internally coherent, connected, and explanatorily finite. Whether $R$ counts as an additional primitive is a substantive ontological question; it does not follow from the mere existence of the relation that the system is contradictory. If Strüver rejects such a model because $R$ is irreducible, he has imposed a stricter *definition of closure*. He must then justify why that definition captures the necessities of reality rather than a preferred research economy.

The objection does not establish that two primitives are preferable, nor does it establish that the relational model is sufficient for actuality. It establishes that the published conditional argument does not **uniquely force** one primitive.

**Finding 1 (HIGH for public-text limitation):** Monoprimitivism is a methodological selection criterion in the disclosed argument. Its status as a necessary ontological conclusion remains unproved.

## 5. Capacity, differentiation, and the modal gap

### 5.1 What Capacity is said to do

Strüver's definition of Capacity concerns the *possibility* and *limitation* of configurations (2026, Research Section 04). His descriptions of distinction, binding, and propagation specify roles these notions would play in an ontology of structured change. Describing a role, however, differs from deducing that its occupant exists or acts.

Let $C$ be the stipulated capacity condition and $T$ the proposition that an actual transition occurs. The weak modal claim is:

**Equation (2)**

$$
C\Rightarrow \Diamond T.
$$

The stronger conclusion required to establish an actual occurrence is:

**Equation (3)**

$$
C\Rightarrow T.
$$

Formula (2) does not entail formula (3). In ordinary modal semantics there can be a world $w_0$ in which $C$ holds, $T$ is false, and $T$ holds in an accessible world $w_1$. This model satisfies the availability of a transition while withholding its occurrence at $w_0$. The same distinction holds between *permission* and *execution* in the author's engineering architecture.

This is an **illustrative logical countermodel**, not a proof that Strüver's full unpublished axioms permit such a world. The decisive request is to identify the additional published axiom or theorem that rules it out.

### 5.2 The transition-entailment test

The countermodel raises a precise question about the explanatory reach of Capacity: if Capacity specifies only the conditions under which transitions are possible, what accounts for a transition actually occurring? A purely dispositional premise is compatible with the absence of any realized change. The issue is whether the proposed foundational derivation establishes dynamical occurrence, and if so, by what additional inferential step. This objection does not presuppose that every physical capacity is causally inert or that Action must ultimately be an independently named primitive.

Three responses remain open to Strüver:

**First**, Capacity might include actual dynamical efficacy in its definition. This may make transition a consequence of the primitive, but the work of explaining dynamism has been relocated into its initial characterization. Whether that is legitimate depends on a noncircular argument for the necessity of the enriched primitive.

**Second**, an additional principle might connect capacity to occurrence. The principle would need to be stated and justified as derivable rather than silently imported.

**Third**, Strüver might restrict the closure claim to the necessary conditions of *possible* transformation. This would be a coherent narrower ambition, but it would leave the obtaining of actual transformations outside the claimed deductive closure unless an independent explanation were supplied.

The paper's statement that propagation *permits* an effect to extend (Research Section 04) supports the weaker modal reading. The text does not supply a public derivation of actual propagation.

**Finding 2 (HIGH for logical distinction; UNCERTAIN for withheld kernel):** The Capacity-to-Action entailment is unestablished in the published exposition. Circularity is a hypothesis for examination, not a demonstrated defect.

### 5.3 Distinction is a separate obligation

The same caution applies one step earlier. The possibility of distinguishable configurations does not alone entail that an actual differentiated configuration obtains. A model with representable alternatives and no actual differentiation is a prima facie countermodel to a stronger entailment. To exclude it, Strüver must show why the starting Capacity necessitates instantiated distinction. A successful derivation of possible distinction would not automatically settle the question of actual occurrence.

## 6. The closure claim and the inaccessible mathematical kernel

Strüver defines closure by the absence of further independent ontological conditions required for realizability (2026, Research Section 05). He reports that the foundational derivation was completed approximately two years before publication, while disclosing that neither the mathematical closure expression nor the complete proof appears in the article (Research Sections 05 and 13).

A claim of *closure* is stronger than an argument that several necessary categories have been identified. It requires an account of the domain over which completeness is asserted, the admissible inference rules, the dependencies among the derived categories, and the proof that no necessary category has been omitted.

The public presentation does not enable the following checks:

1. **Soundness:** Does each derivation follow from explicitly stated premises?
2. **Independence:** Are the supposed derived operations introduced under alternative terminology?
3. **Completeness:** What is the quantified class of possible ontological requirements, and how is exhaustiveness established?
4. **Noncircularity:** Does a dependency graph show that no later derived operation is used to define or justify Capacity?
5. **Modal strength:** Do the theorems concern possible configurations, admissible configurations, or obtaining transitions?

A protected derivation may exist and may resolve some or all of these questions. The correct evidential status is **not independently assessable from the publication**. The author could address this without unrestricted disclosure by supplying a redacted dependency graph, machine-checkable proof certificate, or independent third-party audit of the kernel's stated assumptions and conclusions.

**Finding 3 (HIGH):** The article states a closure criterion and reports its satisfaction. It does not provide public evidence sufficient to verify the satisfaction claim.

## 7. The engineering programme deserves independent evaluation

The applied portions of Strüver's paper are stronger as **engineering prescriptions** than as demonstrations of a unique ontology.

### 7.1 Evidence and identity

His observability principle requires that measured values be bound to the correct entity, meaning, source, and applicable conditions (2026, Research Section 08). This directly addresses a real class of state-estimation and data-assurance failures: an internally well-formed representation can be false because evidence has been associated with the wrong physical object. The principle is sound as a design constraint, though its generality does not establish its derivation from Capacity.

### 7.2 Admissible paths and execution boundaries

The claim that a desirable terminal state does not guarantee feasible intermediate transitions (Research Section 09) is an important constraint on planning and autonomous execution. Strüver's explicit separation of candidate generation, qualification, permission, execution, acknowledgement, and remeasurement (Research Section 10) also supplies a practical audit architecture. It makes failure states inspectable and prevents model-generated proposals from automatically becoming authorized physical actions.

The paper describes closed-loop and hardware-in-the-loop work but does not supply raw traces, comparative baselines, or complete test protocols in the filed article. The proper judgment is therefore that the design is **technically promising** while its reported empirical performance remains **unverified here**.

### 7.3 Conditional disturbance analysis

Strüver correctly distinguishes evaluating the consequences of a specified disturbance from forecasting when that disturbance will occur (Research Section 11). Conditional scenario analysis does not require an occurrence-time probability, although the validity of its consequences depends on model fidelity and the stipulated scenario. This is a valuable methodological distinction.

**Finding 4 (HIGH for textual analysis):** The engineering contributions can be assessed, adopted, or rejected on their own technical merits. Their utility does not logically establish the claimed uniqueness or completeness of the ontology.

## 8. The challenge to predictive physics applies symmetrically

Strüver argues that empirical predictive success does not by itself prove that a theory's primitives are the unique ontological basis of reality (2026, Research Section 12). This is a legitimate underdetermination concern. Several ontological interpretations may agree with the same observations.

Yet the argument is symmetric. A foundational alternative cannot claim empirical superiority merely by offering a more ambitious account of what is necessary. It must identify discriminating consequences, reproducible derivations, or independent necessity arguments that competing frameworks cannot match. Reinterpreting known phenomena through a new vocabulary is not yet a derivation of their physical laws.

Strüver's four verification layers (Research Section 13) are useful for separating errors in deduction, domain mapping, measurement, and execution. To make the foundational claim independently vulnerable to criticism, the programme should publish explicit failure conditions for its reduction and closure propositions, including what would count as a necessary ontological structure that cannot be derived from Capacity.

## 9. Comparison with Triadic Reality Theory

TRT's canonical hard core identifies three irreducible co-primitives (Longmire, 2026a):

**Equation (4)**

$$
\mathrm{TRT}=\langle L_3,I_{\infty},A\rangle,
$$

where $L_3$ denotes ontological logical constraint, $I_{\infty}$ denotes unbounded informational representability, and $A$ denotes primitive state transition or change, defined without presupposing physical time, force, energy, work, or actualization.

The compact identity is:

**Equation (5)**

$$
\chi\equiv\mathsf{A}(I_{\infty}\mid L_3).
$$

It must be read through the typed architecture:

**Equation (6)**

$$
R \longrightarrow D \longrightarrow X(\chi)
$$

In Equation (6), the first arrow denotes application of $L_3$ as a **necessary filter**, and the second denotes the role of $A$ in transition. Here $R$ is representable content, $D$ the actualizable domain, and $X$ what obtains. TRT asserts $D\subseteq R$ and $x\in D\Rightarrow\mathrm{Adm}_{L_3}(x)$. It does **not** assert that logical coherence alone is sufficient for actualizability.

The two programmes converge in their concern for distinction, admissibility, actuality, and the inadequacy of treating mathematical representation as physical existence. They diverge at the point of foundational reduction. Strüver seeks a single sufficient Capacity; TRT argues that constraints, informational differentiation, and transition are mutually necessary and irreducible.

### 9.1 What TRT gains

TRT explicitly refuses the inference from representable possibility to occurring transition. Action is a primitive constituent whose role must be specified in any account of obtaining change. It also distinguishes representability from logical coherence and metaphysical actualizability, preventing a single admissibility predicate from silently carrying all three functions.

### 9.2 What TRT still owes

Naming Action as primitive does not by itself demonstrate that Action is ontologically irreducible. TRT must defend its co-requirement argument against Strüver's demand for a connected foundation, explain the status of relations among the three primitives, and identify whether additional constraints on actualizability exist. Its formal and physics-facing derivations remain open research tasks (Longmire, 2026a; 2026b).

There is also a subtle limit to the slogan *capacity without action is inert*: an ontology of a changeless but determinate actual state would challenge any formulation claiming that **all** determinate actuality requires a nontrivial transition. TRT must explain whether its necessity argument is about the grounding of obtaining actuality, the existence of change, or both. Its canonical definition of $A$ as transition cannot be replaced by “actuality” merely to evade this objection. This is a genuine test of TRT's own precision.

**Finding 5 (MEDIUM comparative judgment):** TRT makes the modal-to-dynamical distinction explicit, but neither programme's full foundational sufficiency has been established by the materials reviewed.

## 10. An adversarial test programme

A constructive next step is to place both programmes under a shared set of tests.

| Test | Question | Evidence that would advance the debate |
|---|---|---|
| Primitive inventory | What exactly is assumed at the start? | Definitions, axioms, type signatures |
| Static-capacity model | Can Capacity hold while no transition occurs? | Model exclusion theorem or acknowledged countermodel |
| Relational co-primitives | Why can jointly necessary primitives not remain irreducible? | Argument against a specific coherent countermodel |
| Modal strength | Does a result establish possibility, admissibility, or occurrence? | Typed theorem statements |
| Closure completeness | What proves all necessary conditions are included? | Scope specification and independent proof audit |
| Physics exposure | What result differs from established alternatives? | Risky prediction and reproducible protocol |
| Engineering exposure | Does qualification improve safe execution? | Raw traces, ablations, independent baselines |

For Strüver, the decisive foundational request is an explicit derivation of *occurring transition* from Capacity, or a principled explanation of why the scope of closure is confined to possible transitions. For TRT, the corresponding task is to show that the triad's co-necessity and irreducibility withstand the best monoprimitive reduction and static-actuality objections.

## 11. Conclusion

Strüver's *Science of Stability and Ontological Closure* deserves serious engagement because it articulates an ambitious ontological reduction, explicitly distinguishes explanatory layers, and connects its methodology to evidence-governed engineering practice. The paper's insistence that physical claims remain answerable to actual configurations is a strength.

Its central foundational claims, however, exceed what the published argument demonstrates. The stated methodological criterion does not independently force a single primitive. Capacity understood as an enabling possibility does not logically entail an occurring distinction or transition. The public dependency sequence is expressly expository, and the complete mathematical closure derivation is withheld. These limitations warrant a request for substantiation, not a verdict that the inaccessible proof has been refuted.

The sharpest open question is therefore whether the modal availability of a transition entails its actual occurrence within Strüver's framework. It does not settle whether Action must be a separate primitive, a constitutive aspect of a richer Capacity, or a consequence of a presently undisclosed theorem. Resolving that issue would materially advance the debate.

The proposed adjudication is therefore **major foundational substantiation required**. Strüver's engineering framework should continue to be evaluated independently, and TRT should remain subject to the same discipline of explicit premises, countermodels, derivational transparency, and severe tests.
