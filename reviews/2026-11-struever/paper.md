## Central Research Thesis

**The ontological foundation must explain the necessary conditions of realizability before a model may claim to explain the observable world.**

For 21 years I have investigated the conditions that make distinction, configuration, interaction, transformation, stability, and structural decay possible. The starting point of this investigation was not a mathematical object, a physical particle, a field, or even my later concept of Capacity. **It was a methodology for determining whether the starting point of reality could be identified without arbitrary independent assumptions.**

The result of that reduction was the monoprimitive I call *Capacity*. From it, my Science of Stability derives distinction, binding, propagation, and relation as conditions of structurally realizable configurations. The next task was to bring these deductions to a mathematically expressible closure. I call the result the **Ontological Closure Condition**.

That condition is intended to make ontology a finite foundational research task. Its completion does not enumerate all entities or future events. It specifies, within my framework, the necessary structures by which different real-world domains can be mapped and their transformations evaluated. This article explains *why* I adopted this method, *what* it claims to establish, *how* the research proceeds after closure, and *where* empirical validation enters.

## Research Section 01: The actual object of foundational inquiry

A theory that reproduces an observed phenomenon and a theory that demonstrates the necessary conditions of that phenomenon answer different questions. The first asks which mathematical description agrees with the measurement. The second asks why a distinguishable physical configuration can exist, what conditions allow it to persist, and what makes a transformation from one configuration to another realizable in the first place.

My research begins with the second question. An observation is evidence of an effect. It is not automatically a demonstration of the unique generative structure of that effect. Different initial configurations may produce observationally similar results. A phenomenological description can therefore be useful while leaving the deeper question of originating conditions unresolved.

For this reason I distinguish the **observable result**, the **physical configuration** that produces it, and the **ontological conditions** required for such configurations and transitions to be possible. These are connected layers of one inquiry, but they cannot be collapsed into one another without losing explanatory precision.

- **Research Object A: Ontology:** The necessary, irreducible conditions for distinction, binding, propagation, relation, and realizability.
- **Research Object B: Configuration:** A domain-specific, distinguishable physical arrangement with capacities, limitations, bindings, and relations.
- **Research Object C: Transition:** A change from an identified configuration to a successor configuration under determinate conditions.
- **Research Object D: Evidence:** Observations and measurements that justify a domain representation and enable a proposed transition to be tested.

The task is to explain how these layers relate without treating the representation itself as reality. The mathematics must remain answerable to the physical configurations that the mathematics is supposed to describe.

## Research Section 02: Methodological reduction precedes every primitive

My first research decision was methodological. I would not select a preferred primitive and then declare everything else derived from it. Instead, I would ask what constitutes an admissible starting point. A starting point is admissible only if the required explanatory operations can be derived without importing additional independent foundational conditions, and only if the resulting system retains explanatory contact with realizable reality.

The reduction continues until another reduction would remove something essential for explaining the existence of distinctions, finite limits, bindings, propagation, relations, or transformations. The objective is not the smallest vocabulary. It is the smallest coherent foundation that retains the power to derive everything the physical world requires.

**Methodological Completeness Criterion:** Irreducibility + Deductive Sufficiency + Realizability.

This line names three requirements of my reduction method. The plus signs indicate joint methodological demands; this is not a numerical physical equation or a replacement for the Ontological Closure Condition.

Irreducibility means that the starting condition cannot be further decomposed into independently assumed foundations while preserving the required explanation. Deductive sufficiency means that the structural operations needed later are not arbitrarily reintroduced. Realizability means that mathematical permission alone does not qualify a configuration as physical reality. Derived claims must ultimately survive domain mapping and empirical confrontation.

These criteria prevent a convenient mathematical object from becoming a primitive merely because it improves a calculation. A proposed primitive must earn its foundational status by what it makes derivable without further assumptions.

## Research Section 03: Why the reduction requires one primitive

Consider two supposedly independent primitives, denoted here as A and B. If they are to contribute to one connected reality, their mutual relation must be explained. If the relation is not derivable from either primitive, it introduces a further independent foundational requirement. If they cannot enter any relation, they do not jointly explain the connected reality under investigation. Under the strict requirement that the foundation itself explain the possibility of relation, the proposed pair is therefore incomplete.

**Methodological Proposition 01: Independent primitives cannot constitute a complete connected foundation when their relation requires an additional independent premise.**

**Premise.** A complete foundation must derive every necessary relation within one connected reality without a new primitive.

**Supposition.** A and B are independent primitives and a relation R(A, B) is necessary for interaction.

**Consequence.** If R is independent of both, the proposed foundation was not complete. If R is derivable, the independence or primitive status of the starting pair requires renewed examination. If no R exists, the pair does not describe a connected interacting domain.

**Conclusion within my method.** The admissible foundation must reduce to one sufficient starting condition from which distinction and relation themselves can be derived.

This is a conditional proof argument within the completeness criterion I adopt. It is not a claim that ordinary formal logic universally forbids languages or theories containing two primitive symbols. The relevant issue is not the number of symbols in a theory; it is whether the theory introduces an unexplained relation at the point it claims to be ontologically fundamental.

For my research the decisive requirement follows: a starting point must not depend on another independent explanatory entity in order to support limitation, distinction, binding, and interaction. It must make those operations derivable from itself.

## Research Section 04: Capacity as the result rather than the premise

The result of my reductive investigation was **Capacity**. I do not mean the everyday quantity of energy stored in a device or a synonym for electrical capacitance. I use Capacity to denote the irreducible condition from which the possibility and limitation of configuration can be derived.

The order of reasoning matters. I did not begin by stating, "Reality is Capacity," and then interpret every observation to fit that assertion. I first imposed the reduction and closure criteria. Only then did I identify Capacity as the monoprimitive that, in my proposed derivation, can support the required structural formation without another independent foundation.

**Capacity**: Starting condition. **Distinction**: Identifiable difference. **Binding**: Coherent constraint. **Propagation**: Transmission of effect. **Relation**: Determinate connection.

The sequence represents the order of derivation claimed by my ontology. It is not a claim that these are five unrelated primitives.

**Distinction** provides identifiable difference and therefore a basis for separating one condition from another. **Binding** concerns the structural conditions by which distinctions can form a coherent configuration. **Propagation** permits an effect to extend through admissible conditions. **Relation** becomes determinable where configurations are connected under their actual structural properties.

The existence of limitation does not prevent unbounded configurational diversity. A limit makes a specific configuration determinate. It creates differences, admissible connections, and boundaries. Across many configurations, those finite conditions permit an open-ended diversity of realizable structures. The distinction between *limits within a configuration* and *unbounded diversity across configurations* is fundamental to my account.

My purpose is to derive the emergence of constrained systems without needing to place a separate, unexplained concept of "system," "interaction," or "possibility" into the foundation. Each of those concepts must be accountable to the prior derivation.

## Research Section 05: The meaning of the Ontological Closure Condition

The most frequently misunderstood part of my work is the word *closure*. I use it to mean completion of the necessary foundational structural conditions that permit physical realizability. I have sought to express this completed structure through a formal mathematical closure condition.

Ontology itself is a comparatively small research domain in this approach. It concerns the irreducible enabling conditions of configurations and their possible interactions. Once those conditions have been derived completely, the ontological task is closed. This does not mean the universe has stopped changing, that all phenomena have been enumerated, or that every physical system has already been measured. It means that no new independent ontological condition should be required merely because a new physical domain is being examined.

**Definition 05: Ontological Closure:** Closure is reached when all independent conditions necessary for configurational realizability and admissible transformation are accounted for in one deductively connected structure.

In my framework, the closed structure must support distinction, limitation, binding, propagation, relation, the formation of configurations, persistence, admissible change, destabilization, and loss of prior structural viability.

The specific mathematical closure expression and its full derivation are not reproduced in this public explanatory article. The criteria here describe its claimed function; they do not constitute an invented substitute kernel.

Completion is therefore a statement about **foundational sufficiency**. The world can contain an immense number of real configurations, each requiring domain-specific measurements. Yet their realizability is analyzed against the same foundational conditions rather than explained by introducing a fresh ontology every time an unfamiliar phenomenon appears.

Stability is not an isolated adjective in this framework. A configuration remains stable while its necessary structural conditions remain satisfied within the relevant regime. A disturbance can change capacities, constraints, or bindings and move the configuration toward a limit. A limit can mark a regime transition; a sufficiently severe loss of necessary conditions can produce destabilization or breakdown. These possibilities belong within the closure analysis because any foundational account of reality must permit both persistence and change.

**The Scope of Closure:** Ontological closure does not calculate every event in advance. It establishes the necessary conditions by which events and transitions can be evaluated when a physical configuration is specified.

For my research, the foundational closure was completed approximately two years ago, following a total research period of about 21 years. Since then the emphasis has shifted toward mapping concrete domains, implementing the analysis computationally, and confronting the resulting deductions with measured reality.

## Research Section 06: Formal structure and the distinction between syntax and reality

A mathematically closed statement can be internally consistent and still require empirical support when applied to the physical world. My method therefore distinguishes three questions: whether an implication follows from the formal starting conditions; whether the mapped domain premises are correct; and whether the resulting transition agrees with actual measurements. None of these questions can be silently replaced by the others.

At the ontological level, the aim is to show that the structural categories follow from Capacity without adding independent primitives. At the domain level, a researcher must specify which observable features instantiate the categories. At the operational level, an action is admissible only when its required conditions are evidenced and the transition qualifies within the mapped domain.

**Expository Inference Form: Not the Kernel Equation:**

Capacity ⊢ Distinction ⊢ Binding ⊢ Propagation ⊢ Relation

Mapped configuration + required evidence + closure qualification ⇒ admissible candidate

The turnstile and implication signs illustrate the logical obligations described in this article. They do not assert that the full formal derivation has been reproduced here, nor do they authorize execution by themselves.

The first line is a claim about deductive dependency. The second names a domain qualification requirement. In both cases the explanatory burden is explicit. One must identify the premises, the rule that permits each step, and the conditions whose absence invalidates the conclusion.

The empirical obligation cannot be eliminated by a proof of internal consistency. A domain mapping may be incomplete, a sensor can be unavailable, and a physical object may have been incorrectly identified. A sound method must expose these failures as missing or contradictory premises. To treat a mathematically successful output as a physical fact without the necessary evidence would violate the purpose of reality-bound analysis.

## Research Section 07: Domain mapping after the completion of ontology

After ontological closure the research procedure changes. A new domain does not require a new starting point of reality. It requires a specification of the entities, conditions, measurements, identity relations, capacities, dependencies, and constraints that are actually present. I call this work **Domain Mapping**.

A physical robot arm and an enterprise supply chain have radically different domain descriptions. The robot has geometry, joints, velocities, physical obstacles, sensors, and actuator limits. The enterprise has resources, contractual bindings, operational dependencies, financial limits, and successive feasible configurations. Both, within my framework, can be investigated by asking what can persist, what can interact, which conditions must remain satisfied, and what occurs when a necessary condition is removed.

The ontological categories do not change merely because the application changes. The physical representation must change, because it must correspond to the actual domain. This is what I mean by one ontological structure with different domain mappings. It is not a claim that the same sensor, measurement unit, or engineering interface can be used everywhere.

### A formal presentation of the mapping obligation

For clarity, let *D* denote an examined domain, *X* its represented configuration, and *E* the evidence available for that representation. Let the notation *Map(D, X, E)* stand for the task of assigning the domain's measured properties to the relevant closure requirements. These symbols are only expository labels for the responsibilities of a domain mapping, not the notation of my protected mathematical kernel.

**Expository Domain Qualification Schema:** Domain D → configuration X → evidence E → closure qualification. Unresolved required condition ⇒ qualification withheld.

The arrows describe an order of analysis, not a probabilistic score, a replacement stability equation, or an unqualified guarantee about a physical device.

The requirement is concrete. A mapper must distinguish what is known because it is measured, what is declared because it is specified, what is derived from the mapped conditions, and what remains unavailable. A missing measurement cannot be converted into a valid premise by confidence alone. If the mapping does not provide a required property, the corresponding qualification is incomplete.

This also makes the framework useful for engineering assessments. In a domain where a closure requirement cannot be evaluated, the diagnosis is not simply that the model is "uncertain." The analysis can identify which kind of information is missing, which dependency is unsupported, or which physical constraint prevents the proposed transition. The resulting engineering task is to repair that deficiency in observation, integration, representation, or design.

**Methodological Proposition 02: A domain mapping cannot establish admissibility if one of its necessary premises remains unevidenced.**

**Reasoning.** A proposed transition relies on specified structural conditions. If a necessary condition lacks evidence, the qualification does not establish that the real configuration satisfies it.

**Engineering consequence.** The action must not be represented as independently justified merely because a candidate successor can be generated. The missing evidence or violated condition must be reported and dealt with explicitly.

## Research Section 08: Identity preservation and the observability of reality

For a physical system, evidence is useful only if the information has been bound to the correct entity and configuration. Suppose ten objects occupy one room. The perception system may observe geometry, heat signatures, velocity, position, and other sensor properties. The engineering task is not exhausted by assigning plausible category labels to ten detections. It must preserve the identity relation that connects the observed properties to their originating entities under the domain's specified measurement conditions.

If the thermal signature of one object is incorrectly bound to the geometry or motion of another, the resulting representation may remain syntactically well formed while describing a configuration that is not present. A later control computation cannot restore the missing physical identity merely by operating consistently on that representation. The error lies upstream, in the evidential premises from which authorization would be calculated.

In my Science of Stability this is a configurational problem. The relation between an entity and its measured signatures must be maintained or explicitly marked unresolved. A state estimate must carry a defined meaning, a declared source, and conditions under which the binding remains valid. If a sensor fails or contradictory information appears, the analysis must not quietly preserve the illusion of a complete current state.

**Observability Principle:** A measured value is not yet a qualified physical premise until its entity, meaning, origin, and applicable conditions are correctly bound.

The observational layer is therefore neither detached from ontology nor a substitute for it. Ontology establishes the need for distinction and binding; the domain mapping tells us which actual measurements can establish a particular identity; runtime analysis determines whether the evidence currently available supports the proposed transition.

This distinction matters in robotics, autonomous machines, medical devices, and enterprises. A corrupted record and a misbound sensor signature are different domain failures, yet both can destroy the validity of a subsequent transition if the system treats them as authoritative premises. The solution cannot be a mere statement that the previous computational operation succeeded. The informational relation to reality has to be reconstructed or the affected transaction withheld.

## Research Section 09: Stability regimes and the limits of realizable transformation

My use of the word *stability* concerns the capacity of a particular configuration to satisfy the conditions required for its continued existence and admissible operation. A system is not globally "stable" because a single performance indicator remains favorable. Stability must be understood relative to its actual configuration, dependencies, limits, and required transformations.

A configuration can remain within one regime while its conditions are satisfied. A disturbance may remove available capacity, break a binding, change a relation, or push the system toward a limit. When a necessary condition is lost, the previous transition path can become inadmissible. The system may then require a hold, a different successor, restoration of a dependency, or a controlled change of regime.

These relationships must be included in the ontological closure because a reality capable of forming and maintaining structures must also permit their alteration and breakdown. Stability, destabilization, entropy-related change, and decay cannot be left as unexplained special cases that require new primitive assumptions whenever conditions deteriorate.

The practical distinction is between an endpoint preference and a structurally admissible path. A planned end state may appear attractive, but its desirability does not guarantee that each necessary intermediate transition can be realized. Conversely, a blocked transition does not imply that every alternative path is blocked. The relevant question becomes which successor configurations remain admissible under the conditions now present.

**Methodological Proposition 03: A desired terminal configuration does not establish the admissibility of its intermediate transitions.**

For a sequence of transitions to be justified, each step must satisfy the conditions applicable at that step. An inadmissible predecessor-to-successor transition is not repaired by the desirability of the eventual endpoint.

The same argument applies to long-horizon plans. An early qualification does not automatically authorize every later transaction when the physical configuration changes.

## Research Section 10: From candidate generation to authorized execution

These principles led me to develop **Reality-Bound Computing** as a technical application of the Science of Stability. Its governing distinction is between producing a candidate and authorizing the corresponding real-world transaction. A candidate may be generated, compared, or investigated without thereby acquiring permission to change the world.

Within my architecture, the chain is explicit: Observation → Representation → Candidate → Transaction → Qualification → Verdict → Permission → Execution → Acknowledgement. After execution, the observed successor state is remeasured and compared with what the qualified transaction required. A discrepancy becomes a falsification or mismatch event to be handled, not a detail to be discarded.

**Execution Boundary:** Candidate ≠ Qualification ≠ Permission. Execution → Remeasurement → Comparison.

These are distinct functional responsibilities. Successful data transmission, a valid message format, or a plausible model output does not by itself satisfy them.

This structure is deliberately non-bypass in the authorization stage. The purpose of that restriction is to prevent a generated successor or a convenient external command from being confused with an admissible and qualified transaction. If a necessary premise is absent, the authorization layer must expose the reason for refusal rather than silently proceeding.

The hardware and simulation work has included digital closed-loop evaluations and hardware-in-the-loop configurations with evidence traces for objectives, sensor frames, authorizations, holds, and remeasurement. These tests are meaningful because they make particular transactions and discrepancies inspectable within the tested setup. They are not a claim that every future robot, plant, or industrial domain has already been validated. Each new device requires its own mapping and qualification of the actual physical interfaces.

Reality-Bound Computing is therefore not another label for statistical artificial intelligence. It is an architecture intended to calculate successor admissibility and execution permission from domain-bound conditions. A separate inference engine may produce a hypothesis or candidate, but that candidate does not become a physical authorization merely because it was produced confidently.

## Research Section 11: Disturbance analysis without pretending to know the future

One frequent objection to nonprobabilistic configurational analysis is that nobody knows whether a particular war, earthquake, political decision, supply interruption, or sensor failure will occur. I do not need to know the future event's occurrence time in order to analyze the consequences of a stated disturbance. Forecasting the time of an event and examining the admissible response to an event are different research problems.

Starting from a measured current configuration, I can introduce a hypothetical disturbance as a specific change of domain conditions. I can then examine which successor paths preserve the necessary conditions of stability, which paths become blocked, and which transformations are required to restore an admissible operating configuration. The scenario can be freely selected as a stress case. Its consequences are analyzed conditionally against the specified structure.

Consider a company dependent on several suppliers and transport routes. A strategist can hypothesize the loss of a supply route due to geopolitical conflict. The significant analysis is not an invented confidence score for the date of conflict. It is the resulting configuration: which resources become unavailable, which obligations remain binding, which operations can continue, and which alternate path preserves the enterprise's viability under those conditions.

When a disturbance actually occurs, runtime monitoring provides a fresh measured configuration. The earlier prepared path must be evaluated again. Conditions may have changed in ways not covered by the original scenario. What was admissible at planning time may no longer be admissible now, and new alternatives may have appeared. The system therefore requires present-tense evaluation rather than blind loyalty to a historical plan.

**Methodological Proposition 04: Conditional disturbance analysis does not require a probability for the time at which the disturbance occurs.**

**Given.** A current mapped configuration, a specified disturbance, and the domain's closure criteria.

**Question.** Which transformations and successor configurations remain admissible if the disturbance changes the premises as specified?

**Limit.** The result is conditional on the modeled disturbance and the validity of the mapping. It does not establish when the disturbance will occur or that every conceivable future disturbance has been covered.

The ability to imagine unusual disturbances can enlarge the stress-test set. The rigor of the subsequent analysis depends on the correct mapping of the resulting configuration, not on pretending that imagination itself constitutes physical evidence.

## Research Section 12: Why predictive success is insufficient as a foundational explanation

My strongest disagreement with conventional theoretical physics concerns the point at which mathematical representation is promoted into ontological explanation. Quantum mechanics and relativity theory have substantial empirical successes. I do not deny the measured phenomena or the usefulness of the relevant calculations. String theory has a different empirical status. My methodological criticism is about the necessity of the structures introduced as foundational, not a claim that all of these theories have identical evidence.

If a model accurately describes a scattering pattern, a time-dilation measurement, or another observed effect, it has established predictive correspondence under specified conditions. It has not thereby uniquely demonstrated why its chosen primitives, mathematical objects, or independent assumptions are the necessary origin of reality. That further conclusion requires its own derivation.

For example, a conditional scattering analysis begins with a specified interaction potential and calculates consequences under an approximation or another formal solution. I can accept the utility of such a calculation while asking a different question: which concrete configurations establish the interaction, how are the participating entities distinguished, and under which necessary conditions is the observed transition realizable? A statistical distribution of outcomes and an ontological explanation of their originating conditions are not interchangeable answers.

That is the basis on which I describe certain fundamental interpretations as **ontological fiction**. The phrase expresses my judgment that a mathematical abstraction has been treated as a description of what fundamentally exists without demonstrating the derivation demanded by my methodology. It does not mean that a successful laboratory result was imaginary, nor that a useful mathematical technique must be abandoned.

Likewise, the mere existence of a mathematical object named "quantum" does not settle whether an independent quantum entity should count as an ontological primitive. If the physical issue is a distinction, an interaction, a constrained transition, or a measurable change of configuration, my approach requires an account in terms of the originating entities, capacities, bindings, and realizable relations. The word chosen for a mathematical representation does not supply that account by itself.

**Methodological Boundary:** Successful mathematical description is evidence of predictive usefulness. It is not, without further derivation, proof of a uniquely necessary ontology.

I therefore reject the practice of treating unexplained foundational assumptions as resolved simply because the resulting predictions work. My alternative is to derive the necessary structure first, map the actual configuration afterward, and test whether the predicted realizable transitions correspond to physical reality.

## Research Section 13: How the research can be examined and challenged

A foundational claim must remain open to precise objections. In my framework, different criticisms belong to different levels and should not be conflated. One can challenge the reduction criterion itself; show a hidden premise in a purported deduction; demonstrate that the closure structure is insufficient; expose an invalid domain mapping; or supply an empirical counterexample to a qualified transformation. These criticisms have different meanings and require different responses.

The first level concerns formal consistency. Does each derivation follow from the declared starting condition, or does it import another independent primitive under a new name? The second concerns closure sufficiency. Does a domain require a foundational structural condition not derivable from the closed system? The third concerns the domain mapping. Are all necessary entities, constraints, and identities represented correctly? The fourth concerns observation and execution. Do the actual successor measurements correspond to the qualified transition?

- **Verification Layer 01: Reduction audit:** Identify every foundational assumption and test whether the derivation covertly depends on more than one independent primitive.
- **Verification Layer 02: Closure audit:** Test whether the claimed structural conditions derive the categories required for realizability without an unexplained addition.
- **Verification Layer 03: Domain audit:** Check whether actual capacities, constraints, signatures, and evidence are correctly represented and bound.
- **Verification Layer 04: Reality comparison:** Compare the qualified successor with the executed and remeasured result, exposing mismatch and incomplete evidence.

These layers explain why an internally consistent formal system and a successful engineering demonstration are not the same type of evidence. A hardware-in-the-loop result supports the behavior observed in the tested implementation. A cross-domain completeness claim requires additional mappings and observations. Conversely, a failed individual sensor reading does not automatically disprove an ontological proposition; the relevant question is whether the failure lies in the mapping, measurement, implementation, or foundational derivation.

This separation is not an excuse to protect the theory from falsification. It makes the object of possible falsification explicit. If the ontology cannot derive a necessary structural condition without inserting an independent premise, its closure claim is challenged. If a domain mapping makes a definite and testable transition claim that repeatedly contradicts the real system under correctly verified premises, that claim must be revised or rejected.

### Scientific Scope of This Publication

This article states my research methodology and the claimed structure of the Science of Stability. It is a formal explanatory account, not a publication of the complete protected closure derivation or a substitute for domain-specific experimental reports.

The assertion that the foundational derivation was completed approximately two years ago describes my research status. General physical validity requires that each claimed application remain answerable to explicit domain conditions, reproducible tests, and observations.

## Research Section 14: Why the ontological foundation is complete and domain research continues

When I say that my ontological research was completed, I mean that the foundational conditions required by my framework were brought to closure. I do not mean that the entire physical world has been catalogued, that every interaction has already been measured, or that all possible engineering implementations have been built.

Before completion, the central question was whether the required structural operations could be derived from one irreducible starting condition. After completion, the central question becomes how a particular real system instantiates those conditions. That second task is open-ended because the physical world contains an enormous diversity of configurations and interactions, even though the foundational conditions are intended to remain invariant.

My work since the foundational completion has therefore emphasized domain mappings, controlled simulations, hardware-in-the-loop evaluations, repeated closed-loop analysis, and the design of execution mechanisms that preserve the distinction between a proposed candidate and an authorized transaction.

The same methodological orientation informs other applications: physical robotics, prosthetic devices, enterprise stability, resource constraints, and the causal evaluation of possible response paths under disturbance. Each domain has its own evidence requirements. None acquires empirical validity merely by borrowing a philosophical description. The ontology supplies the claimed common foundation; the domain supplies the facts that must make the application real.

## Research Section 15: What the Science of Stability represents

My 21-year research program began with a simple but demanding decision: I would not accept a convenient mathematical primitive as the foundation of reality without first establishing a method for discovering what the foundation must be. That method imposed irreducibility, deductive sufficiency, and correspondence with realizable configurations. Under its monoprimitive requirement, the reduction led me to Capacity.

From Capacity I developed the ontological derivation of distinction, binding, propagation, and relation. I worked toward a complete mathematical condition encompassing the necessary structural requirements for configurations to emerge, persist, interact, transform, destabilize, and cease to remain viable. That condition is what I call **Ontological Closure**.

Its completion changes the task of science as I practice it. Instead of adding new fundamental assumptions to accommodate each newly observed phenomenon, I map the actual domain into the closed structural foundation and evaluate the realizability of proposed transformations. Where information is missing, I identify the missing condition. Where a limit is violated, I identify the violated constraint. Where an authorized transition is executed, I compare the expected consequence with remeasured reality.

This is why I distinguish my work from phenomenological modeling, probability-driven forecasting, and statistical inference. They can be practically useful, but their usefulness alone does not establish the necessary originating conditions of reality. My research asks for that derivation, and its applications demand explicit evidence whenever a mathematical conclusion is to govern a real transaction.

**Foundational Research Position:** I began with a method for discovering the conditions of reality. Capacity was the result of that method. Ontological Closure was the goal of the deduction. Domain Mapping is the work that follows. Reality remains the final criterion against which every physical application must be tested.
